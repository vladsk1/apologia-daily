# Scholar-label ledger — how named people may and may not be described

**`apologia-neutrality` consults this before clearing OR flagging any sentence that labels a named
scholar** (their stance, school, religion, or "view"). The site has a recurring, costly failure:
mislabelling a real person a knowledgeable reader can then screenshot and debunk. This ledger turns
that recurring judgment call into a lookup — and records the *source* that settles each one, so the
label rests on evidence, not memory.

> Rules of use:
> - A label here marked **verified** has a cited source and may be used as written.
> - A label marked **contested** must be used in the *safe* form given, never the unsafe form.
> - If a sentence labels a scholar NOT in this ledger in a way that could be wrong, the reviewer
>   verifies it (web search) and, if it's a recurring name, adds a row here in the same change.
> - "How we use it" labels on `our-sources.html` are gated copy — port from the essays' actual use,
>   per the CLAUDE.md "OUR SOURCES" rule; this ledger is the reference for that too.
> - Mechanical enforcement, where a clean pattern exists, lives in `tools/retired-claims.json`
>   (noted per row). Most rows are reference-only because the safe/unsafe forms are context-dependent.

| Scholar | Safe description | Do NOT say | Why / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Roger Penrose** | "non-believing physicist," "no religious commitment," "no theist" | flatly "atheist Penrose" | Self-description is **mixed**: a first-person "I would say I'm an atheist" (Jewish Virtual Library) vs. Craig reporting him as self-describing agnostic vs. Humanists UK patron "no religious doctrine." "Non-believing" holds under all and keeps the "even a non-theist concedes the data" force. **Mechanically guarded** (`retired-claims.json` → `penrose-atheist-label`). Generic "atheist physicists" as a *class* (not naming Penrose) is fine. |
| **Geraint Lewis** | "the multiverse advocate" (in *A Fortunate Universe*, CUP 2016) | "the sceptic" (unqualified), or imply he's the theist | Lewis argues the **multiverse** side; **Luke Barnes** is the theist-leaning co-author. Getting this backwards inverts the book's probative value. He is a sceptic *of the design inference* who affirms the *data* — say that if "sceptic" is used at all. |
| **Luke Barnes** | "the designer-leaning co-author" of *A Fortunate Universe* | the multiverse advocate | Pairs with the Lewis row — the two co-authors agree on the data and divide on the inference. |
| **Victor Stenger** | "the physicist who disputed how narrow the windows are" | lump him with those conceding the fine-tuning data unqualified | He contested the *degree* of fine-tuning; `finetuning.html` fn 1 (Barnes 2012) names him "the antagonist." Claiming unanimity on window-narrowness erases him. |
| **Sabine Hossenfelder** | "a non-theist critic of multiverse reasoning / of untestable physics" | a witness *for* design | Her untestability critique cuts *both* ways (symmetry disclosure); drop that and a methodological critic is miscast as supporting the design inference. |
| **Bart Ehrman** | "the agnostic NT scholar" who concedes crucifixion + the disciples' sincere belief (and Jesus's existence) | that he concedes the resurrection, an empty-tomb consensus, or "<1% meaningful-and-viable variants" in his own mouth | He concedes the *minimal facts' data*, not the inference. The variant-viability figure is Wallace's, not Ehrman's. Repeatedly mislabelled here. |
| **Dale Allison** | "a cautious, agnostic-leaning historian of the resurrection who takes the evidence seriously" | a defender of the bodily resurrection, or a flat sceptic | He is deliberately *undecided* and holds the hardest middle position — don't collapse him to either pole. |
| **Geza Vermes** | "the Jewish scholar who read 'son of man' as an Aramaic idiom" | a supporter of a Danielic-title claim on Jesus's lips | He argued *against* "Son of Man" being a Danielic title Jesus claimed — the opposite of a supporting witness. |
| **Jason BeDuhn** | "the scholar who backs the NWT's 'a god' reading of John 1:1c" | that he concedes the orthodox reading | He supports the Watchtower rendering; the JW card must **refute** him, not cite him as conceding orthodoxy. |
| **Tryggve Mettinger** | "the scholar — the mythicists' own source — whose study *concludes against* the borrowing thesis, while conceding a minority dying-and-rising category" | a straightforward supporter of pagan-copycat parallels | He grants a limited category but rejects the derivation of the resurrection from it; he concedes his position is a minority one. Cite with that nuance. |
| **Richard Bauckham** | cite as a **historian** on historical questions (eyewitness testimony, onomastics) | cite as a theological authority, or list in "further study" without naming the specific work | Standing **universalism fence** (CLAUDE.md): mine the historiography only; naming the work prevents a reader wandering into his universalist theology. |
