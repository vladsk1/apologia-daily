# Outreach messages — Tier 1 (scholar-reviewers) & Tier 2 (creators)

_Companion to `docs/INFLUENCER_GROWTH_PLAN.md` (2026-09-05). Copy-paste-ready drafts with
`[bracketed]` fill-ins. They encode the plan's rules: the product is **free** right now, so there is
**no rev-share / paid-endorsement pitch** (do not send one until Stripe is live); the give is
**labour + credibility**; denominational neutrality and the 1 Peter 3:15 tone are load-bearing; and
nothing implies an endorsement we don't have. Keep every message short — long cold messages don't
get read. Personalise the first line of each or don't send it._

---

## TIER 1 — Scholar-reviewers (the highest-value relationship)

**Goal:** a named, credentialed reviewer (ideally three, across traditions — a philosophy PhD, an
NT/OT PhD, and a pastor) who reviews essays and is credited on a public review-board page + per-essay
byline. This closes the site's standing pastoral/human sign-off gap and is the biggest single
credibility move.
**Channel:** a real email (university/ministry address), or a warm introduction if you have one.
Never a DM for this tier.
**Rule:** be honest that the review is currently automated and you are seeking genuine human
sign-off. Do not overstate scale or imply they'd be endorsing everything. Compensation is a
conversation, not a line in the cold email.

### T1-A · Cold email — philosophy PhD (natural theology / phil. religion)
> **Subject:** A second, named set of eyes on our apologetics essays
>
> Dear Dr [Name],
>
> I run Apologia Daily, a Christian apologetics library — [92] long-form, fully-cited essays on
> the arguments for God, the resurrection, and the reliability of the Bible, written to hold up
> under a knowledgeable critic and kept denominationally neutral (we defend the faith common to
> Catholics, Orthodox and Protestants and don't adjudicate in-house disputes).
>
> Every essay currently clears a five-stage editorial review — but that review is AI-assisted, and
> we say so publicly. What the project is missing, and what would make it genuinely trustworthy, is
> a **named scholar** who has actually read the work and is willing to be credited for it.
>
> Would you be open to reviewing a small number of essays in your area — say, our Leibnizian
> contingency and fine-tuning pieces — and, if they meet your bar, letting us list you as a reviewer
> with your name and affiliation? I'd start by sending **one essay** and asking only for a candid
> yes/no on whether it's sound; no commitment beyond that until you've seen how we work. We'd of
> course discuss your time properly.
>
> The essays are at apologiadaily.com/library. Grateful for even a brief reply.
>
> With respect,
> [Your name]

### T1-B · Cold email — NT / OT scholar

> [Your name]

### T1-C · Cold email — pastor / elder (the pastoral sign-off)
> **Subject:** A pastor's read on our apologetics content
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> I run Apologia Daily, an apologetics resource built to strengthen believers and reach seekers —
> and to do it gently (1 Peter 3:15), never as a debate to be won. We're careful to stay on the

>
> Our content is reviewed against the historic creeds, but that review is automated, and I don't
> think a ministry resource should ship without a real pastor having read it for tone and care as
> well as truth. Would you be willing to look over a few pieces — starting with how we handle
> suffering and doubt — and, if you're comfortable, let us name you as a pastoral reviewer?
>
> I'd send one short piece first and ask only what you think. Happy to talk on a call if that's
> easier.
>
> Grateful,
> [Your name]

### T1-D · Warm follow-up (only after a positive reply)
> Thank you — that means a lot. I've attached [the essay / a link to just this one page]. There's no
> deadline; whenever you have half an hour, I'd value two things: (1) is anything in here wrong or
> overstated, and (2) would you be comfortable being credited as a reviewer once you've seen enough
> to trust it? If you'd rather stay behind the scenes at first, that's completely fine too.

## TIER 2 — Micro / mid apologetics creators (the amplifiers)

**Goal:** they use the library for prep and cite/link it when it's genuinely useful; the strongest
win is a fact-checked reel we make *for* them. **Channel:** platform DM or their site contact form.
**Sequence (from the plan):** ~2 weeks of genuine engagement (real comments, a share) → the gift
message with **no ask** → the ask **only** if they reply warmly. Never lead with the ask.
**Rule:** no rev-share (nothing to pay on a free product); pitch only denominationally-neutral
content to tradition-specific creators; exclude combative "debate-dunk" accounts and (per the
Nicene guardrail) LDS/JW creators as partners.

### T2-A · The GIFT — script-help version (no ask)
> Hey [name] — your [specific recent video] was one of the clearest treatments of [topic] I've seen
> at any channel size. I build Apologia Daily (apologiadaily.com) — every argument on it is
> fact-checked against primary sources and reviewed before it ships (we've pulled fabricated quotes
> that circulate all over apologetics). If it's ever handy for prepping a script or grabbing a
> sourced answer, it's all free and open — no catch, no sign-up wall. Keep up the good work.

### T2-B · The GIFT — made-for-you reel version (the strongest give)
> Hey [name] — really appreciated your [specific video] on [topic]. Quick, genuine offer with zero
> strings: I run Apologia Daily, and we make short, fully-fact-checked explainer videos on
> apologetics topics. **Tell me a topic you'd cover next and I'll make you a 30-second, sourced reel
> you can use however you like** — yours, no attribution required. Just thought it might save you
> some time. Either way, keep it up.

### T2-C · The GIFT — receipts-link version (for a creator who makes strong claims on camera)
> Hey [name] — loved the [topic] video. One small thing that might help: when you make a claim like
> "[the claim they made]," some folks in the comments want a source. We have a footnoted deep-dive on
> exactly that at [essay link] — feel free to pin it as the receipts, or not. It's free and fully
> cited. No ask; just thought it'd be useful.

### T2-D · The ASK (only after a warm reply)
> Glad it's useful! If it's ever worth a mention to your audience, no pressure at all — a link in a
> description or a pinned comment is more than enough, and I'd happily make you a custom reel for it.
> Whatever's genuinely helpful to you; I'm not looking for anything you wouldn't do gladly.

### T2-E · Campus / Ratio Christi / RF chapter (student-run accounts — warmest yes)
> Hi [chapter name] — I run Apologia Daily (apologiadaily.com), a free, fully-cited apologetics
> library, and I'd love for it to be useful to your chapter. It's denominationally neutral and built
> for exactly the questions students get asked — the resurrection, God's existence, Islam, the
> reliability of the Bible — with the sources laid out. Totally free, no sign-up needed. If it's a
> fit, we'd be glad to be on your resources list, and I'm happy to make your group a custom study or
> short video on whatever you're working through. Either way, thank you for what you're doing.

## Do-not-send checklist (read before every message)
- [ ] No rev-share / commission / "earn money" language (the product is free; nothing to pay).
- [ ] No claim of the author's or reviewer's endorsement we don't have.
- [ ] Tradition-specific creator → only denominationally-neutral content offered.
- [ ] Recipient passes the tone filter (gentle, not a debate-dunk account) and the Nicene filter
      (not LDS/JW as a *partner*).
- [ ] First line is genuinely personalised to their actual work, or don't send.
- [ ] It's a gift first. The ask waits for a warm reply.
