## Argument map (the cumulative case)
Thesis: a Swinburne **C-inductive** case — the salient facts are vastly better explained by R (bodily
resurrection, c. AD 33) than by ~R; structure = explanatory → comparative → probabilistic.
- **Textual assumptions:** Gospels + Acts + undisputed Paulines treated as substantially accurate
  secular-grade sources by eyewitnesses/associates; **no inspiration/inerrancy assumed**; minor
  discrepancies argue *against* collusion (legal-testimony principle); late-dating rebutted by
  archaeology + external patristic evidence.
- **Background facts:** Jesus died (swoon refuted); buried in a tomb (Crossan's no-tomb thesis
  rebutted).
- **The salient independent lines** (their Bayes factors multiply, treated as first-approximation
  independent "channels"): **W** = the women's testimony to the empty tomb + the risen Christ;
  **D** = the ~13 named male witnesses who claimed to see the risen Jesus and showed willingness to
  die, incl. the *physical/"polymodal"* character of the appearances; **P** = Paul's conversion
  (hostile outsider); **James the Just** folded in as the "thirteenth witness" (separate, individual
  conversion of a pre-Easter unbeliever). Rival ~R sub-hypotheses (fraud, group hallucination, wrong
  tomb, body-moved, swoon, legend, conspiracy) each shown causally inadequate. Then: worldview/prior
  worries, Plantinga's "dwindling probabilities," and a closing refutation of Hume.

## Primaries to chase (UNVERIFIED LEADS, as the chapter cites them)
- **Women / embarrassment:** Mark 16:1–8; Luke 24:1–11 (esp. 24:11 "idle tales"); 24:22; John 20:1–18;
  Luke 23:55–56; Josephus, *Antiquities* 4.219 (women's testimony disallowed); **Talmud,
  *Rosh Hashanah* 1.8c** ("any evidence which a woman gives is not valid") **and *b. Sotah* 31b** (the
  nuanced/qualifying view — the chapter cites both, to avoid overstating).
- **Physical resurrection claims:** Luke 24:39–43 ("flesh and bones," eats fish); Acts 2:29–32 (David's
  body decayed, Christ's did not); John 20:27 (Thomas); John 21:9–13.
- **No prior expectation (fear/doubt, not grief-longing):** Mark 14:66–72; John 20:19, 20:25; Matt
  28:17 ("some doubted"); **N. T. Wright, *The Resurrection of the Son of God* (2003), ch. 4,
  pp. 200–06** — first-century Jewish categories did **not** predict an individual's resurrection
  before the general one.
- **Willingness-to-die established EARLY at Jerusalem:** Acts 4:18–20; 5:17–18, 29, 33, 40; Acts 7
  (Stephen, within ~1–3 yrs); Acts 8; James bar Zebedee executed Acts 12:2; extra-biblically
  **1 Clement 5–6** (≤ c. AD 96) and **Pliny, *Ep.* 10.96 to Trajan** (c. 113, the execute-or-recant
  procedure) + Trajan's reply.
- **James the Just:** unbelief Mark 3:21, 31; John 7:5; individual appearance **1 Cor 15:7**; leadership
  Acts 12:17, 15; death Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200; martyrdom narrative **Hegesippus in Eusebius,
  *Hist. eccl.* 2.23** (parapet, recant-demand, thrown down, stoned, clubbed).
- **Paul:** Acts 9; 1 Cor 15:3–8; Gal 1:1, 11–16; Gal 2:2; Phil 3:4–8.
- **Legal-testimony / variation principle:** Thomas Starkie (1833), Simon Greenleaf (1874), William
  Paley ("substantial truth under circumstantial variety").
- **Reliability/archaeology (out of scope here — park for a gospel-reliability page, and use the
  corrected 2026 Bethesda account):** Pool of Bethesda (John 5:2), Pilate inscription, Sergius Paulus
  stone, Pool of Siloam, p52; Papias, Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* 3.1.1, Clement, Origen.
- Modern scholars engaged: Wright (2003), Bauckham (2006), Strauss (1879), Sherwin-White (1963),
  Hengel & Schwemer (1997), Maier; skeptics Crossan, Kirsopp Lake, Klausner, Jeffrey Lowder, Carrier,
  Michael Martin, Hume.
