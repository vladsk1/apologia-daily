## Argument map (SEP sections)
- **1. Concepts/definitions.** 1.1 miracle = event exceeding the productive power of nature (Aquinas,
  *SCG* 3.102–103; *ST* 1.110.4); 1.2 miracle as "violation of a law of nature" (Hume) — flagged
  problematic; Swinburne's "non-repeatable counter-instance" and Mackie's redefinition as fixes;
  1.3 religious context; definitional project hits diminishing returns.
- **2. Arguments FOR.** 2.1 categorical vs confirmatory (Swinburne P-/C-inductive); 2.2 deductive
  (Paley's syllogism; Hume's reductio) / criteriological (Leslie's four criteria) / explanatory
  (the "minimal facts" argument — Habermas, Craig; SEP questions whether scholars grant the richer
  claims: Perrin, Dodd, Sanders) / probabilistic (Bayesian odds; Butler; Babbage on independent
  witnesses; the independence assumption — Kruskal, Ahmed).
- **3. Arguments AGAINST.** 3.1.1 impossible (Spinoza; **Voltaire**, two arguments; Paley's reply);
  3.1.2 never rationally believable (Hume's maxim; interpretive debate — Hájek, Coleman, Vanderburgh,
  Millican; early critics Price, Adams). 3.2 Hume's four particular reasons: inauspicious conditions
  (Leland); passions of surprise/wonder (Morgan; Campbell replies); ignorance/barbarism (Toland);
  **parity** (Vespasian, Saragossa, Abbé Paris; Adams/Campbell/Douglas/Leland reply). 3.3 the large
  18th-c. response; modern split (Earman vs Fogelin etc.).
- **4. Arguments FROM miracles.** 4.1 would it evidence God? (Powell's fact/cause split; the "miracles
  against God" literature — Overall/Chryssides/Dawes — mostly rejected); **4.2 how much would it
  establish?** — background beliefs loom large; miracle evidence "never strictly demonstrative"; neither
  theism nor atheism establishable on it alone.

## Primaries to chase (UNVERIFIED LEADS, loci as SEP gives them)
- **Voltaire**, *Philosophical Dictionary* (1764), "Miracles" — two impossibility arguments
  (contradiction-with-immutable-law; theological: a perfect foreseeing God would not intervene).
- **Paley**, *A View of the Evidences of Christianity* (1794) — "in what way can a revelation be made
  but by miracles?"; miracles as the inevitable authentication of a revelation.
- **Antony Flew**, *God and Philosophy* (1966) — three propositions that a historian's criteria
  "inevitably preclude proof of a miracle." (We cite Flew only for the *definition*, fn 1 — a
  different locus.)
- **F. H. Bradley**, "The Presuppositions of Critical History" (1874) — the English-language cousin
  of Troeltsch's analogy principle as a ground-rule of historical method.
- **William Adams**, *An Essay in Answer to Mr. Hume's Essay on Miracles* (1767) — parity reply: a
  false miracle via a false religion is easy; a false religion via false miracles is hard.
- **George Campbell**, *A Dissertation on Miracles* (1762); **John Douglas**, *The Criterion* (1757);
  **John Leland**, *A View of the Principal Deistical Writers* (1755) — further parity/criteria replies.
- **Richard Whately**, *Historic Doubts Relative to Napoleon Bonaparte* (1819) — satire: Humean
  scruples "prove" Napoleon never existed; a **testimony-specific** "proves too much."
- **Hume**, *Enquiry* §10 Part II — his own examples, incl. the **Jansenist / Abbé Paris** tomb cures
  (conceded well-attested, yet dismissed a priori).
- **Aquinas**, *SCG* 3.102–103; *ST* 1.110.4; **Swinburne**, *The Concept of a Miracle* (1970) — the
  "non-repeatable counter-instance" definition (we cite Swinburne, not this locus).

- *Adams's parity asymmetry* — a sharper structural reply than our date/independence weighing in the

  origin-of-universe/life, further from Hume's actual terrain. **Weaker/Missing.**

is a feature. Our **honest Vespasian concession** also stands (do not trade it for a flat dismissal).

**Non-recommendation (6).** (a) The **Bayesian/odds apparatus** (Babbage, Hájek balancing, Holder,
