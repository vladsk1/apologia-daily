## Thesis (our words)
A **survey of the scholarly landscape**: Habermas catalogues **1,400+ academic publications** on Jesus'
death and resurrection (1975–2005, across Europe and North America) and reports where critical scholars —
conservative through skeptical — actually land. Its value to us is **the consensus map** (what even
non-conservative scholars grant), not a fresh argument. **(Scope: a survey/lit-review, not a proof; and
much of what it maps is already on our resurrection pages — this is chiefly consensus-confirmation + a
citation-upgrade opportunity.)**

## Argument map (the survey's areas → primaries/leads to VERIFY in the full text)
Habermas organizes the field into several major research areas. From the abstract + known content
(confirm the exact list + per-area scholars against the full text):
1. **Pre-Pauline creedal traditions + early dating of 1 Cor 15:3–7.** The near-unanimous acceptance
   (conservative→liberal) that Paul is transmitting an early creed reaching back to within a few years of
   the crucifixion. → *Primaries/leads:* **1 Cor 15:3–7; Gal 1:18–19 (the *historeō* Jerusalem visit);
   Gal 2:1–10**; scholars to confirm per the article: e.g. **Hengel, Lüdemann, Pannenberg, Bauckham** —
   *already the backbone of `library/earlycreed.html`; this is consensus-confirmation.*
2. **The disciples' experiences (the appearance data).** Even skeptical scholars grant the disciples had
   *real experiences* they believed were of the risen Jesus. → **bounded verdict guardrail:** they grant
   *experiences*, NOT the resurrection. Leads: Crossan, Lüdemann, Ehrman concessions (confirm exact
   wording/venue).
3. **Naturalistic/alternative theories** and their decline in the literature. → cross-check
   `video-research/habermas-mcdowell-alternate-theories-interview.md` (swoon, hallucination, stolen body,
   copycat) — *already served by the live `resurrection-*` + `pagan-myths-copycat` briefs.*
4. **The nature of the resurrection body ("spiritual body").** Debates over *sōma pneumatikon* (1 Cor
   15:44) — bodily vs. non-bodily readings. → cross-check `library/respred.html`; N. T. Wright.
5. **The empty tomb** and its scholarly standing.
6. **The interpretive spectrum**, framed around **J. D. Crossan vs. N. T. Wright** as the poles.

## Guardrail fit (read before pulling)
- **Resurrection rule:** lead with the early creed; this survey *documents* the consensus, it does not
  by itself prove the resurrection.
- **Bounded concession:** "even skeptics grant the disciples had experiences" — keep it to *experiences*,
  never "skeptics grant the resurrection." (This is exactly the over-concession the guardrails watch.)
- **No overstatement of "consensus":** report what the article claims scholars hold, attributed; don't
  inflate a majority into unanimity beyond what the piece says.
- **Cite the primaries, not Habermas's survey.** The survey points to creeds/texts/scholars — we cite
  *those*, verified, never "Habermas's survey says scholars agree."
