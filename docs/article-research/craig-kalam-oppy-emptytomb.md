## Argument map

### A. Craig vs Oppy on the *kalam* (*Sophia* 1993 — reply to Oppy *Religious Studies* 1991)

Craig answers Oppy under five heads. **Every move is already carried by `kalam.html`, and the essay
engages Oppy at a *later and sharper* round of the same debate** (the 2010 *Faith and Philosophy*
"Uncaused Beginnings" exchange + Oppy's 2015 reply + Craig's "Reflections," all cited in the essay's
fns 18/19/21), so this 1993 piece is an *earlier* round of a conversation we already carry at its
current state.

- **1. Existence of an actual infinite.** Oppy (via Mackie): Cantorian set theory shows infinite
  worlds are possible, so no good a priori argument blocks an infinite past. Craig's reply: *logical
  (first-order) consistency ≠ metaphysical possibility*; the actual infinite is mathematically coherent
  but the claim to be defended is that it cannot be *concretely instantiated*.
  → **Already ours**, almost verbatim: `kalam.html` concedes "Cantorian set theory shows the actual
  infinite to be perfectly coherent mathematically — Hilbert's Hotel is counterintuitive, not
  contradictory — so the defender's claim must be narrowed to this: an actual infinite cannot be
  concretely instantiated in the physical world." (Craig's supporting flourishes here — Plantinga's
  "2+1=7" / "a prime weighing more than Jackie Gleason," *Faith and Philosophy* 3 [1986]: 117;
  Wittgenstein's *Lectures on the Foundations of Mathematics* — are colour, not substance.)
- **2. Formation by successive addition.** Oppy charges Craig with a "prejudice against certain sorts
  of infinites" and assuming every series has a first member; Craig replies that an ω\* (beginningless)
  series is as impossible to *complete by successive addition* as an ω series, and that traversal does
  not require a beginning. → **Already ours**: this is exactly the Morriston "no first moment to set out
  from" exchange in `kalam.html`, with the defender's "the worry was never about a starting point"
  reply, plus the Malpass & Morriston 2020 "symmetry breaker" citation — i.e. we carry the *2020* form
  of this dispute, not just the 1991 one.
- **3. Whatever begins to exist has a cause.** Craig: *conceivability ≠ metaphysical possibility*
  (cites **Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, rev. ed. 1980, 140–42**, on necessary a posteriori truths
  whose denials are conceivable); denies the kalam proponent must *argue* for the causal premise; runs
  the Edwards point (if things can come uncaused, why not constantly?). → **Already ours in substance**:
  `kalam.html` runs the *ex nihilo nihil fit* defence, the Edwards/Prior reply (fn 20), and the
  "nothing has no properties and no powers, so it can produce nothing" metaphysical-insight reply to
  Morriston. The *one* thing the essay does **not** do is cite **Kripke** by name for the
  conceivability↔possibility step — see the discretionary non-row below.
- **4. God and the origin of the universe.** Craig's "timeless without creation, temporal since" model
  (cites his "God, Time, and Eternity," *Religious Studies* 14 [1978]) answers Mackie's "completely
  mysterious" charge; *factual* vs *broadly logical* necessity. → `kalam.html` deliberately stays
  **neutral** between classical timelessness and divine temporality (the "Then isn't God infinitely
  old?" subsection + the "Eternal, and not remote" orthonote). Craig's own contested "God could enter
  time" is **already fenced out by name** in the essay's stamp and must stay out. Not a gap — a guard.
- **5. The initial cosmological singularity.** Oppy: the Big Bang needn't be *creatio ex nihilo*; Craig:
  the singularity has no positive ontological status, and the *fallback* holds anyway — even a real
  singular state comes to be without material or efficient cause. → **Already ours**: the essay's
  "virtual particles / the vacuum is not nothing" section + the "material cause vs efficient cause"
  distinction (Bussey, fn 6) + Quentin Smith named (fn 4) cover this.

### B. Craig on the empty tomb ("Jesus' Resurrection" scholarly art. + Q&A #309)

Every empty-tomb argument Craig lists is already in `emptytomb.html`:

- Burial story implies the empty tomb; burial is historically reliable (in 1 Cor 15, in the pre-Markan
  passion source, no legendary traces, fits known tomb types, no rival burial account) → **ours**
  (§"The fact and its supports," fns 1–3, 12–13).
- 1 Cor 15 "he was buried … he was raised" presupposes a vacated grave (Jewish "the body is raised")
  → **ours** (Wright, fn 19; the whole "Paul's silence" section).
- Pre-Markan source very early (unnamed high priest ⇒ Caiaphas's tenure, within ~7 yrs) → **ours**
  (fn 13; Pesch cited).
- Simple, unembellished narrative vs the *Gospel of Peter* / *Ascension of Isaiah* → **ours**
  ("none of the guards, angelic choreography, or spectacle the second-century apocryphal gospels would
  later heap on").
- Women as discoverers (criterion of embarrassment) → **ours** (§3, Bauckham/Wright, fns 7–9).
- Enemy attestation (Matt 28 stolen-body charge) → **ours** (§2, Justin/Tertullian, fns 4–5).
- The no-cult-at-the-tomb datum → **ours** (the Greco-Roman section's close, Wright *RSG* 577).

**Scholar-counting Craig adds (Kremer's 28 + Craig's 16 more; E. E. Ellis "square circle"; van Daalen
"extremely difficult"):** deliberately **NOT imported.** `emptytomb.html` already handles the
consensus question with explicit *caution* — it names the Habermas 75% figure, flags Alter & Slade's
critique of the sample, and rests on a named roster (Brown, Wright, Grant, Maier) rather than a
headcount. Importing more scholar-tallies pushes toward the very over-counting the essay is careful to
avoid. (See verdict ⑤.)

## Six-verdict cross-check

- **① Corroboration — dominant.** Both target essays already make every substantive move in these
  Craig pieces, and `kalam.html` carries the Oppy dispute at a *more current* round (2010/2015) than the
  1993 article mines. Recorded as corroboration; no row.
- **② Error in ours — none.** Nothing in Craig contradicts a claim either essay asserts.
- **③ Weaker in ours — none that warrants a row.** The lone candidate is citing **Kripke** for the
  conceivability≠possibility step in Premise 1 (see discretionary non-row). The essay's own
  being/non-being reply already does this work number-free; adding Kripke is at most a P4 cosmetic
  citation and would re-open a **dual-consensus-tier** essay for a non-defect (rule 9 / "nine rounds is
  a failure"). **Discretionary non-row — logged here only, not in the backlog.**
- **④ Missing from ours — none.** The one empty-tomb-adjacent item these pieces add that `emptytomb.html`
  does not carry (the Church of the Holy Sepulchre identification: Constantine/Helena/Eusebius, the
  "inside the later walls" answer) is **already live on `library/archaeology.html`** — built and
  dual-consensus gated 2026-08-17, confirmed 2026-09-29 (content-backlog row at line ~134). So it is not
  missing site-wide; it is housed in the archaeology essay, which is the correct home (`emptytomb.html`'s
  stamped scope is "that grave alone"). Corroboration of already-shipped content.
- **⑤ Hazard — ours is better.** Craig's Q&A #309 states flatly that the Holy Sepulchre tomb is "in
  fact, the actual tomb" of Joseph of Arimathea. That identification is **more confident than the
  scholarship warrants and than our own frame allows.** `archaeology.html` deliberately lands the arc as
  "there is a plausible, *locatable* 1st-c. tomb site," judges Holy Sepulchre and the Garden Tomb by the
  *same* first-century-date test, denominates neither (the two sites map onto a Catholic-Orthodox /
  Protestant devotional split — strict neutrality is the blocking criterion there), and never says "this
  is the tomb." **Do not import Craig's "in fact, the actual tomb" wording into any surface.** Flag only.
- **⑥ Non-recommendation.** The scholar-tally material (Kremer 28 / +16; Ellis "square circle"; van
  Daalen) is a real body of citable support we are deliberately **not** adding, because `emptytomb.html`
  intentionally resists consensus-by-headcount (it fences the Habermas 75% figure with Alter & Slade).
  Adding it would work against the essay's own carefully-bounded "strong, well-defended position, not a
  settled fact" verdict. Logged with reason; no row.
