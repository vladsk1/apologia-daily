## What the article argues (by section)

**Part 1 (4.1, 1989) — survey + "known facts."** Sorts the field into five models (from "deny/can't-know
the appearances" to "bodily resurrection") and offers a *minimal-facts-style* argument: it lists ~11–12
"facts … agreed upon by almost all critical scholars" (crucifixion, burial, the disciples' despair, the
empty tomb [flagged as less widely accepted], the appearance experiences, the disciples' transformation,
early Jerusalem preaching, the birth of the church, Sunday worship, the conversions of James and Paul)
and argues the resurrection is the best explanation once naturalistic theories fail. **Does NOT discuss
the 1 Cor 15 creed, and gives no dates.** (This is the historical ancestor of the later "minimal facts"
work `minimalfacts.html` is built on — our essay already runs this, with current sourcing.)

**Part 2 (4.2, 1990) — the creed.** This is the part the user wanted. Claims:
- **Pre-Pauline, borrowed formula.** Evidence listed: the "delivered/received" transmission verbs;
  parallelism + stylized content; the proper names Peter and James; non-Pauline vocabulary; a possible
  Aramaic original; the Aramaic "Cephas"; a threefold "and that" (Aramaic/Mishnaic narration style); two
  "according to the Scriptures" clauses. → **All already in `earlycreed.html` §"The Marks of a Borrowed
  Formula."**
- **Dating.** "Many scholars date Paul's receiving of this creed from two to eight years after the
  crucifixion," ~AD 32–35; Paul most likely received it at the Gal 1:18–19 Jerusalem visit with Peter
  and James (1 Cor 15:5, 7). Named dating roster (n. 8): Grass, Cullmann, Goppelt, Pannenberg, Fuller,
  Dodd, Hunter, R. E. Brown, Sheehan, Ladd, Küng, Perrin. Also: **Küng** AD 35–45; **Perrin** "no later
  than AD 50"; **O'Collins** "knows of no scholar" placing reception after the AD 40s; **Grass** favours
  Damascus (→ earlier). → **Corroborates our dating; ours is tighter and better-sourced** (Lüdemann's
  "within 2–5 yrs," the Gallio/Delphi anchor, Dunn "within months").
- **Named scholars on antiquity.** **Jeremias** "the earliest tradition of all"; **Wilckens** "indubitably
  goes back to the oldest phase"; **von Campenhausen** meets "all the demands of historical reliability";
  **Hunter** repeats von Campenhausen; **Dodd** — whoever says Paul erred on the apostolic gospel "must
  bear the burden of proof."
- **Apostolic/eyewitness.** Four indications: Paul recorded early appearance material (and likely got the
  list from disciples); Paul is himself an eyewitness (15:8); the apostles taught the same message
  (15:11); Paul checked his gospel with the leadership (Gal 1:11–2:10). **Farmer** (n. 10, *CTR* 2 [1987])
  on *historēsai* in Gal 1:18 as inquiring/Petrine. → **Corroborates `earlycreed.html` §"Counting Backward"
  and `appearances.html` (the 15:11 "whether I or they" point is already on `appearances.html`).**
- **Luke 24:34.** The article says Luke 24:34 ("The Lord has risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon!")
  is "believed to be based on tradition perhaps as early as that of the creed." → **THIS IS THE ONE GAP
  (see below).**

## Verdicts

formula** — a fixed confession embedded in Luke, independent of Paul, that names the appearance to Peter
(= 1 Cor 15:5, "appeared to Cephas"). It gives the creed's FIRST appearance a **second, independent early
stream**, which directly strengthens the reply to the "it's only Paul" / legend moves the essay already
answers. Absent from all six surfaces (confirmed by grep). **Primary to verify & cite (not Habermas):**
Luke 24:34 itself + the pre-Lukan-formula scholarship (Jeremias, *New Testament Theology*; and it is noted
in standard commentaries). **Target:** `library/earlycreed.html` §"What the Creed Establishes" or the
independence point in §"Strongest Objections." **Bounded:** cite it as a *second early tradition for the
Peter appearance*, NOT as independent proof of the resurrection (same belief-vs-truth firewall the essay
already draws). Resurrection tier = dual-consensus if built.
