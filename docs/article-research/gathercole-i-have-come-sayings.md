## Thesis (our words)
Two layers.
**(1) The restated thesis of *The Preexistent Son*.** In ancient Jewish literature, **angels** repeatedly
announce themselves with the formula **"I have come" + a statement of purpose** — meaning *I have come
from somewhere else to accomplish this mission*. Jesus uses the same formula in the Synoptics, and — as
Gathercole argues — **not** about local travel (to Capernaum, to Nazareth) but about **his whole mission**:
"I have come to cast fire on the earth" (Luke 12:49); "I have not come to abolish [the Law and the
Prophets] but to fulfil them" (Matt 5:17). If the idiom carries its ordinary force, the Synoptic Jesus is
saying he **came from somewhere** — which implies **preexistence in Matthew, Mark, and Luke**, not only in
John and Paul.
**(2) The article's own contribution.** A newly identified early instance of the formula in the
Pseudo-Philonic homily **On Samson (*De Sampsone*) 13.1**, surviving only in Armenian, where the angel who
visits Manoah says **"I have come to give you good news about a gift."** Because *De Sampsone* is dated
close to the New Testament period, this is one of the **earliest and nearest** parallels yet — which
"might further bolster" the case.

## Argument map → primaries to VERIFY
**A · The Synoptic "I have come" sayings (all directly checkable — this is the core).** Gathercole names,
or the article's fn 17 lists, the following as the *eki* + purpose-infinitive set: **Mark 2:17 // Matt
9:12 // Luke 5:32**; **Matt 5:17** (twice); **Matt 10:34–35** (three times); **Luke 12:49, 51**. His book
covers the wider set at *The Preexistent Son*, **83–189**. → **Verify each in the Greek** (*ēlthon* +
infinitive of purpose) before building anything on it; do not rely on English translations, which blur
"came" and "have come."
**B · The ancient Jewish angelic parallels (the evidential base).**
- **Daniel** — **Dan 9:22–23; 10:12, 14, 20; 11:2 (OG)**. Gathercole says his discussion of the angelic
  passages *begins* with Daniel. (fn 18 adds that the Armenian Bible uses the same *eki* + infinitive
  construction at Dan 9:22–23 and 10:14.)
- **Tobit 5:5** (Codex **Sinaiticus** [א] text specifically — note the textual specification).
- **4 Ezra 6:30; 7:2**; **2 Baruch 71:3** — discussed at *Preexistent Son*, **119–124**.
- **⭐ NEW: *De Sampsone* 13.1** — the angel to Manoah. Armenian: **եկի** (*eki*, 1 sg. aor., "I have
  come") + **աւետարանել** (*awetaranel*, inf., "to announce"); in Armenian as in Greek, an infinitive
  after a verb of motion commonly expresses purpose (Meillet, *Altarmenisches Elementarbuch* [1913], 111;
  Jensen, *Altarmenische Grammatik* [1959], 215). Biblical background: **Judg 13:2–21**.
  ⚠ **A textual-honesty point Gathercole makes and we must preserve:** the modern **German and French
  translations obscure the construction** — Siegert renders it "I… am a messenger who announces…," and the
  French follows him, because both are translating a *reconstructed Greek Vorlage* rather than the Armenian
  itself. The Armenian, and the 1826 Latin *editio princeps* (Aucher: *veniens ad annunciandam*), have the
  "coming + purpose" structure. Gathercole thanks two Cambridge Armenianists (James Clackson, Bert Vaux)
  for checking it. **If we ever use this, say plainly that the reading rests on the Armenian against two
  standard modern translations** — that is exactly the sort of detail an honest presentation includes.
**C · Dating of *De Sampsone* (state the range, not the best case).** Originally attributed to Philo (so:
first century) by Aucher's 1826 *editio princeps*; **no longer attributed to Philo**, but still regarded as
an early *Jewish*, non-Christian work. Siegert: "1st c. BC to 2nd c. AD," and elsewhere more narrowly "the
time of Philo"; Stemberger: first century; the *Sources Chrétiennes* editors give termini of **2nd c. BCE
to 4th c. CE** while concluding in favour of a Philonic/NT-era date; Muradyan & Topchyan's English
translation (in *Outside the Bible*, JPS 2013) follows the cautious wide range. Judith Lieu's review
(*JJS* 52 [2001]: 160–61) cautions that texts like this "could participate in the debate as to how we
determine both date and the labels 'Jewish' or 'Christian.'" → **Use the wide range. The whole value of the
find is that it is early; overstating the date would destroy the point it is making.**

## Guardrail fit (read before pulling — this is high-stakes Christology)
- **⚠⚠⚠ THE ANGEL PROBLEM — the single biggest risk in this whole batch, and an `orthonote` CLARIFIER
  CANDIDATE.** The argument runs: *Jesus' "I have come" sayings pattern with **angelic** announcements.*
  Compressed to a card, a reel, or a pull-quote, that is one short step from **"Jesus speaks like an
  angel"** — which is precisely the **Arian / Jehovah's-Witness angel-Christology** (Jesus as Michael, a
  created spirit being) that `library/trinity_jw.html` and `library/hands.html` exist to refute. The
  guardrail: **the parallel is to the FORM of the utterance — a heavenly being announcing the purpose of
  a mission to earth — not to the NATURE of the speaker.** Gathercole's book is an argument for a **high**
  Synoptic Christology, expressly against developmental/adoptionist accounts; the angelic material is his
  evidence that the *idiom* means "come from heaven," and the Synoptics then apply that idiom to one who
  is far more than an angel. **Any use of this on-site must (a) state that limit explicitly in the same
  breath, and (b) carry an `orthonote` ("Is saying / Not saying") — the ideal wording being roughly *is
  saying: Jesus speaks in the idiom of one who came from heaven; **not** saying: Jesus is an angel.***
  Non-interactive formats (reel, card, push copy) cannot host the ＊ box, so there the fix is a **wording**
  fix — or simply don't compress this argument into those formats at all.
- **⚠ DUAL-CONSENSUS REQUIRED.** Deity/person of Christ ⇒ `apologia-orthodoxy` **and** `apologia-neutrality`
  must both certify CLEAN before anything ships (`CLAUDE.md` § *Deploy workflow*).
- **⚠ Keep Gathercole's own bound.** His closing verb is **"might therefore further bolster"** — a
  cumulative contribution to a case, not a proof. And the thesis is **contested**: it is a minority-to-
  moderate position in Synoptic Christology, not a consensus. Present it as *a serious scholarly argument
  that the Synoptics already carry preexistence*, never as "scholars agree the Synoptics teach
  preexistence." (This is the fine-tuning rule's shape: the *data* — the idiom's distribution — is the
  checkable part; the *christological inference* is the contested conclusion.)
- **⚠ Steelman owed.** The standard counter is that *ēlthon* + purpose is ordinary Greek idiom that need
  not imply heavenly origin, and that Mark 1:38 ("let us go… for that is why I came out") plausibly refers
  to leaving Capernaum. A responsible presentation must state that objection at full strength before
  answering — the article, being a research note, does not.
- **⚠ Denominational neutrality:** **Tobit** and **4 Ezra / 2 Baruch** are deuterocanonical or
  pseudepigraphal — canonical for some traditions, not others. Cite them strictly as **evidence for a
  first-century Jewish literary idiom**, never with any implication about canonical status. (Same standing
  flag as `book-research/in-defense-of-the-bible.md` ch. 16 and the Millard note.)
- **No fabrication:** the Armenian, the German/French translation divergence, and the *De Sampsone* dating
  spread must all be reported as they stand. Do not simplify.
