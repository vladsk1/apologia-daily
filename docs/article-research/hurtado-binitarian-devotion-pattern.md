## Distinct from what we already cite
This 2008 essay is a **compressed restatement** of the thesis of *Lord Jesus Christ* / *At the Origins
of Christian Worship*, with one added accent: it argues that the binitarian devotional **practice was a
driver of later doctrinal articulation** (building on Maurice Wiles, *The Making of Christian Doctrine*,
1967) and illustrates that from **Justin Martyr's exegetical defense of worshipping Jesus**. The
worship-drove-the-definitions thesis is **already the spine of `early_church_trinity.html`** ("The
praying came first; the definitions came to defend the praying"). The only genuinely fresh material is
in the *case studies*: Justin's Isaiah-42:8 / "Name-and-glory" defense (Dial. 63–65) and the
"prophetic-oracles-in-Jesus'-name" datum.

## Argument map (full text read)
- **Thesis.** Earliest Christian devotional *practice* — Jesus reverenced alongside God while strict
  monotheism is kept — is (a) a remarkable phenomenon in its own right and (b) a significant *driver*
  of doctrinal development about God and Jesus. Cites Maurice Wiles as precedent (*lex orandi* shapes
  doctrine), but corrects Wiles: the NT worship of Jesus was not merely "popular" piety but
  leader-affirmed and official.
- **The "binitarian shape."** Two distinguishable yet linked figures (God and Jesus) are the sole
  rightful recipients of devotion; a "shaped two-ishness" that keeps functional subordination of Jesus
  to the Father inside monotheism. John 5:23 is "perhaps the most explicit" ("honour the Son just as
  the Father"). Phil 2:6–11 acclamation *Kyrios Iēsous Christos*; 1 Cor 12:3 / Rom 10:9 *Kyrios Iēsous*
  as cultic acclamations; *epikaleō* ("call upon," Rom 10:13 quoting Joel 3:5 LXX).
- **The specific devotional actions (the constellation that has no Jewish analogy):** prayer to Jesus
  (1 Thess 3:11–13; 2 Cor 12:8–9; Acts 7:59–60) and the grace/peace formulae; ritual invocation /
  confession (1 Cor 1:2 "all who call on the name"; the Aramaic *Marana tha*, 1 Cor 16:22); baptism in
  Jesus' name (Acts 2:38; 8:16; 10:48) and name-use in healing/exorcism; the Lord's Supper (*deipnon
  kyriakon*, 1 Cor 11; *eis tēn emēn anamnēsin*); hymns to Christ (Pliny; the NT hymn passages);
  **prophetic oracles uttered in Jesus' name** (David Aune: 19 NT instances + 9 in the Odes of
  Solomon — against the Deut 13 scruple about prophecy in any name but YHWH's).
- **Early + widespread:** Aramaic *Marana tha* (Jerusalem church) + the Aramaic *Abba* (Rom 8:15; Gal
  4:6) together form the binitarian pattern; 1 Cor 1:2 "in every place."
- **Case studies — worship driving exegetical theology:**
  - A NT example: Phil 2:9–11 as a **binitarian reading of Isaiah 45:22–25** ("charismatic exegesis,"
    Aune) that bases reverence for Jesus in God's exaltation of him while serving "the glory of God the
    Father."
  - **Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 63–65:** Trypho's *strongest* objection is specifically to
    **worshipping** Jesus (Dial. 38.1; 64.1; 65). Justin defends the practice by exegesis — Psalm
    45:6–11, Psalm 99, Psalm 72:17–19 ("his glorious Name") — and, most strikingly, reinterprets
    **Isaiah 42:8** ("my glory I will not give to another," Trypho's own proof-text) to mean God shares
    his glory with "his Christ **alone**" (the closed circle of God and his "Name"). John 12:37–43
    reads Isaiah 53:1 ("arm of the Lord") and Isaiah 6:1–5 (the throne-vision glory) christologically.
  - **Justin's further terms:** Dial. 61 (Son "begotten … by an act of will," fire-from-fire analogy);
    Dial. 128 (light-from-sun; the Son "numerically distinct," *arithmō heteron*, without dividing the
    Father's *ousia*); 1 Apol. 13 (Son "in the second place," Spirit "in the third"); **1 Apol. 36.2 —
    the earliest extant use of *prosōpon*** to represent Father/Son unity-and-distinction (anticipating
    Latin *persona*/*substantia*).
- **Conclusion:** the binitarian worship pattern was unprecedented in Judaism, required and *shaped*
  the exegetical/theological effort to accommodate it within monotheism, and produced a genuinely novel
  "exclusivist monotheism" — the matrix out of which Trinitarian doctrine was articulated.
