case we already carry. The genuine value is in **three specific, bounded "verdict-3 (weaker-in-ours)"

currently leave half-made.

### "Is the source better than our essays anywhere? Where, and how?" (mandatory standing answer)
**Yes — in three narrow places only, none of them a rewrite:**
1. **`eternal_generation.html`'s steelman of the subordination/incoherence charge rests on Wayne Grudem,
   who *recanted* (affirmed eternal generation at ETS, Nov 2016).** That subtly weakens the objection
   ("even the skeptic changed his mind"). **William Lane Craig did not recant** and presses the charge
   harder and more precisely (below). Naming Craig strengthens the steelman — and our essay *already
   carries the reply* (Calvin's *autotheos*).
2. **`philosophical_trinity.html`'s "Honest State of the Scholarship" reports Tuggy's verdict but answers
   only his *logical* (transitivity) argument** — it leaves his *historical* ("the Trinity replaced the
   Father") and *substance-metaphysics* claims unanswered. **Radde-Gallwitz (2020) answers exactly
   those**, by name.
3. **`philosophical_trinity.html`'s model-survey (social vs. Latin / relative-identity / constitution)
   omits the now most-discussed recent development: the "conciliar" route (Pawl)** — read the doctrine
   off the first seven councils; the councils leave the person–nature relation open; the processions do
   not entail objectionable subordination. This sits *closest to our essay's own "read it off the creeds"
   instinct*, and Owen & Dunne (2019) independently converge on our footnote-3 point (a third sense of
   "is" beyond identity and predication).
**Everywhere else, ours is as good or better** (see corroboration list).

## Article-by-article verdicts

### 2019 "The Son of God" (Vol. 3 No. 1)

**① W. L. Craig, "Is God the Son Begotten in His Divine Nature?" (pp. 22–32) — READ IN FULL.**
→ `eternal_generation.html`. **Verdict 3 (weaker-in-ours) → P3 row.**
Craig **rejects** eternal generation *of the Son in his divine nature*, on two grounds: (1) **no clear
scriptural warrant** — he reads *monogenēs* (and the sonship texts) as about the **Incarnation / the
historical Jesus's special sonship**, not a pre-creation eternal procession (he follows Marcellus of
Ancyra's observation that the Logos is not called "Son" until after the incarnation, while correcting
Marcellus); (2) **it introduces an objectionable ontological subordinationism** — if the Son *derives*
his being from the Father, he exists *ab alio*, not *a se*, and so **lacks aseity**, which Craig argues
is a great-making property the Father alone would then have ("to be dependent upon the Unoriginate for
one's existence is to lack a ground of being in oneself alone… the same way in which created things
exist," citing Leftow). He relocates sonship to the **economic** Trinity (the persons of the ontological
Trinity are "equal and underived"; "the Son is whichever person becomes incarnate").
⚠⚠ **HAZARDS — read before building this row:**
- **Craig AFFIRMS the full deity, *homoousios*, co-eternity, and uncreatedness of the Son.** This is an
  **intra-orthodox** dispute about *how* to secure the Son's deity, **not** a denial of it. Never present
  Craig as Arian or as denying Christ's deity. Steelman his **objection**; do **not** endorse his
  **positive model** (sonship as purely economic; "Father"/"Son" as near-arbitrary role-labels), which
  itself strains Nicaea from the *opposite* side and is contested.
- **The reply is already in our own essay.** Craig's aseity objection is precisely what **Calvin's
  *autotheos*** answers — the essay already cites it (fn 8, *Institutes* I.13.23–29; Ellis, *Calvin,
  Classical Trinitarianism, and the Aseity of the Son*, OUP 2012): the **person** of the Son is eternally
  *from* the Father, but the **deity/essence** is *a se*, not a "secondhand" divinity received as from
  another god. Craig's charge directly contests our John 5:26 `orthonote` ("granted names an eternal
  relation of origin, never a dependence in *being*"); the autotheos move is the classical answer that
  keeps the orthonote true. **Deploy the answer we already have; name the critic we currently omit.**
- **Dual-consensus tier** (deity/Trinity). ⚠ Mark Makin's "essential dependence" model of eternal
  generation (*Religious Studies* 54.3, 2018) is Craig's named foil — a defender, available if the reply
  needs reinforcing; Craig critiques it in his footnote 5.
- *Also:* Craig's *own* social model (one soul, three sets of rational faculties — Moreland & Craig,
  *Philosophical Foundations* ch. 29) is **already cited** in `philosophical_trinity.html` (the Cerberus
  passage). Corroboration there; no row.

**② Skylar D. McManus, "Oneness Pentecostalism, the Two-Minds View, and the Problem of Jesus's Prayers"
(pp. 60–87) — abstract + conclusion read.**
→ `modalism.html`. **✅ BUILT & CERTIFIED 2026-11-08 (see action-item 3 below) — the optional naming edit was made, gated clean, and kept on the orthodox side of the Nestorian line. The analysis below is now HISTORY, not a to-do.**
→ `modalism.html`. **Verdict 5 (ours is better) + optional 4 → P4 row at most.**
McManus argues Oneness Pentecostals **can** adopt Thomas V. Morris's **two-minds view** (*The Logic of
God Incarnate*, 1986) to answer the "Jesus prays to the Father, so they must be two persons" objection —
blocking the inference from "Jesus prays to the Father" to "Jesus ≠ the Father" — **provided they drop
the claim that Jesus believes he simply *is* the Father.** Our essay **already defeats this move
decisively** without naming it: the **John 17 argument** (the petitioner asks for "the glory that I had
with you before the world existed," 17:5, and "you loved me before the foundation of the world," 17:24)
reaches **behind the incarnation**, so it **cannot** be the human nature/human mind addressing the
indwelling deity — exactly the gap a two-minds-of-the-incarnate-Jesus account cannot cross.
⚠⚠⚠ **HEAVY HAZARD — this is why it is at most P4 and may be left as a flag:** do **NOT** import Morris's
two-minds Christology into our own voice. Our Gethsemane `orthonote` deliberately grounds the scene in
**one undivided divine will + the Son's genuine *human* will** (the Chalcedonian/dyothelite answer) — not
in "two minds / two centres of consciousness," which drifts toward **Nestorianism** (two subjects). The
only safe use is to *name* the two-minds-modalism reply as a live move a reader might meet, and show
John 17:5/24 is precisely what it cannot absorb. If in doubt, leave `modalism.html` as-is (it already
wins) and log this as corroboration.

**③ Matthew Owen & John Anthony Dunne, "The Son of God and Trinitarian Identity Statements" (pp. 33–59)
— abstract + landing-page read.**
→ `philosophical_trinity.html` (identity section / fn 3). **Verdict 3, bundle into the phil-trinity row.**
They test "the Son is God" under the two readings of "is": on **numerical identity**, "logical
incoherence seems immanent"; on **predication**, it is inaccurate and licenses polytheism — so they argue
Trinitarian discourse needs a **third sense of "is."** This independently reinforces our essay's own
footnote-3 move (the counting reply works only once "is God" is *not* strict identity). Cite alongside
Pawl as converging evidence, not as a separate row.

### 2020 "Conciliar Trinitarianism" (Vol. 4 No. 2)

**④ Timothy Pawl, "Conciliar Trinitarianism, Divine Identity Claims, and Subordination" (pp. 102–128)
— READ IN FULL.**
→ `philosophical_trinity.html` (+ possibly `relations.html` / `eternal_generation.html`). **Verdict 3/4 →
P3 row (the phil-trinity row).**
Pawl extends his well-known **Conciliar Christology** program (OUP 2016) to the Trinity: read the doctrine
**off the first seven ecumenical councils**. Two load-bearing moves for us: (a) the councils answer "what
is there one of / three of / how related / what attributes," but **leave the person–nature relation
open**, and **neither strict identity nor instantiation fits the conciliar texts** (strict identity
collapses the persons by transitivity; instantiation makes three gods / tritheism) — so the "third sense
of is" our essay's fn 3 gestures at is exactly the open question; (b) **the processions do not entail
objectionable ontological subordination** — a proponent of Conciliar Trinitarianism can affirm the Son is
begotten / the Spirit proceeds *and* deny that this ranks them in deity. Move (b) is also useful
**corroboration** for `eternal_generation.html`'s reply to the subordination charge, and a direct answer
to Craig's move (①). Pawl is directly citable (OA). ⚠ He brackets the *Filioque* throughout (neutrality-safe).

**⑤ Andrew Radde-Gallwitz, "God is Not a Thing: A Response to Dale Tuggy" (pp. 52–68) — READ IN FULL.**
→ `philosophical_trinity.html` ("Honest State of the Scholarship") + `modalism.html`. **Verdict 3 →
bundle into the phil-trinity row.**
A direct, high-quality rebuttal of **Tuggy — whom BOTH our essays cite as the lead critic** — from a
leading Cappadocian scholar (Notre Dame). Three points, answering exactly the Tuggy claims our essay
currently leaves standing: (1) Tuggy's **historical "replacement" thesis is unproven** — the
Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed says "one God **the Father** almighty" and does not identify the three
collectively with the one God; showing a few authors made the identification does not show the older
"one deity in three hypostases" idiom died out; (2) Tuggy's **concept of substance is anachronistic** —
he treats universals as causally-inert **abstract properties** exemplified by concrete particulars (the
same analytic dogma behind Plantinga's simplicity critique, which Wolterstorff answered), whereas for the
Cappadocians (and Aristotle's hylomorphism, and Aquinas) a nature/form is a **real component** that
grounds activity, not a causally-inert property — so Gregory of Nyssa's "distinguished in hypostasis, not
divided in underlying subject" is **not** the contradiction Tuggy alleges; (3) Tuggy wrongly treats
**"God" and "Father" as proper names.** Naming this rebuttal lets our essay's claim — "Tuggy's verdict is
a verdict on the models, and does not reach the doctrine" — actually answer Tuggy's *own* argument, not
just the transitivity puzzle. ⚠ Note Radde-Gallwitz mentions **John Behr & Beau Branson's "Monarchian
Trinitarianism"** (the Father is the one God) — the same **monarchy-of-the-Father** theme our essay
already fences with an `orthonote`; corroboration, keep the fence.

**⑥ Mark Edwards, "Is Subordinationism a Heresy?" (pp. 69–86) + R. T. Mullins reply (pp. 87–101) —
abstract + conclusion read.**
→ `eternal_generation.html` (the 2016 EFS section). **Verdict ≈ corroboration (ours already careful) +
optional light cite → P4, NEUTRALITY-SENSITIVE.**
Edwards (Oxford patristics) argues the "subordinationism = heresy" charge "outpaces historical knowledge":
all Christians **before Nicaea** held the persons "differed in rank" (aetiologically, by relation of
origin); **not every subordinationist is Arian**; the "Nicaea taught strict equality of persons" reading
was a systematic-theologians' construction historians shared "only until recently." Mullins replies,
defending that eternal-authority-submission is still problematic. **Our essay already makes a sophisticated
version of Edwards's point** ("an inference drawn from Catholic, Orthodox, or classical Reformed teaching
should not be reported as that tradition's rendered verdict on a question it never faced"). ⚠⚠ **HAZARD:
`eternal_generation.html` is deliberately NEUTRAL on the intramural EFS dispute. Over-citing Edwards
tilts toward the EFS/ERAS side; over-citing Mullins tilts against. If used at all, cite the *pair* to
show the HISTORICAL label is contested among specialists — never to adjudicate.** Most likely: no row,
corroboration only.

**⑦ Dennis Bray, "Gregory of Nazianzus' Trinitarian Argument in Oration 23" (pp. 138–160) —
abstract + landing-page read.** → `/sources` candidate? **Verdict 5 (ours already better-sourced) →
NO ROW.** Bray analyzes Gregory of Nazianzus's *Third Oration on Peace* (Or. 23), anti-Eunomian (four

(no public-domain English translation ships with Schaff's NPNF2-07), so it **cannot** enter `/sources`

genuinely PD English translation surfaces; we already have the better PD Cappadocian material.
