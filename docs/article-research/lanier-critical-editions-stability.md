## Thesis (our words)
A **quantitative** study of how much the *reconstructed* Greek biblical text has actually changed across
roughly a century of textual-critical work — run in parallel on the **New Testament** (Westcott–Hort 1881
→ Nestle–Aland / *Editio Critica Maior*) and the **Greek Old Testament / Septuagint** (Rahlfs 1935 →
Göttingen). Both fields poured decades of international labour and a vastly larger pool of manuscript
witnesses into new critical editions. Lanier's finding: the resulting text is **~98.5% identical to the
edition it replaced** on *both* sides of the canon. Some textual critics read that as a letdown (J. K.
Elliott's "damp squib" / "bland result"); Lanier argues the honest reading is the opposite — it indirectly
indicates that the main line of transmission of the Greek Bible was **not wild and uncontrolled but
exceptionally stable**. **(Scope: this is a claim about TRANSMISSION and about the STABILITY OF SCHOLARLY
RECONSTRUCTION — it says nothing whatever about whether the content is true. See the guardrail below.)**

## Argument map (beats in our words → the primaries/data to VERIFY)

**Frame · the two analogous streams.** NT: Westcott & Hort, *The New Testament in the Original Greek*
(1881) → Nestle (1898/1901) → Nestle–Aland → the INTF *Editio Critica Maior* (ECM), which uses the
**Coherence-Based Genealogical Method (CBGM)** and has released Catholic Epistles (2nd ed. 2013) and
Acts (2017). OT: Alfred Rahlfs, *Septuaginta* (1935; a self-declared *provisional* hand edition, revised
by Hanhart 2006) → the Göttingen *Septuaginta: Vetus Testamentum Graecum* series (~75% complete at
writing). Both older editions leaned heavily on the three great majuscules (**Sinaiticus, Vaticanus,
Alexandrinus**); both successor projects tapped hundreds of additional witnesses (ECM: 180 Greek
witnesses for the Catholic Epistles, 135 for Acts; Wevers's Göttingen *Genesis*: 132 Greek witnesses —
8 majuscules, 29 papyri, 95 minuscules).

**Method (verify before quoting any percentage).** Lanier defines a **"textual unit"** — variant units
*plus* non-variant units, so the whole text is the denominator, not just the variants. Basis: the ECM
identifies **10,509 variant units** across Acts + the Catholic Epistles, comprising **22,172 words**, i.e.
**~2.1 words per unit**; that is 85% of the 26,058 total words, and the remaining 3,886 words are assumed
to divide into ~2.1-word units too, giving **12,356 total textual units**. Percentages then = (units −
differences) / units. He collated **~5.5% samples** of each corpus, chosen essentially at random, excluding
movable-nu, punctuation, accents, versification, and minor orthographic differences.

**1 · Greek NT results (the headline numbers to verify).**
- **NA-25 vs. Westcott–Hort: 558 differences → ~99.2% agreement** (the Alands' own figure, contextualised
  by Lanier's denominator). By corpus: Gospels 99.0 · Acts 99.2 · Pauline 99.4 · Catholic 98.9 · Rev 99.0.
- **NA-28/ECM vs. Westcott–Hort: ~98.5% agreement** (Lanier's own collation: 416 verses, est. 3,661
  textual units, 55 differences). By corpus: Gospels 98.8 · Acts 97.2 · Pauline 99.2 · Catholic 98.1 ·
  Rev 99.3.
- **⭐ THGNT (Tyndale House, Jongkind & Williams eds., Crossway 2017) vs. Westcott–Hort: ~98.4%** (59
  differences over the same 3,661 units) — and **THGNT vs. NA/ECM: ~98.8%** (99.5% in the Catholics).
  *Why this matters most:* THGNT starts from **Tregelles** (not WH), uses a more philologically focused
  method, and relies almost exclusively on **pre-sixth-century witnesses** — an independent route to
  nearly the same text.
- **SBLGNT (Holmes, SBL 2010) vs. Westcott–Hort: ~98.8%** (800 differences per Holmes's own preface).
- **⭐ Byzantine text vs. Westcott–Hort: ~92–93%** — the *most* methodologically opposed reconstruction
  (minuscules/later majuscules, Byzantine-priority method) still agrees ~92–93%, i.e. the outer bound of
  live disagreement is ~7–8%, and that includes the well-known set-pieces (ending of Mark, *pericope
  adulterae*).
- **⭐ Manuscript-level:** NA/ECM fully agrees with **Codex Vaticanus at ~96.3%**, and with **at least one
  of the major majuscules 99.9% of the time.** → *Primary to verify:* Gregory R. Lanier, "Taking Inventory
  on the 'Age of the Minuscules': Later Manuscripts and the Byzantine Tradition within the Field of
  Textual Criticism," *Currents in Biblical Research* 16/3 (2018): 263–308, at 279.
- Net change to the text of **Acts** in the latest ECM relative to NA-27: **less than 0.7%**. NA-28
  introduced 33 changes (Catholic Epistles only); the ECM of Acts, 52.
- **Total variants** in the Greek NT: **~500,000** on the current peer-reviewed estimate → *Primary:*
  **Peter J. Gurry, "The Number of Variants in the Greek New Testament: A Proposed Estimate," *New
  Testament Studies* 62/1 (2016): 97–121.**

**2 · Greek OT / Septuagint results (net-new territory for us — we have no OT-text essay).**
- **Göttingen vs. Rahlfs overall: ~98.5%** (1,245 verses collated, est. 22,369 textual units, 331
  differences). By editor: **Wevers** (Gen/Exod/Num/Deut) 98.8 · **Hanhart** (2 Chron, Esdras A, Esdras B,
  Tobit) 99.1 · **Rahlfs** (Psalms) 100.0 · **Ziegler** (Job, Sirach) 99.0 · **Ziegler** (Prophets: Isa,
  Jer, Baruch, Ezek, Hos, Zech, Daniel) 98.1 · **Kappler** (1 Maccabees) 98.5.
- Range within that: Isaiah **99.6%**; Esdras A 99.8; 2 Chronicles 99.3; Esdras B 97.9; Deuteronomy 98.3.
- **⚠ The honest outliers (must be carried with the headline).** **Daniel OG ~91.7%** (Ziegler–Munnich's
  revision made full use of **Papyrus 967**; the parallel Daniel Θ is 97.1%). Jonathan Hong's study of
  **LXX Psalms 49 and 103** suggests up to ~5.2% divergence from Rahlfs. The **Antiochene** critical
  edition of 1–2 Kingdoms sits at **83.8%** vs. Rahlfs (91.5% if typical Antiochene pluses are excluded) —
  though Lanier notes it is not trying to reconstruct the same thing.
- → *Primaries to verify:* Rahlfs, *Septuaginta* (1935; Rahlfs–Hanhart *editio altera*, 2006); the
  Göttingen fascicles named above; Jonathan Hong, "In Search of the 'Old Greek' in the Septuagint Psalter:
  A Case Study of LXX Psalms 49 and 103," *TC: A Journal of Biblical Textual Criticism* 23 (2018): 1–12;
  Ziegler & Munnich, *Susanna, Daniel, Bel et Draco*, 2nd ed. (Göttingen, 1999); Fernández Marcos & Busto
  Saiz, *El Texto Antioqueno de la Biblia Griega I: 1–2 Samuel* (Madrid, 1989); Jobes & Silva,
  *Invitation to the Septuagint*, 2nd ed. (Baker Academic, 2015), 69–70.

**3 · Lanier's four concluding observations (our words).** (a) Westcott, Hort, and Rahlfs were
exceptionally good — their pre-computer text has stood up. (b) Editors in the main streams are
**conservative as editors**. (c) Modern textual criticism is now more about the *apparatus* (and the
history of the text and its reception) than about changing the *Ausgangstext*. (d) **Confidence in the
transmission of the early Greek NT and OT should be very high** — the "bland result" indirectly indicates
a transmission line that was stable rather than wild; ironically, the sea of newly-catalogued variants
leaves us with *more*, not less, confidence in the reconstructed text.

**Steelman kept on the record.** The dissenting voice inside the article is **J. K. Elliott**, who called
the ECM results a "damp squib" and a "bland result" — the disappointment of a thoroughgoing eclectic who
expected the new data to yield a substantially different text. → *Primaries:* Elliott's reviews in
*Novum Testamentum* 40/2 (1998): 195–204, and *JTS* 64/2 (2013): 636–42; cf. his more sanguine *JTS* 69/1
(2018): 286–94.

## Guardrail fit (read before pulling)
- **⚠ Bible-reliability rule (`CLAUDE.md`): manuscripts prove ACCURATE PRESERVATION, not that the content
  is TRUE.** Everything here is transmission-only. Never let "98.5% stable across a century of editing"
  slide toward "so the New Testament is true." `library/manuscript.html` already models this bound
  perfectly — keep it.
- **⚠⚠ THE BIG HONESTY FLAG — do not present ~98.5% as pure independent confirmation.** Lanier himself
  says part of the stability is **editorial conservatism**: several Göttingen editors (Hanhart explicitly)
  *start from the Rahlfs text and revise only where necessary*, and the NT field carries what Holmes calls
  a "Hortian" inertia. An argument gate would (rightly) flag "the text hasn't changed in 140 years, so it
  was never changing" as an overstatement. **The defensible form is narrower and still strong:** the
  comparanda that are *methodologically independent* — the **THGNT** (different base text, different
  method, pre-6th-century witnesses only) at ~98.4%, the **SBLGNT** at ~98.8%, and above all the
  **Byzantine** tradition (opposite method, opposite manuscript base) at ~92–93% — converge anyway. That
  convergence is not explainable by editorial inertia. Lead with those, not with the raw ECM-vs-WH number.
- **⚠ Method caveats to state if any figure is used:** these are **Lanier's own collations of ~5.5%
  samples**, using an **estimated** "textual unit" denominator (~2.1 words/unit) extrapolated from the ECM
  data. He says so plainly; we must too. Don't round "~98.5%" into "98.5% of the Bible."
- **⚠ Carry the outliers.** Daniel OG (~91.7%) and the Antiochene 1–2 Kingdoms (~83.8%) are real and must
  travel with the headline. Suppressing them would be exactly the manufactured confidence
  `CLAUDE.md` § *The mission* forbids.
- **Denominational neutrality:** the Göttingen/Rahlfs corpus includes **Tobit, Sirach, Baruch, and
  1 Maccabees**. Discuss them as *Greek OT textual witnesses* — never adjudicate their canonical status.
  Same discipline as the `in-defense-of-the-bible.md` ch. 16 flag.
- **Author tilt:** RTS (confessional Reformed) and Tyndale House (evangelical), but the piece is a
  quantitative collation study; the numbers are checkable and the caveats are the author's own. Low
  overstatement risk from the source — the risk is ours in compressing it.
- **Not to be confused:** the ~500,000 variant figure (Gurry) is *higher* than the 200,000–400,000 our
  essay currently attributes to Ehrman. That is fine and in fact more honest — the variant count was never
  the point. If we adopt Gurry, adopt him **with** the "by-product of abundance" framing already live.
