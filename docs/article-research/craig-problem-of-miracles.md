## Argument map
**"The Problem of Miracles" (scholarly — qualitative throughout, no numbers):**
- Rejects "violation of a law of nature" as **incoherent** on both regularity and necessitarian
  accounts; prefers a miracle as a **"naturally impossible event"** requiring a supernatural cause.
  Endorses "natural law is description, not prescription," but warns the defender must drop "violation"
  framing. (We deliberately use Flew's definition instead — see non-recs.)
- Logical / natural / historical possibility: given a transcendent personal God a miracle is
  *historically* possible — "only an atheist can deny the historical possibility of miracles" (the
  agnostic must leave the door open).
- **Spinoza:** objection (1) is "system-dependent upon pantheism"; objection (2) irrelevant to
  apologists who used miracles to show God's *action* not his existence; via Swinburne, one
  non-repeatable counter-instance leaves a law intact. (Credits the pantheism-dependence reply to
  Vernet, Houtteville, Less.)
- **Hume Part I:** "question-begging or mistaken" — "uniform experience" either assumes the miracle
  did not occur, or is merely general and compatible with it. **Confuses science and history:** a
  regularity shows at most *natural* impossibility, never that the event *did not occur*. Via Sherlock,
  a miracle is sense-perceptible and testimony-provable; via Paley, Hume's principle yields unrealistic
  scepticism.
- **Hume Part II:** adopts Less's + Paley's point-by-point replies; Paley's test case = twelve honest
  men who die rather than recant their report of a miracle. Prior improbability is one "sound
  historical testimony [can] surmount." **No numbers.**
- **Historian / analogy:** Flew makes "an unjustifiable identification" of natural with historical
  impossibility (question-begging); engages Nineham ("purely human phenomena"), Becker's relativism
  ("no amount of testimony … a thing that cannot be found in present reality"), identifies Becker with
  Troeltsch; endorses **Pannenberg**: analogy interprets the unclear by the known, it does not exclude
  every non-analogous event.

**Craig–Ehrman debate (RETIRED-math material):** "Ehrman's Egregious Error" — Ehrman computed only the
intrinsic Pr(R/B) and ignored Pr(R/B&E); a low intrinsic prior does not settle the posterior. Craig
**concedes historians don't use Bayes** ("this isn't a debate about what … historians are permitted to
do") and deploys the calculus only to rebut Hume/Ehrman; calls Ehrman's "historians can't speak about
God" line "literally self-refuting."

  the definition).

  **Swinburne** (*Concept of Miracle*, 1970), **Earman** (*Hume's Abject Failure*, 2000).

## Six-verdict cross-check
**Corroboration (1):** our "violation is question-begging / description-not-prescription"; our
Spinoza-presupposes-pantheism rebuttal; our Lewis circularity reply to Hume I; our Pannenberg handling
of analogy; our minimalfacts "won't suffer and die for what they'd know to be a fraud" (Paley 1794 is a
citable primary behind it). The debate independently **vindicates our Ehrman fence** (he disputes the
empty tomb while granting crucifixion + the disciples' experiences).

**Error-in-ours (2):** none found.

**Weaker-in-ours (3):** (a) the **science/history cut** — Craig's "a regularity shows at most *natural*
impossibility, never that the event *occurred*" is a sharper single sentence than our scattered
pieces; (b) the **possibility verdict** — "only an atheist can deny the historical possibility" (the
agnostic can't rule it out a priori) is a crisper landing than our longer closed-box treatment.

**Missing (4):** the early-modern evidentialist lineage (Sherlock 1729 / Less 1758 / Paley 1794) that
answered Hume II on its own terms — our essays jump from Hume to 20th–21st-c. respondents; Becker +
Nineham as named proponents of the historian's-limit objection; and **Craig himself is cited nowhere**
in either essay — for the resurrection-as-best-explanation case he is the most obvious missing
authority ("The Problem of Miracles" is a clean add).

**Hazard — ours is better (5):** our honest Vespasian concession (Tacitus/Suetonius "within about the
same span of time as the Gospels") — Craig, via Paley, simply calls Vespasian "not comparable"; **keep
ours**, the candour is the stronger posture. And the whole numerical/slide apparatus breaches the
retired-math rule.

**Non-recommendation (6):** (a) import Craig's Bayesian "Egregious Error" calculation / the Pr(R/B&E)
formula / Swinburne's ~97% / "Y→0 ⇒ ratio→1" — retired math; the qualitative kernel is already in both
essays (miracles.html's "metaphysical prior, not a finding of experience" + Earman; minimalfacts's
Allison "probability is in the eye of the beholder"). (b) Swap our Flew-based definition for Craig's
"naturally impossible event" — no gain, and it disturbs certified sibling wording.

## Is Craig better than our essays anywhere?
Only in **two narrow, qualitative, framing** spots: the science/history cut in the Hume-I rebuttal, and
the crispness of the possibility verdict. Everywhere else our essays are equal or better — more candid
(Vespasian), broader on modern critics (Allison, Cavin, Carrier, Loke, McGrew/Larmer), and free of the
numerical apparatus Craig leans on against Ehrman. The qualitative Bayesian point he is famous for is
already present in both essays without the math.
