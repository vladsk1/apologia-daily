## Argument map (our words — do not reproduce the paper's prose)
The paper is a **defence of Loke's "New Moral Argument" (Loke 2023) against a 2024 critique** by Evan
Jack, Mustafa Khuramy & Erik Schulz ("The New Moral Argument for God Fares No Better," *Res
Philosophica* 101 [2024]: 705–14).

- **Loke's "New Moral Argument"** is an *elimination/disjunctive syllogism* (same shape as his
  resurrection book's "provably exhaustive" method): objective moral truths exist → they are grounded
  in an impersonal entity, a non-divine personal entity, a divine personal entity (God), **or they are
  brute facts** → eliminate all but God → therefore God. It is a *different* structure from Craig's
  "If God does not exist, objective moral values and duties do not exist."
- **Jack et al.'s central objection = "relaxed (moral) realism"** (they credit Parfit, Scanlon,
  Dworkin): a metaethical view on which objective moral truths exist but have **no truthmaker at all /
  are not metaphysically substantive / morality is "autonomous" and immune from ontology.** They argue
  Loke's disjunction is not exhaustive because it omits this option (relaxed realism is *distinct from*
  the "brute fact" horn, which does posit facts-as-truthmakers that merely lack explanation).
- **Teh & Loke's replies** (the mineable content):
  1. **Fix the formulation**: replace "brute fact" in the disjunction with "metaphysically
     ungrounded," which absorbs relaxed realism (and add the "combination of impersonal + non-divine
     personal" horn Loke's original omitted). The reconstructed Jack et al. version works but is
     needlessly long.
  2. **The math analogy fails (category mistake).** Relaxed realists lean on "moral truths are like
     mathematical truths — objective, irreducible, needing no grounding." Reply: math is *abstract and
     independent of persons/relationships*; morality is about *obligations, rights, duties owed between
     persons*. Different ontological domains; the analogy is a category mistake. (They cite Sarah
     McGrath 2014, "Relax? Don't Do It! Why Moral Realism Won't Come Cheap," which argues the same
     against cheap realism.)
  3. **Negative existentials fail (category mistake).** Jack et al.'s "canvas" picture treats moral
     truths like "Satoru Gojo doesn't exist" — truths that add no "paint" to reality. Reply:
     non-existence has nothing to ground; an *existing* moral truth ("rape is wrong") requires a real
     wrongness-property, so the parallel collapses.
  4. **Relaxed realism faces a collapse worry (toward quasi-realism).** The "canvas" picture lets each
     person add their own "paint," which looks like subjectivity, not objectivity. ⚠ The paper leans
     on **Karimi 2024** and **Tucker 2025** here; on reading those sources, Karimi 2024 actually
     *defends* relaxed realism's distinctness from quasi-realism (a functionalist reply to the
     collapse dilemma) and Tucker 2025 proposes a relaxed realism that drops moral properties — so the
     collapse is a **live debate, not a settled concession.** Port it as a worry/dispute, never as "it
     collapses." (This correction was applied in the live edit.)
  5. **Obligation needs a commander / a truthmaker with "ontological weight."** The load-bearing move:
     moral obligations are marked by *accountability, responsibility, expectation, prohibition, a
     binding character* — "things without ontological weight cannot lay claim over us and give us
     duties." A duty is *owed to someone*; a law-like obligation implies a lawgiver; this points to a
     necessarily-existent personal God. This rests on **C. Stephen Evans, *God and Moral Obligation*
     (OUP, 2013)** (social-relations/accountability) and Adams 2000.
  6. **God's aseity** (§6.4): God, as the *ground* of all moral obligation, has no moral obligations
     himself (no higher standard above him) — rebutting the "wouldn't God be responsible to himself?"
     objection. Cites Moreland & Craig 2003 and Swinburne 1993.
