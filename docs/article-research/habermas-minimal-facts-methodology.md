## Thesis (our words)
The minimal-facts method rests on **two prerequisites** an event must meet to make the list: (1) it is
established by **strong, multiply-attested historical evidence**, and (2) it is granted by the **vast
majority of scholars across the spectrum, sceptics included**. Habermas is explicit that **the first
criterion is "by far the most crucial"** — the evidence is what *establishes historicity*; the scholarly
consensus is secondary and "may be mistaken or it could change." He keeps **two lists**: a longer ~12-item
"Known Historical Facts" list, and the stricter **four-to-six "Minimal Facts"** extrapolated from it by
demanding near-unanimity. The essay then walks through Licona's *The Resurrection of Jesus* (2010) as the
fullest scholarly application, and closes with methodological caveats. **Scope: an apologetic *strategy* for
establishing historicity on the critics' own terms — Habermas insists it is NOT a verdict on Scripture's
reliability and must never be used to unsettle a believer's faith.**

## The primaries / claims to VERIFY (all page-precise to the STR 2012 text)

- **Two criteria; the FIRST is primary** — p. 16: "Of the two criteria, I have always held that the first
  is by far the most crucial… this initial requirement is the one that actually establishes the historicity
  of the event. Besides, the acclamation of scholarly opinion may be mistaken or it could change" (n. 5).
- **Two lists** — p. 16: the longer "Known Historical Facts" (~a dozen) vs. the stricter four-to-six
  "Minimal Facts"; the short list was briefly called "Core Facts" (n. 6). He is "even more selective than
  the majority of critical scholars" to get the sceptics on board (pp. 16–17).
- **The bibliographic survey** — p. 18: "presently at about 3400 sources and counting," French/German/
  English, tracking ~140 sub-issues; a private 600-page manuscript. **The self-selection defence:** he
  "erred in the direction of cataloguing the most radical positions," including non-credentialed skeptics
  (mythicists, naturalistic-theory proponents) whom mainline scholars "rarely even footnoted," so his count
  is **skewed toward the skeptical end, not the conservative** (pp. 18, n. 9).
- **The "~90th-percentile" self-report** — p. 17: for the most important facts he "frequently think[s] in
  terms of a ninety-something percentile head-count," while conceding the figures are "something between a
  rough guess and an estimate." ⚠ **Do not quote as a hard statistic.**
- **Licona's bedrock vs. second-order** — pp. 22–24: Licona counts only **three** as historical bedrock
  (crucifixion; the disciples' appearance-experiences; Paul), and treats **James, the empty tomb, Jesus'
  death/resurrection predictions, and the bodily nature of the appearances as "second-order facts"** used
  only "where no clear winner emerges." James is dropped from bedrock **not because it is doubted** but
  because "the number who comment on the material is small" (Licona p. 461).
- **The empty tomb is NOT a Minimal Fact** — pp. 25–26: "I have never counted the empty tomb as a Minimal
  Fact; it is very obvious that it does not enjoy the near-unanimity." Habermas & Licona 2004, 69–70:
  "The empty tomb… does not meet our two criteria… because it is not accepted by nearly every scholar."
  (Between two-thirds and three-quarters favour it — his own tally — with **23 reasons for / 14 against** in
  the literature, p. 22.)
- **The time factor as a *separate* minimal fact** — pp. 24–25: he stopped conflating the disciples'
  experiences with the *early date* of their proclamation and counts the earliness separately.
- **"This is an apologetic strategy"** — p. 26: it is "not a prescription for how a given text should be
  approached… or how a sermon is written… it should never be concluded that the use of such methods…
  indicate a lack of trust in Scripture… **Nor should it cause others to question or doubt their beliefs.**"

## Cross-check against live content (the six verdicts)

**Corroboration (verdict 1 — we already have it, accurately):** the two criteria; the ~140 sub-questions;
the "several thousand sources" survey; the four-to-six list; the inference-to-best-explanation engine;
the tier-discipline ("keep the tiers distinct"); the skeptic concessions bounded to *experiences*. All live
and correct in `library/minimalfacts.html` (which already cites this paper at fn 2–3).

**✅ Hazard — OURS IS BETTER (verdict 5 — do NOT "improve" backwards):**
- Our essay says "**several thousand sources**" rather than pinning "3,400 (2012)." The number grows
  yearly; our vaguer phrasing is deliberately staleness-proof. **Keep it.**
- Our essay says "**near-universal assent / great majority**," not a percentage. Habermas himself hedges the
  "ninety-something percentile" as "between a rough guess and an estimate." **Do NOT add a hard %.**

**⚠ Weaker in ours / worth strengthening (verdict 3) → P2 row:** our essay answers the Carrier/GCRR
**self-selection objection** ("the population may over-represent the religiously committed") only with
"the strongest facts don't need the headcount" + "the empty tomb is the soft point." It omits **Habermas's
own two-part defence**: (a) the **evidence criterion is primary** — the case never rested on the vote, and
Habermas grants the consensus "may be mistaken or could change"; and (b) his dataset is **skewed
skeptical, not conservative** (he over-counted non-credentialed radicals). Adding both — bounded — makes our
reply to the sharpest live methodological objection markedly stronger, in Habermas's own words. ⚠ Keep it
bounded: this does not refute the GCRR transparency point (the dataset is still unpublished/unauditable);
present as "Habermas replies…," not "the objection fails."

**⚠ Refinements (verdict 3/4) → P3 rows:**
- **Licona's strict 3-fact bedrock + "second-order facts."** Our essay keeps tiers distinct but doesn't note
  that the leading practitioner (Licona) restricts *bedrock* to three and relegates James **and** the empty
  tomb to "second-order" — James only for lack of comment, not for doubt. Adding it models the very
  restraint the essay praises and shows the tiering is internal to the scholars, not just our caution.
- **Habermas's own taxonomy excludes the empty tomb from the Minimal Facts.** Our essay lists the empty
  tomb among the "four-to-six" (following the 2004 popular book, which flags it). The 2012 methodology
  paper is sharper: the tomb is a "Known Fact," never a Minimal Fact. A one-clause tightening reinforces our
  "soft point in the chain" treatment and gets Habermas's taxonomy exactly right. (Note the genuine
  2004-popular vs. 2012-methodology tension; footnote both.)
- **The mission-aligned pastoral caveat (p. 26):** Habermas's own insistence that the method is a bounded
  *apologetic strategy*, "not a lack of trust in Scripture," and must **not "cause others to question or
  doubt their beliefs."** This maps exactly onto the site mission ("build the reader up, never nearer to
  doubt") and belongs in the essay's honest-scope close.

**Missing that we should NOT add (verdict 6 — non-recommendation):** the paper's long inside-baseball on
Licona's *sōma*/resurrection-body analysis vs. Gundry, and the five named naturalistic theorists Licona
weighs (Vermes, Goulder, Lüdemann, Crossan, Craffert) — these belong in `library/appearances.html` /
`library/emptytomb.html` if anywhere, not the methodology essay. Logged, not filled here.
