## Executable rows (all EXISTENCE-fenced; own words; verified primaries)
- **Row 1 (P2, HIGH) — the Pauline argument-from-silence.** Rebut the mythicist claim (Doherty) that Paul's near-silence on Jesus's earthly life ⇒ no earthly Jesus: the epistles are *occasional letters to insiders in a high-context culture* — earthly detail is presupposed, not narrated; a silence argument applied to the wrong genre. Primary in own words (Casey p.109, 134 as lead only); target "The earliest witness: Paul" (lines 150–154). ⚠ Steelman Doherty; keep to existence.
- **Row 2 (P3, MED) — evidence-free interpolation ("principle of convenience").** Mythicists excise inconvenient texts as "interpolations" with **zero manuscript support** (the disputed Pauline passages appear in *every* manuscript), and no trained classicist regards Tacitus/Suetonius/Pliny as Christian insertions; Ehrman's "passing strange" they didn't also excise the virgin birth/miracles. Lead: Ehrman *DJE* 133, 55, 118. Target: outside-witnesses section (lines 164–166). ⚠ Frame the target as the *selective, evidence-free* use — some interpolation claims (e.g. 1 Thess 2:14–16) are legitimate independent scholarship.
- **Row 3 (P3, MED-LOW) — siblings multiply attested.** Reinforce the anti-Carrier "brother" reply: the sibling relationship is independently attested (Ehrman counts four first-century sources) — add **Mark 3:21, 31–32** to the existing Mark 6:3 + Josephus + Galatians. Lead: Ehrman *DJE* 151. Target: line 186 (preserve Carrier's cultic-title reading at full strength).
- **Row 4 (P3, MED) — manuscript-based late-dating fallacy.** Mythicists (Murdock) push the Gospels to ~180 AD by dating them to their *surviving manuscripts* — a method no classicist uses (earliest full Thucydides copy is 10th-c. CE). The field (agnostic→conservative) dates them to the last third of the 1st c. Lead: Casey p.50 (Thucydides), 107. Target: Gospels section (156–158). ⚠ Do NOT adopt Casey's idiosyncratic *early* dates (Mark ~40) as our voice — anchor on mainstream "last third of the first century."
