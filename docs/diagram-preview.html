Three figure types. Every word below is ported verbatim from a certified essay or tab card — nothing is authored. Each figure names its source underneath itself.

1. The argument, as a chain

The card already prints these three lines as a numbered list. This is the same three lines, shown as the chain the reader is being asked to follow.

On the grey box at the bottom. That sentence is ours, not a critic’s — it is the essay bounding its own conclusion, and the site’s guardrails require it (the Kalam reaches a transcendent cause, not yet the God of the creed). Without it the picture would claim more than the paragraph beside it, which is how a reader gets ambushed the first time they meet a real objection.

2. The gap, shown instead of asserted

The most common objection the site answers is “written decades later.” Every date here is already in the essay. Drawn, the answer takes about a second.

3. What the critics themselves grant

The strongest affirmative evidence on the site, and the hardest to wave away. It quotes what a sceptic concedes — never what a sceptic argues, so there is no position here to misrepresent.
