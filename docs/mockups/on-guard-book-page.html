On Guard

Defending Your Faith with Reason and Precision

William Lane Craig · Foreword by Lee Strobel · David C. Cook, 2010

Level Beginner-friendly Length 10 chapters Covers God's existence → the resurrection Reading club 10 sessions

A step-by-step tour of the central arguments of Christian apologetics: why anything exists at all, why the universe began and why it is fine-tuned, whether we can be good without God, what to make of suffering, who Jesus was, and whether he rose from the dead. It is the accessible, popular-level companion to his scholarly work.

Four things to know as you read

Every book has places where scholarship has moved on or where Christians disagree. We say where, gently, so you can read with confidence.

The science is from 2010 Draft

The cosmology chapters reflect research up to about 2008. The core argument stands, but check newer work, and hold the science as strong corroboration, not proof.

Chapters 4–5 · Updated in our Kalam and Big Bang essays

Morality: the ground, not good behaviour

The claim is about the foundation of morality, never the bald "you can't be good without God." Atheists can live good lives and know right from wrong; the question is what grounds objective moral values and duties.

Chapter 6 · The Moral Argument

The "Son of Man" section Draft

Chapter 8 treats the figure in Daniel 7 as itself divine. We don't rest on that reading. The stronger case runs through the clouds, elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible God's own conveyance, and through what Jesus did with the scene. The chapter's other evidence stands.

Chapter 8 · The titles of Jesus

Where Christians disagree

On the fate of those who never hear, and the nature of final judgment, Craig defends particular positions of his own. Orthodox Christians hold a genuine range of views on these questions, and we don't settle in-house disputes.
