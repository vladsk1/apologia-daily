### Introduction — "Knowing Jesus as God"
**Framing / "so what":** people resist Jesus' deity because deity implies *exclusivity* — if Jesus is uniquely
God incarnate he is uniquely the way to God. The book draws this out of a hostile witness: **John Hick** (ed.,
*The Myth of God Incarnate*, 1977) concedes the entailment while rejecting the deity. Also engages the *Da Vinci
Code* "deity invented at Nicaea ~300 yrs later" myth (answered as conspiracy-not-history) and the Jesus Seminar.
→ *Lead (minor): the "deity ⇒ exclusivity, which is why it offends" angle is a fresh pastoral framing our deity
essays don't foreground. Hick is a citable hostile witness for the entailment.*

**Stated method (matters for how we cite the book):**
- A **comprehensive, cumulative** NT case — deliberately *not* proof-texting ("many were taught deity is proved
  by one or two verses… but there is much more"). **"The deity of Christ is a major theme throughout the New
  Testament"** — the anti-developmental (anti-Ehrman) thesis that high Christology is NT-wide, not just John.
- **Assumes** NT reliability + inspiration, but says skeptics interested in what the NT *says* still find help.
- Explicitly affirms, as givens: Jesus a real human, **died on the cross, rose bodily**, and **in the resurrection
  remains human, glorified/immortal** (Chalcedon-safe); and **Jesus is not God the Father** (anti-modalist).
  The NT *both* distinguishes Jesus from God *and* identifies him as God — "sometimes in the same breath."
- Trinity per se is **not** the book's topic (note 18), though Jesus' deity is a key part of it.

**The HANDS framework (defined here; matches `hands.html` fn 2):** Honors / Attributes / Names / Deeds / Seat =
"Jesus shares the {honors due to / attributes of / names of / deeds that God does / seat of God's throne}."
⭐ **Mnemonic origin our essay omits:** the acronym recalls **Thomas** seeing the nail-prints in Jesus' *hands*
(John 20:24–29) — "the hands of deity." Called "a tested and proven device," not a gimmick (note 19).

**⭐ "Same-breath" distinguish-yet-identify texts (Introduction):** **John 1:1; John 20:28–31; Heb 1:8–9;
2 Peter 1:1–2.** → *LEAD (verify + gate): `hands.html`'s NAMES section uses John 1:1 & 20:28 but omits
**Heb 1:8–9** ("Your throne, O God") and **2 Peter 1:1** ("our God and Savior Jesus Christ," a Granville-Sharp
construction). Both are strong, and the book has whole tables on them (Sharp's Rule; Heb 1). Candidate enrichment.*

**Citations seen (front matter/intro):** John Hick, ed., *The Myth of God Incarnate* (1977); Grant Osborne (on
recovering worship/awe of Jesus as God); *The Da Vinci Code*; notes 1–19 (to capture from the Notes section).

### Part 1 — HONORS ("The Devotion Revolution: Jesus Shares the Honors Due to God")

#### Ch. 1 — "All Glory, Laud, and Honor"
**Thesis:** the early *Jewish* Christians didn't just praise Jesus; they gave him the honors their own
Scriptures reserved for **YHWH alone** — and agreed those honors belonged to God alone, then gave them to
Jesus anyway. Honor in that culture was a limited good (to honor a creature as deity detracts from God's honor):
**Philo**, *"They who deify mortal things neglect the honour due to God"*; **Jerome Neyrey** on honor as a
limited good.

⭐⭐ **TOP LEAD (P2) — the early-devotion quotes we lack.** Against the "belief evolved over centuries / invented
at Nicaea" story, the book lands two field-standard soundbites, and **neither is in `hands.html`** (which cites
Hurtado but not these):
- **Larry Hurtado:** devotion to Jesus emerged as **"a veritable 'big bang'"** — explosively rapid, an established
  feature *within the first two decades*. *(note 2 — get exact locus; likely Hurtado, *Lord Jesus Christ*, or
  *At the Origins of Christian Worship*.)*
- **Martin Hengel** (Tübingen): more happened in beliefs about Jesus in the **~20 years between the cross and
  Paul's letters "than in the whole subsequent seven hundred years of church history."** *(note 3.)*
→ *These belong in `hands.html`'s Ehrman reply and/or `earlycreed.html` / `ev-s3`. Verify loci in the Notes; both
are widely cited and safe. Directly rebuts the developmental thesis our essay already engages.*

**Honor texts developed at length (vs. our compressed H section):**
- **John 5:23** ("honor the Son just as they honor the Father") — *we have this*; the book adds the frame
  *"linking the honor due God with the honor due anyone else… was **unprecedented** in the Jewish Scriptures"* and
  the vv.19–23 context (does whatever the Father does / gives life / all judgment entrusted). Corroboration-plus.
- **Hebrews 3:3–4** — Jesus is *"worthy of more glory than Moses, as the builder of a house has more honor than
  the house"*; Moses = part of creation ("house"), Jesus = the **builder**, and "the builder of all things is God."
  → *Lead (P3): a clean honor/deity argument NOT in `hands.html`.*
- ⭐ **The DOXOLOGY argument (strong, systematic — P2/P3).** Doxologies are stylized praise to **God** (1 Chron
  29:10–11; Ps 72:18–19; Rom 11:36; Gal 1:4–5; Phil 4:20). The NT gives **identical doxologies to Jesus**:
  Heb 13:20–21; **1 Pet 4:11** (glory "through Jesus Christ… to whom belongs the glory and dominion forever");
  **2 Pet 3:18** (glory to Christ *with no mention of the Father*); Rev 4:11 (God) ∥ **Rev 5:12–13** (the Lamb).
  The book's **table "Doxologies to God and the Lamb in Revelation"** maps the *sevenfold* honorifics —
  ἄξιος/worthy, εὐλογία, τιμή, δόξα, κράτος, δύναμις, ἰσχύς, πλοῦτος, σοφία, εὐχαριστία, σωτηρία — showing an almost
  complete overlap between "God/One on the Throne," "the Lamb," and **1 Chron 29:11–12**. **Bauckham:** we should not
  *"underestimate the boldness that was necessary to alter these traditional Jewish forms"* (note 12). **Matthias
  Hoffmann:** the predicates *"do not seem to distinguish God and the Lamb… but rather express an equal status"*
  (note 13; Hoffmann, *The Destroyer and the Lamb*, WUNT 2/203, 2005). Also note 11: the Lamb *"is appropriately
  worshipped on equal terms with God."*
  → *`hands.html`'s H currently rests on John 5:23 + Rev 5:13–14 + the angel-refuses-worship contrast. The doxology
  overlap (esp. the God∥Lamb∥1 Chron 29 mapping + Bauckham/Hoffmann) is a meaningful upgrade. Candidate: thicken H,
  or a short "worship in Revelation" sub-point. DEITY TIER ⇒ dual-consensus; verify Hoffmann/Bauckham loci.*

**Citations to verify (ch. 1):** Philo (deify mortal things) n.5–6; Jerome Neyrey; Hurtado "big bang" n.2; Hengel
n.3; Hoffmann n.13; Bauckham "boldness…" n.12. (Capture exact refs from the Notes section.)

#### Ch. 2 — "The Worship of the Carpenter" (the WORSHIP argument — core to `hands.html` H)
Frame: **R. T. France** — *"The basic fact which lies behind all the theological terms and titles is the worship
of the carpenter"* (n.1; the book's title source). Semantics: *shachah / proskuneō* = bow/prostrate; can be mere
respect to a superior, so **context decides** (immediate + whole-book).

**⭐ The Matthew-internal worship argument (P3 — NOT in `hands.html`):** Matthew first has Jesus establish that
**God alone** is to be worshiped — Satan offers the kingdoms if Jesus will "fall down and worship" (proskunēsēs)
him; Jesus refuses, quoting **Deut 6:13** ("Worship the Lord your God, and serve only him," Matt 4:10). *Yet* Jesus
is himself the object of proskuneō repeatedly in that same Gospel (2:2,11; 8:2; 9:18; 14:33; 15:25; 20:20; 28:9,17).
Two are clearly deity-grade (the only two where *edistasan*, "doubted," also occurs): **Matt 14:33** (after walking
on water: "Truly you are the Son of God") and **Matt 28:17–18** — the disciples worship, "some doubted," and Jesus
answers the doubt not by proving he's alive but by claiming **"all authority in heaven and on earth"** — i.e.
affirming the worship is appropriate. Neat inclusio: *what Jesus refused to take from the Devil (worship) he received
from the Father and from his disciples.* The Great Commission (28:19–20) makes the disciples' worship a **model**.

**⭐⭐ Hebrews 1:6 — "Let all God's angels worship him" (P2, strong, MISSING from `hands.html`).** Worship (a)
**commanded by God** ("God *told* them to worship Jesus"), (b) offered by **angels**, not humans — which defeats
the "bowing was just Near-Eastern court custom" rationalization, since **angels have no such custom** (n.10); and
(c) the directive is an **OT quotation originally about worship of YHWH himself** — **Ps 97:7 (96:7 LXX)** or, on
the majority view, **Deut 32:43** in the LXX / Odes 2:43 form (*kai proskunēsatōsan autō pantes angeloi theou*),
n.11–14. → *One of the cleanest worship-of-Jesus proofs, with a built-in answer to the agency/JW "just respect"
move. Pairs with Heb 1:8–9 (Names) — the whole of Hebrews 1 is under-used by us.*

**⭐ Jason BeDuhn engaged (P3 — connects to `ev-s6` JW card + `hands.html` agency reply).** BeDuhn (the NWT-defending
scholar our `ev-s6` already refutes) claims most translations "add… the false idea that the disciples are depicted
worshipping Jesus" at **Matt 14:33** — they "merely are reacting to his evident powers with awe" (n.6). The book
rebuts him (worship tied to the confession "Son of God" + the Satan-temptation frame + Matt 28). → *Useful ported
rebuttal for the JW/agency line; verify the BeDuhn quote + book locus.*

**Citations to verify (ch. 2):** R.T. France n.1; Deut 6:13/Matt 4:10 n.3; the Matthew proskuneō list n.4; BeDuhn
n.6; Matt 28:18 "worship appropriate" n.8; angels-no-custom n.10; Ps 97:7/Deut 32:43 LXX + Odes 2:43 nn.11–14.

**Add to ch. 2 (agency-reply reinforcements, P3):** the book strengthens the "no worship of agents/angels in
Judaism" ceiling with **Peter Carrell** (*Jesus and the Angels*, SNTSMS 95) — *"a monotheism which allows for Jesus
to be included with God as the object of worship and which envisages Jesus sharing the divine throne with God"* —
and **Loren Stuckenbruck** (*Angel Veneration and Christology*, WUNT 2/70) on how Jewish sources stop short of
actually *worshiping* angels; plus **Philo** (satraps/Great King analogy) against angel-worship. It also gives a
clean **table "Worship of Jesus vs. Worship of an Angel in Revelation"** (Christ in 1:12–20 *accepts* / the angel in
19–22 *refuses*; the parallel is deliberate). → *Our `hands.html` agency-reply cites Bauckham; Carrell + Stuckenbruck
are strong added witnesses that agency never conferred worship. The Rev-1-vs-Rev-19 table is a tighter version of the
angel-refuses contrast we already use.* Memorable line (n.17): *"to worship him, if he is not God, is idolatry; to
withhold worship from him, if he is, is apostasy."*

#### Ch. 3 — "What a Friend We Have in Jesus" (the PRAYER argument — ⭐ NEW to us, P2/P3)
Definition move: *a deity is an object of prayer.* Prayer belongs to God alone — only the transcendent/omniscient/
omnipotent God hears all prayers and is the Savior (Ps 65:2; Isa 45:20–22); angels are **not** prayed to (they are
"sent to render service," Heb 1:14). **Yet the NT has believers praying to Jesus from the very start:**
- **Acts 1:24–25** — the disciples pray ("Lord, you know everyone's heart") to choose Matthias; the "Lord" is Jesus
  (Luke's *kurios* usage; Peter just said "Lord Jesus" v.21; Jesus is the one who *chose* the apostles, 1:2; Lk 6:13;
  Acts 9:15). *proseuchomai* = technical prayer term (n.1).
- ⭐ **Stephen (Acts 7:59–60)** — *"Lord Jesus, receive my spirit"* (*epikaleō*, "call on," n.5). Mirrors Jesus' own
  *"Father, into your hands I commit my spirit"* (Luke 23:46 / Ps 31:5): **Jesus performs God's function of receiving
  the dying one's spirit.** **Jaroslav Pelikan:** for Stephen to commit his spirit to the Lord Jesus, when Jesus had
  committed his to the Father, *"was either an act of blatant idolatry or the acknowledgment of the kurios Iēsous as
  the fitting recipient of the dying prayer"* (n.6). → *strong hostile-adjacent historian witness.*
- ⭐ **"Call on the name of the Lord [Jesus]"** — Acts 9:14, 21; 22:16; **1 Cor 1:2** ("all… who call on the name of
  our Lord Jesus Christ," ~AD 54); **Rom 10:12–14** — which is a **standard OT formula for prayer/worship offered to
  YHWH** (Gen 4:26; Joel 2:32). *R. T. France:* this shows they thought of Jesus "not only as the proper recipient of
  prayer but also as God himself." → *Directly reinforces `hands.html`'s NAMES section, which already uses Rom 10:13/

#### Ch. 6 — "Beyond Resemblance" (method + Col 2:9)
**Method (rigor worth borrowing):** to prove Jesus has God's attributes we must show a description of God is applied to
Jesus **with the same meaning / same sense and extent**, not merely applied. Communicable vs **incommunicable**
attributes (eternity, omniscience, omnipotence — the ones a creature cannot share). "No one is like God" is an OT
refrain (Exod 8:10; 15:11; 1 Kings 8:23; Ps 86:8; Isa 40:18,25; 46:9; Jer 10:6–7) — yet the NT says Jesus is.
Citations: John Piper; Thomas C. Oden (definition of "attributes"). Chalcedon kept: Jesus also has **human**
attributes (tired/hungry/tempted; Heb 2:17 "made like his brothers in every way") — two sets of attributes, so the
human ones are no objection.

⭐ **Anti-modalist precision tool (P3) — attributes vs. PROPERTIES.** The book distinguishes God's shared
*attributes* from the persons' *properties* (the interior relations of the Godhead): "being the Son of the Father"
is a **property** of the Son, not the Father; "being the one who sent the Son" is the Father's, not the Son's —
**"the Son did not send himself."** So "Jesus has all the attributes of God" does **not** mean he has the Father's
person-properties. → *A clean clarifier candidate for `nt_trinity.html` / `modalism.html` / `hands.html` — it heads
off the modalist misread at the exact point our clarifiers exist for.*

⭐ **Colossians 2:9 (P2/P3 — MISSING from `hands.html`'s A).** "In him the whole fullness of **deity** (*theotēs*)
dwells **bodily**" — one of the most emphatic single deity texts, paired with **Col 1:19** ("all the fullness was
pleased to dwell in him"). The book shows Col 1:19 echoes the LXX of **Ps 68:16 (67:16 LXX)** — the language of God
*dwelling* on Mount Zion — but goes further: even the temple was not God's true dwelling (heaven was; 1 Kings 8:27),
whereas *all* the fullness of Godhood permanently resides bodily in Christ (Murray Harris). *theotēs* = full deity,
not "a god." → *Strong A-section enrichment; verify Col 2:9 isn't already carried better on `jesus_as_god_nt.html`/
`titles.html` (likely somewhere — check before treating as net-new). Deity tier ⇒ dual-consensus.*

#### Ch. 11 — "Name One" (Names framework + the name "Jesus")
Method: "name" covers **titles** too; **context** (not capitalization — which post-dates the mss) decides whether a
divine name = deity. Three "contextual factors" make the names weight-bearing: an array of divine names, often
together (John 20:28; Titus 2:13; 2 Pet 1:1); in OT-quotation/allusion contexts about God; alongside the divine
honors/attributes/deeds/seat. ⚠ Lists **Isa 9:6** ("Mighty God, **Everlasting Father**") as a messianic "name" —
usable but "Everlasting Father" needs a modalism fence (it is a title, not identifying Jesus as God *the Father*).
⭐ **New strand — the name "Jesus" (P3):** "Jesus" = "YHWH saves"; the NT lavishes on the *name of Jesus* the
reverence the OT reserved for **Yahweh** — exalted "above every name" (Phil 2:9–11; Eph 1:21); miracles/salvation

#### Ch. 12 — "Immanuel: God with Us" (the "God" name — deep dive; ⭐ gate-safe, anti-modalist, Colwell-FREE)
**OT precedents:** Isa 7:14 Immanuel ("God with us"; the *almah/parthenos* handling); Isa 9:6 "Mighty God";
⭐ **Isa 40:3** ("prepare the way of *the LORD*" applied by the Synoptics to preparing the way for **Jesus** — clean

**John's three "God" texts (1:1, 1:18, 20:28 — preincarnate / incarnate / postresurrection; Murray Harris):**

**Ch. 12 completion — Heb 1:8 anti-modalist handling + Granville Sharp (both ⭐ high-value for `hands.html` N):**

  gate-safe upgrade to `hands.html`'s Titus 2:13 line (add 2 Pet 1:1). ⚠ Present WITH the restriction (like Colwell,

  overstate. Murray Harris (Jesus as God) is the citation.* (Watch for 1 John 5:20 as a further, more disputed text.)
