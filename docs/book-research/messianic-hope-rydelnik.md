## THESIS & METHOD (chs. 1–2)

**Spine.** The Hebrew Bible is **directly, textually messianic** — key texts are messianic *predictions in
their own compositional/intended meaning*, not merely figures later re-applied to Jesus. Rydelnik argues a
"growing evangelical movement away from reading the Hebrew Bible as a messianic book" (naming **Tremper
Longman III**, **Klyne Snodgrass** — "the conviction about his identity did NOT derive from the OT" —
**Larry Hurtado**, **John Walton**, **Daniel Block**, **Paul Wegner**, **Gordon McConville**) concedes the
skeptic's ground. His positive method is **John Sailhamer's compositional/canonical** reading: the MT is a
late consolidation (text criticism first), then a close reading of the final-form text + its innerbiblical
echoes yields "far more direct messianic prediction than is commonly held." ⭐ **Money quote (thesis
stakes), Walter Kaiser:** the interpretation of the Messiah in the OT "could be a defining moment for
evangelical scholarship." ⚠ **Fence #2 lives in the thesis itself** — our essays sit near the views he
opposes (Snodgrass "conviction came first"; Chris Wright "promise not prediction"; dual-fulfillment).

**The 7-model taxonomy (ch2, "Summary of Positions").** A genuinely useful map of where every view sits:
1. **Historical** (Anthony Collins; critics) — all prophecy refers to the prophets' own day; Christianity is history's *outgrowth*, not prophecy's *fulfillment*.
2. **Dual Fulfillment** (Thomas Sherlock; = *sensus plenior*) — immediate historical + ultimate messianic; divine author adds meaning. **← where our Isa 7:14 sits.**
3. **Typical/Typological** (Aage Bentzen) — a historical person is a *type* of Messiah. **← where our `typology.html` sits.**
4. **Progressive/Epigenetic** (Beecher, Kaiser) — one author-intended meaning in seed form, growing.
5. **Relecture** (Clements, Wegner) — literal = prophet's day; later re-read to "fill up" with messianic sense.
6. **Midrash/Pesher** (Longenecker, Juel) — NT uses inspired creative exegesis moderns can't reproduce.
7. **Direct** (Hengstenberg [dogmatic]; **Sailhamer/Horbury [compositional-canonical]**) — prophecy refers directly to Messiah. **← Rydelnik's own, via Sailhamer.**

## CH 3 — Text-Critical Perspectives (messianic readings in the critical apparatus)

**Thesis:** the MT is a sound but *late, post-Christian rabbinic consolidation* (consonants fixed 2nd c. AD;
vowels/accents 9th–10th c. AD), so messianic readings may sit in the LXX / Samaritan Pentateuch / DSS / the
"Three" (Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion) rather than the MT. Framework: Sailhamer; **Emanuel Tov,
*Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible* (1992)**; Ernst Würthwein, *The Text of the OT* (1979). ⚠ Rydelnik
is explicit (note 84, from Culver, *BETS* 7 [1964]: 91–97) that he recognizes **FOUR** fulfillment
categories — direct, typical, applicational, summary — and affirms typology as legitimate (relevant to
fence #2).

**Worked textual examples (each an individually-weighed lead; see fence #4):**
- **Judges 18:30** — the suspended *nun* (Moses→Manasseh) — rabbinic theology in the MT (not messianic).
- **Numbers 24:7** — MT "higher than *Agag*" (→ Saul/David) vs "higher than *Gog*" (LXX, Sam. Pent., the Three → eschatological Messiah vs Ezek 38); W.F. Albright preferred "Gog"; T.R. Ashley (*Numbers* NICOT, 491) keeps MT but calls it "possibly corrupt." (ch10 preview.)
- **2 Samuel 23:1** — MT *al* "on high" (→ David exalted) vs reading *al* "concerning" (LXX → "raised up *concerning* the Messiah"). ⚠ variant-preference.
- **Psalm 72:5** — MT "they will fear you" vs LXX "he will endure" (→ the king's reign forever); Rydelnik prefers LXX. ⚠ contested (Knut Heim argues against, in *The Lord's Anointed*).
- ⭐ **Isaiah 9:5[6] "Mighty God" (*El Gibbor*)** — the accentuation yields the Davidic child's four divine titles (cf. Isa 10:21). **Franz Delitzsch** rejects a rival re-division (that would assign the first titles to *God* and only the last to the child, negating the child's deity) as "resting upon a misunderstanding," an MT bias "that a born child could not be the 'Mighty God.'" ⭐ **DEITY lead** (dual-consensus; present as Delitzsch's argument, contested text-critically). Lead: Delitzsch, *Isaiah*, 249–50.
- ⭐⭐ **Psalm 22:17[16] "pierced"** — MT *kaari* "like a lion" vs LXX *ōryxan* (rendering Heb *kaaru* "they pierced"); LXX + Syriac + Vulgate. **Decisive external support: the Nahal Hever Psalms fragment (5/6HevPs, c. AD 50–68) reads the verb "they pierced."** Peter Craigie (*Psalms 1–50* WBC, 196): the MT "can scarcely be correct." Strong **mainstream, non-apologetic** leads: **C.R. Gren, *JETS* 48 (2005): 283–99**; **VanderKam & Flint, *The Meaning of the DSS* (2002), 125**; **Abegg/Flint/Ulrich, *The DSS Bible* (1999), 519**; Flint, DJD XXXVIII (2000). Even the NET Bible concedes the MT is "grammatically awkward." → **Already fully on our site; see cross-check (ours is MORE balanced).**

## CH 4 — Innerbiblical Perspectives (⭐ the book's strongest, lowest-hazard method)

**Thesis:** later OT writers read earlier texts messianically *before* the NT did — rests on observable
intertextual verbal echoes, not on variant-picking. Three Torah test passages:
- **Genesis 49:8–12 "Shiloh"** — best read *shelloh* "to whom it belongs" (LXX "the things stored up for him"; Targum Onqelos; Aquila/Symm/Theod; Talmud *Sanhedrin* 98b; Qumran 4Q252). ⭐ **Clinched innerbiblically by Ezekiel 21:27[32] "until he comes whose right it is"** (Ralph Alexander: a definite reference to Gen 49:10).
- **Numbers 24:14–19** (star + scepter, "in the last days," "not now… not near") — ⭐ **clinched by Amos 9:11–12** (8th c.), which Sailhamer/Kaiser read as "a transparent intertextual reference to Num 24:18"; James then cites Amos 9:11–12 at **Acts 15:16–17**.
- **Deuteronomy 18:15–19** (prophet like Moses) — read with **Num 12:6–8** (Moses' uniqueness) + **Deut 34:10–12** (a final-redaction appendix: "no prophet has arisen like Moses" → points to a still-future eschatological prophet; Acts 3:22; 7:37).

⚠ Fence #2: Rydelnik presses the *direct* over the *collective-messianic* reading; we can use the
**observable intertextual links** (Ezek echoes Gen 49:10; Amos echoes Num 24; Deut 34 reads Deut 18 as
still-future) without ruling direct-vs-typological.

## CH 5 — Canonical Perspectives (Sailhamer's "canonical seams")

The Tanak's final redactional *shape* is messianic at the "seams" (Deut 34 / Josh 1; Mal 4 / Ps 1); a
messianic hope is an "earmark of canonicity." Supports: James Hamilton; **Rabbi Johanan, b. Berakhot 34b**
("Every prophet prophesied only of the days of the Messiah"); Acts 3:24. The "seed" (*zeraʿ*) trajectory
is traced through the Torah (Gen 3:15 → 22:17 → 49:8–12 → Num 24:7–9). ⚠ **Sailhamer's distinctive literary
thesis, NOT a consensus** — present as "Sailhamer argues"; limited cross-check value (do NOT conflate with
`canon.html`, which is about *which books* are canonical and is already flagged "ours honours denominational
neutrality better than its source").

## CH 6 — New Testament Perspectives (did the prophets *know*?)

Jesus took the whole OT to be about Him (Luke 24:25–27, 44–46; "Moses… wrote about Me," John 5:46). Two
load-bearing passages: **Acts 2:29–31** (David "being a prophet" wrote Ps 16 of the Messiah's resurrection)
and **1 Peter 1:10–12** (Rydelnik via Kaiser + Grudem: the passage does *not* teach the prophets were
ignorant that they wrote of Messiah — they searched the *time/identity*, not the *meaning*). ⭐ **Fig 6.1 —
the one clean, low-hazard framing device:** *Prophets → Messiah (MEANING); Apostles → Jesus (REFERENT).* ⚠
Fence #2: the "the prophets consciously knew" claim is the contested direct-prediction view; the 1 Pet
1:10–12 Kaiser/Grudem reading is usable as "Kaiser argues," never to overturn our `prophecy.html` posture
(Snodgrass "conviction came first").

## CH 7 — ⭐⭐ How the NT Reads the OT: the Matthew 2 four-category framework

**The direct answer to "the NT rips the OT out of context / uses arbitrary midrash."** Moisés Silva vs
Longenecker: the NT's exegesis is **discernible and reproducible**. Demonstrated via the four OT quotations
in Matthew 2, each a different *category* and a different place:
1. **DIRECT — Matt 2:5–6 / Micah 5:2** (Bethlehem). ⭐ "His origin… from days of eternity" (*miqqedem, mimei olam*) = eternity past → the ruler is **human and divine** (Kaiser; Blomberg). DEITY lead.
2. **TYPICAL — Matt 2:15 / Hosea 11:1** (Egypt). Hosea plainly speaks of Israel's exodus → **typology, not a false prediction claim**. The type is grounded in the Torah itself (Fig 7.1, the Balaam Oracles pattern: Israel [Num 23] ‖ the future King [Num 24]). → **Our `typology.html` covers this superbly; corroboration.**
3. **APPLICATIONAL — Matt 2:16–18 / Jeremiah 31:15** (Ramah). Jer 31:15 describes the exile inside the *new-covenant* chapter; Matthew *applies* its language, not claims it predicted Herod. ⭐ **Rebuts the "midrash" charge: David Instone-Brewer (*Techniques and Assumptions in Jewish Exegesis before 70 CE*) + Klyne Snodgrass — pre-AD-70 Jewish exegesis read Scripture IN CONTEXT; the atomistic style is *later*.** → **NOT on our site; improvement lead.**
4. **SUMMARY — Matt 2:23** (Nazarene). No single verse says it; Matthew cites "the prophets" (plural) — "Nazarene" = a term of *derision* encapsulating the prophets' Messiah-despised theme (Pss 22 & 69; Isa 53:2–3). OT precedent for a no-single-verse summary: Ezra 9:10–12. → **NOT on our site; improvement lead.**

S. Lewis Johnson: the apostles' *method* (not infallibility) is reproducible. ⚠ Low hazard — Rydelnik
*concedes* Hosea 11:1 and Jer 31:15 were not predictions, which is our honest posture.

## CH 8 — ⚠⚠⚠ Rashi's Influence (THE most neutrality-sensitive chapter)

**Thesis:** Rashi (1040–1105), amid the disputations and the 1096 Crusade, redefined *peshat* to mean the
*historical* sense specifically, as a polemical tool against Christological readings; this entered Christian
interpretation via the Victorines → **Nicholas de Lyra** ("Rashi's Ape") → **Luther** → the Reformation
Hebraists, producing today's dual/typical readings. Well-sourced (E.I.J. Rosenthal, *Cambridge History of
the Bible*; Herman Hailperin, *Rashi and the Christian Scholars*, 1963; Beryl Smalley).
- ✅ **The ONE mineable, low-hazard point:** the **ancient Jewish messianic readings** (Targums, Talmud,
  Midrash) of Gen 3:15, Ps 2, Isa 9:6, Isa 53, Zech — showing the messianic reading is **not a Christian
  imposition** but has ancient Jewish roots. Charitable, strong apologetic data.
- 🔴 **THE DISQUALIFYING HAZARD (fence #3) — DO NOT PORT:** Rydelnik's framing that Jewish interpreters
  read these texts non-messianically "to combat Christianity," and that evangelicals who do so are using
  "anti-Christian Jewish polemic." This impugns good faith and disparages our *own* (historical/typological)
  method. **Net verdict for most of ch8: non-recommendation / hazard.** Mine ONLY the ancient-reading data.

## CH 9 — ⭐⭐ Genesis 3:15 (case study, from the Law)

Rydelnik defends a messianic reading of the *protoevangelium* — but his case is **innerbiblical/trajectory,
not a bare standalone prediction**, and he explicitly **rejects two common apologetic overclaims**:
- 🔴🔴 **CORRECTION LEAD (checked — turned out to be a NON-ISSUE, see cross-check): Gen 3:15 does NOT predict
  the virgin birth.** Rydelnik states it flatly ("While *not* predicting the virgin birth of Messiah…");
  note 19: the "seed of the woman → virgin birth" argument fails because "Ishmael and his descendants are
  called Hagar's seed (Gen 16:10)." **Even this maximalist rejects the "seed of the woman, not the man"
  virgin-birth argument.** → We checked `virginbirth.html`: **we never make that error** (ours-is-better).
- ✅ **The careful case (low-hazard):** serpent is more than a snake (Rom 16:20; Rev 12:9; even J. Skinner
  ICC grants a demonic background); *zeraʿ* oscillates collective→individual, and 3:15's singular pronoun
  *huʾ* + singular verb → a particular future individual (Jack Collins' test); two comparable death-blows →
  victory *through his own death* (**Heb 2:14–15**).
- ⭐ **Port-worthy honest concession (= our exact posture): T. Desmond Alexander** — "Viewed solely within
  the context of ch. 3, it is virtually impossible to sustain a messianic interpretation of 3:15. Considered
  … in the light of Genesis as a whole, a messianic reading … is not only possible but highly probable."
- Primary leads: **R.A. Martin, *JBL* 84 (1965): 425–27** (LXX read it messianically — masculine *autos*
  with neuter *sperma*); **J.M. Hamilton, *SBJT* 10 (2006): 30–54**; T.D. Alexander in *The Lord's Anointed*.

## CH 10 — ⭐⭐ Isaiah 7:14 (case study, from the Prophets) — THE virgin-birth text

Three views (p.163): direct prediction (Rydelnik), historical-only, dual/multiple fulfillment (⭐ **note 29
names Blomberg's "double fulfillment" — the view OUR essays hold** — which Rydelnik rebuts). His distinctive
move: **two prophecies** — a long-term one to the house of David (7:13–15, the virgin-born Immanuel; address
shifts to plural "you," "Listen, house of David!") and a short-term one to Ahaz (7:16–25, Shear-Jashub).
- ⭐⭐ **THE *ALMAH* WORD-STUDY (the lowest-hazard payload):** *almah* has the article ("*the* almah," a
  specific woman in a vision); the controversy began when **Aquila (2nd c.)** replaced the LXX's *parthenos*
  "virgin" with *neanis* — so the **pre-Christian LXX read *parthenos***. Survey of every HB occurrence
  (Gen 24:43 Rebekah [paralleled with *betulah*]; Exod 2:8 Miriam; Ps 68:25; Song 6:8; Prov 30:19): *almah*
  is **never clearly a non-virgin**. ⚠ **betulah nuance (Wenham VT 1972):** *betulah* is **not** the
  unambiguous "virgin" word either (it can need a qualifier, even denote a widow, Joel 1:8), so *almah*
  expresses virginity at least as reliably — **a point our essay's "betulah is the more precise word" line
  does not carry (improvement lead).** ⚠ fence #3: Rydelnik's "except for an antisupernatural or
  antimessianic bias" closing line — do NOT port the motive-attribution.
- **Deity reading:** *harah* "(is) pregnant"; **Immanuel = "God with us"** → the child's divine nature
  (Isa 8:8; 9:6 Mighty God/Eternal Father); "the Messiah would be God in the flesh." Apologetic payoff: the
  virgin birth confirms Jesus as the signed Son of David AND is bound to His deity (Luke 1:35) and the
  atonement.
- ⭐ **Innerbiblical confirmation (not only Matt 1:23):** **Isa 9:6–7** + **Isa 11:1–10** (Robert Culver
  concession, note 31) + ⭐ **Micah 5:2–5** ("when she who is in labor has given birth" long read as an
  intertextual allusion to Isa 7:13–15; even N. Snaith, who denies the messianic reading, grants Mic 5
  cites Isaiah).
- Primary leads: **G.J. Wenham, "Betûlâ: A Girl of Marriageable Age," *VT* 22 (1972): 326–48**; **R.
  Niessen, *BSac* 546 (1980): 133–50**; C.H. Gordon, *JBR* 21 (1953): 106 (Ugaritic); J.A. Motyer,
  *The Prophecy of Isaiah* (1993), 84–86; B.B. Warfield, "The Divine Messiah in the OT" (1921). For the
  honest steelman: J.H. Walton, *JETS* 30 (1987): 289–306; Blomberg in Beale & Carson (2007).

## CH 11 — ⭐⭐ Psalm 110 (case study, from the Writings) — the divine Priest-King

Psalm 110 = the most-quoted OT text in the NT; Rydelnik defends it as a direct prediction of a divine
Priest-King. Structure (Davis, *BSac* 157 [2000]): 110:1–3 Divine King · 110:4 eternal Priest · 110:5–7
victorious Warrior; the thematic focal point of Pss 107–113.
- **Davidic authorship** carried by Jesus (Matt 22:41–46; Mark 12:35–37) — Delitzsch: without it Jesus'
  argument "would lack cogency." (Steelman: Merrill [David of himself]; Bateman [Solomon].)
- ⭐ **DEITY-from-the-Hebrew lead:** 110:1b "my lord" (*adoni*) used of the angel of the Lord (Josh 5:14;
  Judg 6:13); **Robert Alden** — the psalm was consonantal, vowels added 8th–10th c. AD, so "do not base
  interpretation on a single vowel"; 110:5 *adonay* (a Divine Master); Ps 45:6 "Your throne, God."
- ⭐ **Melchizedek eternal Priest-King (110:4 → Hebrews 7):** unites priest + king, non-Levitical, "forever"
  (Kidner: "the most significant clause of all").
- **Innertextual (Ps 110 ↔ Ps 2):** dominion, righteous wrath, "I have begotten you" (Ps 2:7 + the Ps 110:3
  variant *yelidtika*; Heb 1:5; 5:5). ⚠ 110:3 "begotten" rests on the **variant-preference** (fence #4) —
  the deity case does NOT need it.
- **Intertextual:** **Zechariah 6:9–15** (Branch/priest-king → Ps 110:4); **Zechariah 14**.
  ⚠⚠ **DANIEL 7 TRIPWIRE:** Rydelnik ties Ps 110:1 to Dan 7:9–14 (Akiba's two-thrones; the Son of Man's
  "everlasting dominion"). **Our site RETIRED `daniel7-figure-is-divine` / `pelach-only-to-God` (it took
  `library/titles.html` five gate rounds to remove).** This is the **THIRD** book-research note to carry the
  Dan-7-as-divine-figure material (after IDHEF/Geisler-Turek and Strobel). **DO NOT port the Dan-7 deity
