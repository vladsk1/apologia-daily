## Thesis / spine
`CONJ` walks nine "encounters" — nine titles the Gospels give Jesus — as a cumulative portrait of **one
first‑century Jew who claimed to be the maker of all things, the King of the Jews, the mighty healer, the
greatest teacher, the ultimate lover, the suffering servant, the perfect sacrifice, and the universal Lord.**
The method (stated in the Introduction via a *Hamilton* analogy) is that, *unlike* a musical, the Gospel
authors claim to **report** Jesus's actual words and deeds. The book's recurring apologetic through-line is
**divine identity and status**: title after title, read in first‑century Jewish context, places Jesus on the
Creator's side of the Creator/creature line — and the cross/resurrection are where that claim is vindicated.
It is a sequel to her *Confronting Christianity* (Crossway, 2019).

  names the sources individually, steelmans Carrier/Price/Wells).
- *Reliability* — Bauckham's named‑eyewitness argument ("I got this from Mary Magdalene"; *Jesus and the
  Eyewitnesses* 39–66, 308–9); the memory point (Gospels written as eyewitnesses were dying out). → **corroboration

- *Canon* — Nicaea did **not** select the Gospels (she says so explicitly, fn 16); *Gospel of Thomas* late/2nd‑c.;
  Ehrman, *Truth and Fiction in The Da Vinci Code* (OUP, 2004), 102. → **corroboration of `canon.html`** / the IDB note (Bock).

- *Nazi de‑Judaized Bibles* — **Susannah Heschel, *The Aryan Jesus*** (Princeton, 2010), 106–10 → anti‑Semitism is
