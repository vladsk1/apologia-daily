## Thesis and method (our words, from the publisher/review summaries)

*On the Resurrection* is Habermas's full-length, career-culminating deployment of the **minimal-facts
method** he pioneered from the mid-1970s. The method's engine is the same one our essay already describes:
isolate a short set of events around Jesus's death and its aftermath that are (1) **strongly, multiply
evidenced** and (2) **granted by the great majority of qualified scholars across the spectrum, sceptics
included**, and then ask what best explains that agreed data. Reviews describe the four volumes as a
division of labour:

- **Vol. 1, *Evidences*** — the positive case. Reported to be organised in several parts: a methodological
  foundation (historiography, the postmodern challenge to knowing the past, the fact-vs-interpretation
  distinction), then the core argument built on the minimal facts (reported as Jesus's crucifixion; the
  disciples' resurrection experiences; their transformation; Paul; James), with the **empty tomb** carried
  as a well-evidenced but **less universally granted** further fact. This is the counterpart to
  `library/minimalfacts.html`.
- **Vol. 2, *Refutations*** — the defensive case: the point-by-point rebuttal of the naturalistic
  alternatives (hallucination/vision theories, swoon/apparent-death, legend/myth, theft, cognitive-
  dissonance, etc.). Counterpart to `library/appearances.html`, `library/emptytomb.html`, and the "swoon"
  material now in `library/minimalfacts.html`.
- **Vol. 3, *Scholarly Perspectives*** — ⭐ **the volume of most interest to us.** Reported to publish
  Habermas's lifetime **survey/bibliography of the critical literature** — the running catalogue he began in
  1975 (reported at *over 3,500 sources*, of which *2,400+* are journal articles), and his position-by-
  position tally of where scholars land on each resurrection sub-question. This is the dataset behind the
  famous "roughly three-quarters of scholars grant the empty tomb" figure (search summaries repeat the
  **~75%** number). **See the cross-check — this is the one place the book plausibly *updates* our essay.**
- **Vol. 4, *Theology and Practice*** — the theological/pastoral payoff: the resurrection's bearing on
  theism, the deity of Christ, and miracles, plus applications (evangelism, suffering, doubt, the fear of
  death). Announced ~Aug 2026; possibly not yet in print. Counterpart topics touch `library/titles.html`,
  `library/miracles.html`, and the pastoral-care material.

**Scope note (carry into any pull):** exactly as our essay already frames it, this is an **apologetic
strategy for establishing historicity on the critics' own terms**, not a verdict on Scripture's
reliability. The guardrails are unchanged: lead the positive case with the **1 Cor 15:3–7 early creed**;
never inflate "the great majority" into "virtually all"; keep the **skeptic concessions bounded to the
experiences/appearances**, never to "the resurrection happened"; the **empty tomb stays the softer,
more-contested fact**; steelman **Allison** as *undecided on the cause*, never "refuted."
