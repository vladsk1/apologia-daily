## Thesis (our words)
A deliberately short, popular-level cumulative case that the four Gospels are trustworthy historical
sources, built not from one knockdown argument but from a **battery of small, independently checkable
tests of local knowledge** — the kind of thing a distant or late author could not have faked and would
have had no motive to fake. The book's centre of gravity is ch. 3's "did the authors know their stuff?"
material: geography, hydrology, roads, gardens, personal names, Jewish custom, botany, finance, and
language. Written substantially as a response to the popular reach of Bart Ehrman.

## Chapter map (reported structure → the primaries to VERIFY)
1. **What do Non-Christian Sources Say?** — **Tacitus** (~pp. 18–24), **Pliny the Younger** (~24–31),
   **Josephus** (~31–35); how fast the movement spread, and how early outside observers registered
   Christian belief in the deity of Christ (~28–31). → *Primaries:* **Tacitus,** *Annals* 15.44;
   **Pliny,** *Ep.* 10.96–97 (the *carmen Christo quasi deo* line); **Josephus,** *Ant.* 18.63–64 and
   20.200. ⚠ On the *Testimonium Flavianum*, follow the existing house rule: **neutral core only, never
   the full interpolated text** (see `book-research/in-defense-of-the-bible.md` ch. 10 flag and
   `book-research/josephus-and-jesus.md`).
2. **What Are the Four Gospels?** — dating, interdependence, traditional authorship. The Matthew-the-
   tax-collector argument from that Gospel's distinctive interest in money (~82–83). Cites **Brant Pitre,
   *The Case for Jesus*** (~p. 43 n. 9) against the "the Gospels were all originally anonymous" claim.
   ⚠ The anonymity question is genuinely contested — Pitre's manuscript-title argument has serious critics;
   do not present it as settled.
3. **⭐ Did the Gospel Authors Know Their Stuff?** — the heart of the book. Charts on **geography**
   (~52–57), **hydrology** (~57–58), **roads** (~58–61), **gardens** (~61–62); **personal names**
   (~64–78), built on **Bauckham** and **Tal Ilan** (see n. 28 at p. 64); **Jewish customs** (~78–81);
   **botany** (~81–82); **finance** (~82–83); languages.
   ⭐ **The distinctive observation worth chasing (net-new for us):** Matthew, Mark and John call Galilee
   a **sea**; **Luke calls it "the lake"** — and uses *sea* only three times, never of a specific body of
   water. On the traditional identification of Luke with Antioch on the Orontes, a man who knew the
   Mediterranean would not call the small Sea of Galilee *the sea*. → *Primaries to verify directly:* the
   Greek of **Luke 5:1–2; 8:22–23, 33** (*limnē*) against **Matt 4:18; Mark 1:16; John 6:1** (*thalassa*).
   This is checkable in a concordance and does not depend on Williams.
   → *Other primaries:* **Tal Ilan,** *Lexicon of Jewish Names in Late Antiquity, Part I: Palestine 330
   BCE–200 CE* (Mohr Siebeck, 2002); **Bauckham,** *Jesus and the Eyewitnesses*, chs. 3–4.
4. **Undesigned Coincidences** — follows **Lydia McGrew's** development of **J. J. Blunt**. Reported
   cases: Mary and Martha's personalities (~88–91); the feeding of the 5,000 and the significance of the
   **green grass** (~91–94); the Gospels and **Josephus on Herod Antipas** (~94–96). → *Primaries:*
   **Luke 10:38–42** with **John 11–12**; **Mark 6:39** with **John 6:4**; **Josephus,** *Ant.* 18.116–119.
   ⚠ **Blunt is public domain** (*Undesigned Coincidences in the Writings both of the Old and New
   Testament*, 1847) and is already legally hosted at BiblicalStudies.org.uk — a genuine `/sources`
   candidate if a citations pass verifies the text.
5. **Do We Have Jesus's Actual Words?** — ancient vs. modern standards of quotation; the languages Jesus
   used (~106–109). Reported argument: **language contact** means a Jew speaking Greek to a Jewish
   audience could naturally use Aramaic words, and many Greek words had by then been loaned *into*
   Aramaic. → *Primaries:* **Matt 5:22** (*raka*), **6:24** (*mamōna*), **Luke 15:25** (*symphōnia*);
   plausible Greek-speaking settings at **John 12:20–23; Matt 8:5–13; Mark 7:26; Mark 12:13**.
6. **Has the Text Changed?** — Williams's own field. The rapid, wide geographic spread makes a
   successful doctrine-changing interpolation implausible (~120–122). → **Cross-check
   [`article-research/lanier-critical-editions-stability.md`](../article-research/lanier-critical-editions-stability.md)**,
   which quantifies exactly this, and `library/manuscript.html`, which already makes the branching-vs-
   telephone argument. ⚠ **Preservation, not truth** — the standing rule.
7. **What about Contradictions?** — brief; focuses on *formal* contradictions as deliberate literary
   devices meant to make readers read deeply (~p. 127). ⚠ **Handle with care.** This does *not* answer the
   substantive harmonisation questions; for those the better map is
   `book-research/in-defense-of-the-bible.md` **ch. 11 (Huffman)**, whose three cautionary principles and
   humility about unresolved cases are the model. Never let "the tension is deliberate" become a
   universal solvent.
8. **Who Would Make All This Up?** — the cumulative close: the simplest explanation of the Gospels as we
   have them is that they are what they claim to be.

## Guardrail fit
- **Cumulative-case honesty.** This is a *convergence* argument; no single test carries it. Presenting one
  chart (names, or the *limnē*/*thalassa* point) as decisive would overstate what Williams himself claims.
- **Local knowledge ≠ truth of the content.** The tests show the authors knew first-century Palestine —
  the same bound as the archaeology and manuscript arguments. They do not establish that a miracle
  occurred. `library/names.html` already states this correctly and even carries the **critics**
  (Gregor & Blais; Tuckett) on the page. Keep that standard.
- **Popular-level compression is the risk.** 153 pages covering this much ground means the objections get
  short treatment. Our essays must not inherit that compression — where Williams has a paragraph, we owe
  a steelman.
- Denominational neutrality: clean. Orthodoxy: nothing at stake beyond the ordinary Gospel-reliability
  frame.
