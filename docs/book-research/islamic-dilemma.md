## Thesis
**The "Islamic Dilemma"** is an **abductive, internal (immanent) critique** of Islam built entirely from the
Qur'an as primary witness. Its two premises:

- **P1.** The Qur'an affirms the **inspiration, authority, and (in the Qur'an's own telling) preservation** of
  the **Tawrat** (Torah) and **Injil** (Gospel) *as they existed in the possession of 7th-century Jews and
  Christians* — i.e. the actual biblical texts.
- **P2.** The Qur'an makes theological/historical claims that **fundamentally contradict** those very
  Torah/Gospel it claims to confirm.

**Conclusion:** the author of the Qur'an established **no genuine continuity** with the Torah and Gospel, so
Muhammad cannot be a prophet of the same God — casting the maximal (polemical) verdict that "Islam is false."
**THE FORK that makes it a dilemma:** a Muslim must either (a) **affirm** that the Qur'an endorses the prior
scripture → **incoherence** (it endorses what it contradicts), or (b) **deny** that the Qur'an endorses the
prior scripture → **apostasy** (contradicting the Qur'an's own repeated testimony).

⭐ **THE SINGLE MOST USEFUL MOVE FOR US — the "decoupling":** the author explicitly states that **the Bible's
level of preservation is IRRELEVANT to the argument.** Even if the Bible *were* corrupted, the Qur'an still
erred by affirming it as true/divine. This sidesteps **both** traps at once: (i) the Muslim *tahrif*
("your Bible is corrupted") escape, **and** (ii) our own retired **Dead-Sea-Scrolls overreach** ("the Bible is
preserved unchanged"). The dilemma needs only: *Qur'an affirms the prior scripture as divine* + *that scripture
contradicts the Qur'an.* This is the framing our content should use if we ever build this.

**⚠ Bounded verdict for us:** the book's endpoint ("Islam is false / Muhammad not a prophet") is the maximal
polemical claim. Per our **EXPLICIT-VERDICT rule**, our content would land the *bounded* verdict the evidence
supports (the specific confirmation-vs-contradiction incoherence, stated plainly in our own voice), gracious in
tone, never triumphal.

### PART 1 — The Torah & Gospel of the Qur'an = the actual biblical texts (identity)
Four-step method: find every naming verse → establish the facts the Qur'an attributes → analyze → best explanation.
- **The Torah** (Tawrat, named 18× / 16 verses): sent by Allah to Moses; still revered, "preserved and transmitted
  by their rabbis and scholars" in the 7th c.; Qur'an affirms its **legal rulings** (Q 5:45 "life for life, eye
  for eye… in the Torah"); challenges Jews to **"Bring the Torah and read it"** to settle a dispute (**Q 3:93**,
  tied to Jacob's sinew, **Gen 32:22–32 / gid hanasheh**).
- **The Gospel** (Injil, 12×, mostly Medinan, surahs 3 & 5): Arabic ← Syriac loanword ← Greek **euangelion** (the
  term for the fourfold Gospel; Jeffery, *Foreign Vocabulary of the Qur'an*); granted to Jesus, written, held by
  7th-c. Christians; the Qur'an "contains NO mention of this Gospel being textually altered… nor of multiple
  Gospels." Companions likened to a growing plant **"in the Injil"** (Q 48:29); monasticism as post-Jesus
  innovation (Q 57:27).
- **Paraphrased biblical stories** (proof of dependence): Abraham (Gen 12–22 // Q 21, 29, 11, 37), Joseph
  (Gen 37–47 // Surah 12, the longest Qur'anic prophet narrative), Moses (Exod 1–14 // surahs 2, 7, 20, 26, 28).
  The Qur'an assumes the audience supplies context *from their scripture* → treats it as preserved/authoritative.
- **Biblical "turns of phrase"** (Reynolds): "eye for eye" (Q 5:45 // Exod 21:24), the covenant idiom inverted to
  "we hear and disobey" (Q 2:93, 4:46 // Exod 24:7), **mustard seed** (Q 21:47, 31:16 // Matt 17:20), **camel
  through the needle's eye** (Q 7:40 // Matt 19:24 — incl. the Greek *kamēlos*/*kamilos* & Arabic *jamal*/*jummal*
  variant, per Reynolds & **Andrew Rippin**), "seventy times" forgiveness (Q 9:80 // Matt 18:22), Q 21:105 //
  **Psalm 37:29**.
- **The non-canonical tell:** Jesus forms a **bird from clay** (Q 3:49, 5:110) — from the **Arabic Infancy
  Gospel / Infancy Gospel of Thomas** (J. K. Elliott, *The Apocryphal New Testament*, 1993; Elias & Orfali, *The
  Gospel of the Infancy*, Brill 2019).
- **External/manuscript evidence** (the 7th-c. text ≈ today's): **Dead Sea Scrolls**, **Septuagint**, **Leningrad
  Codex**, **Codex Sinaiticus** (c.330–60; Parker 2010) & **Vaticanus**, and — the distinctive move — the
  **Garima Gospels** (Ethiopic Ge'ez, radiocarbon 330–650; McKenzie et al., *The Garima Gospels*, Oxford 2016)
  tied to the **First Hijra to Aksum (~613–615)**, so Muhammad's own companions engaged the church that held the
  four Gospels. Plus the **Najran martyrs** (Q 85:4–10) whom the Qur'an calls **"believers" (*mu'minin*)** —
  Nicene-affirming Miaphysite Christians who held the Garima Gospels.
  ⚠ **DSS FRAMING:** the book mostly says "same core narratives," but a few lines slip toward "substantially
  identical" — **use Ulrich, *The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Biblical Text* (1999), and Tov, *Textual Criticism of
  the Hebrew Bible*, for the *pluriformity* framing** (matches our own retired `deadseascrolls.html` overreach).
- **Early Muslim engagement with the Bible** (the *Isrāʾīliyyāt*): **Ibn Saʿd**'s *Kitāb al-Ṭabaqāt* (Ka'b
  al-Aḥbār retelling the Hezekiah/Isaiah "15 years," 2 Kings 20); **Nuʿaym al-Marwazī**'s *Kitāb al-Fitan*;
  **Ibn Ishāq**'s *Sīra* (via Ibn Hishām; Guillaume tr.) reading the **Paraclete** (John 15:23–16:1) as Muhammad;
  hadith echoing the Gospel (Bukhari 1423 // Matt 6:3–4; the workday parable // Matt 20; **Abu Dawud 4452**,
  Muhammad judging by "what you find in the Tawrat," with the eyewitness detail traced to the **Talmud, Makkot
  7a** — a model of the book's fair objection-handling).

### PART 2 — The Qur'an CLAIMS CONTINUITY with the Torah & Gospel (the affirmation horn, P1)
Four categories, with the **Qur'an Passage Index** (below) as the master verse list.
- **Confirmation (*muṣaddiq*):** 18 verses, broad/blanket, spoken by Allah, Muhammad, Jesus, and the Jinn (+ John
  Q 19:12, Mary Q 66:12). The exegetical crux = *muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayhi* ("confirming what is between the
  hands") — past vs **contemporary** scripture; the author argues contemporary (6 of the 18 use *limā maʿakum*,
  "what is WITH you," explicitly contemporary), leaning on **Ibn Abbas, al-Jalālayn, Ibn Kathīr** and translation
  care (Shakir vs Pickthall vs Yusuf Ali).
- **Names:** the Qur'an gives the Torah & Gospel its *own* honorifics — Al-Kitāb, Al-Furqān, Al-Hudā, An-Nūr,
  Al-Dhikr — and calls the Torah **"Book of Allah" (Kitāb Allāh, Q 5:44)** and "Book of Moses."
- **Accountability:** the Torah is authoritative for judgment (Q 5:43), rabbis "required to guard the Book of
  Allah" (Q 5:44); Christians commanded to "judge by what Allah revealed in it [the Injil]" (**Q 5:47**);
  Muhammad told to **"ask those who read the Book before you"** if in doubt (Q 10:94, 16:43, 21:7).
- **Belief commanded:** believing "the Books of Allah" is a pillar (Q 2:285); the *ummī* prophet "found written
  in the Tawrat and Injil" (Q 7:157).
- **Two strong analytical points:** (a) the Qur'an's whole strategy (appeal to the Torah's Muhammad-prophecy;
  hold Jews to the Mosaic Law) *presupposes a reliable Torah*; (b) neither Muhammad nor (per Q 3:50) Jesus ever
  *warns* of textual corruption — which they surely would if it had occurred.

### The pivot chapters — why "general confirmation" and "corruption" both fail
- **Total vs general confirmation:** the "the Qur'an only confirms it *generally*" escape is a **modern ad hoc
  invention** absent from classical scholarship; the Qur'an itself **condemns** believing "in one part of the
  Scripture and rejecting the other" (**Q 2:85**) and "those who break the Qur'an into parts" (Q 15:90–93). And
  the *major* themes (crucifixion, resurrection, incarnation) are exactly what the Qur'an denies. Even "shared
  monotheism" fails as common ground — the Qur'an calls "Jesus is the Son of God" *shirk* (a **FALSE-COMMON-
  GROUND** model handled well).
- **"Conscious edits":** the theory that the Qur'an deliberately *corrects* corrupted scripture fails 3 ways —
  (1) **no motive** (why would Jews corrupt their own text to make Moses's hand "leprous," Exod 4?); (2)
  **question-begging** (assumes the Qur'an is true); (3) **unfalsifiable** ("agreement = confirmation,
  disagreement = correction" explains everything, so nothing). Q 5:48 **"muhaymin"** ("guardian over it") — the
  main counter — is answered via **Nicolai Sinai** (*Key Terms of the Qur'an*, 2023): *muhaymin* ≈ *muṣaddiq*
  ("trustworthy, entrusted with authority"), correcting *interpretation/concealment* (*bayāna*), not the text;
  the verse's own continuation ("do not follow *their inclinations*") judges *people*, not a text (cf. Q 45:16–18).
- **"Universal textual corruption is impossible":** partial corruption leaves the original *waḥy* intact (forcing
  reconciliation); universal, simultaneous corruption across Egypt/Yemen/Syria/Spain and many languages is
  untenable — **Muslim scholars concede** the Bible is too widespread to be universally corrupted. Historical
  witnesses (**Josephus, Philo, DSS, LXX, church fathers**) attest the text; and to escape the early Gospel
  manuscripts a Muslim must date corruption *immediately* after Jesus — meaning his own disciples (Muslims per
  the Qur'an) fabricated Christianity at once.
- **⭐ The Syriac milieu** (the "conscious edits" become "borrowed legends"): the Qur'an's biblical retellings
  reflect **Christian Syriac tradition**, not the Hebrew Bible — **Joseph Witztum, *The Syriac Milieu of the
  Quran*** (Princeton diss., 2011); the Fathers **Ephrem the Syrian** (d. 373), **Aphrahat** (*Demonstrations*),
  **Jacob of Serugh** (d. 521), **Narsai** (d. c. 502); apocrypha **Life of Adam and Eve** (*Vita Adae*),
  **Cave of Treasures** (Budge tr.), **Genesis Rabbah** (Abraham & the idols), **Book of Jubilees** (Charles tr.,
  the Abraham-fiery-furnace legend, traced through Jerome's Vulgate → Augustine's *City of God* → Targum
  Pseudo-Jonathan). **Charbel Rizk, *The Joseph Story in the Qur'an and in the Syriac Tradition* (Brill, 2023).**

### PART 3 — The eight specific contradictions (P2) — each with Qur'an ↔ Bible verses
Whether the Bible is *true* is beside the point; the Qur'an *claims* one voice (Q 3:3–4, 2:136). **Uses Q 4:82
correctly** — the Qur'an's OWN no-contradiction test turned *internally* (not a false "same standard" symmetry,
the error our CLAUDE.md flags). Abrogation (*naskh*) can only touch *legal* rulings, not theology.
1. **Jesus is crucified.** Q 4:157 denies it; Matt 27 / Mark 15 / Luke 23 / John 19 + Josephus/Tacitus affirm it.
   Rebuts the "substitution / it appeared so" theory (collective misperception of crucifixion + appearances +
   empty tomb; and it makes **Allah a deceiver**). **Aligns with our minimal-facts resurrection content.**
2. **Jesus is more than a prophet** (deity of Christ — **DUAL-CONSENSUS for us**). Qur'an: mere human (Q 5:73
   "third of three"); Gospel: divine authority — forgives sins (Mark 2:10), commands demons/nature (Mark 1:25,
   Luke 8:24), Great Commission Trinitarian formula + worship (Matt 28:17–20), Son of Man (Dan 7:13–14 // Mark
   14:62), the **Logos** (John 1:1–14; and the book **refutes** the "Logos is abstract" move — John's Word is a
   *real, uncreated, causal* agent), Thomas "My Lord and my God" (John 20:28). **Q 5:116 misidentifies the
   Trinity as Allah + Jesus + Mary.**
3. **God is a Father.** Qur'an denies a son (Q 9:30 incl. the puzzling "Ezra is the son of Allah"; Q 43:81, 4:171)
   and deliberately says "son of Mary"; Torah/Gospel affirm God as Father (Deut 32:6; John 5:23 "honor the Son
   *as* the Father"; Matt 3:16–17). **Quotes the Nicene Creed as the target; denominationally neutral on the
   Eucharist; notes the Qur'an may attack *adoptionism* — which orthodoxy also rejects.**
4. **Muhammad prophesied in the Torah & Gospel** (an *absence* contradiction). Q 7:157 says the *ummī* prophet is
   "written with them" — but the Paraclete = the Spirit (John 14:26), Deut 18 = a Jewish prophet/Jesus, and the
   Isaiah 29:11–12 "sealed book" proof-text is about Jerusalem's judgment (Jesus quotes 29:13 re hypocrisy).
5. **The command to wage warfare.** Q 9:111 (& 61:10–14) says fighting is "binding in the Tawrat and the Injil"
   — but Jesus's kingdom is "not of this world" (John 18:36).
6. **God loves the unbelievers.** Gospel/Torah: God loves the world/enemies/foreigners (John 3:16, Luke 6:27–36,
   Deut 10:17–19); Qur'an: Allah does *not* love the unbelievers (Q 3:32, 30:45). **A MODEL of honest argument** —
   concedes the OT wrath passages, keeps the critique *internal*. ⚠ Distinguishes *raḥma* (mercy) from *maḥabba*
   (love) — **the careful way; avoid the "Islam has no grace" overreach.**
7. **Heaven and hell.** Q 87:18–19 puts hellfire "in the scriptures of Ibrahim and Musa" — but the Torah has only
   *Sheol* (Johnston, *Shades of Sheol*); plus marriage/sexual pleasure in *jannah* (Q 2:25, etc.) vs Jesus's "no
   marriage in the resurrection" (Matt 22:30). ⚠ Present the sexual-paradise material factually, never mockingly.
8. **Stories of the prophets.** Substantial divergences with no plausible corruption motive (Joseph "wolf"
   Q 12:13 vs "ferocious animal" Gen 37:33; the serpent = Satan, from Rev 12:9 not Genesis; Iblis refusing to
   prostrate to Adam, from the *Vita Adae*) → the Qur'an engages *modified legend*, not the original text.

### PART 4 — The "corruption verses" (taḥrīf) — none establishes TEXTUAL corruption
- The verb **yuḥarrifu** (4×: Q 2:75, 4:46, 5:13, 5:41) = **verbal/oral** distortion (Q 4:46 "with their
  tongues"); the **concealment** charge (Q 5:15) *presupposes the text exists* ("you can only conceal what you
  have"). 3 of 4 target Jews, not the Gospel.
- **The single strongest verse, Q 2:79** ("those who **write** the book with their hands"): the verb *yaktubūna*
  = "to **write**," not "to distort" (some translations, e.g. Khattab's *Clear Qur'an*, inject a bias). Its
  context (Surah al-Baqarah, the Children of Israel) is about a *specific party* inventing a false doctrine
  (Jews in hell "a few days," Q 2:80) — a claim traceable to the **Talmud (Shabbat 33b) / Mishnah (Eduyot 2:10)**,
  **not the Torah**. ⭐ **Clever resolution:** the "book written with hands" = the **rabbinic tradition (Talmud)**,
  which the Qur'an scrutinizes, while it *affirms* the Torah (bracketed by Q 2:41 & 2:89, "verifying what is with
  you"). So even the strongest verse gives no basis for *taḥrīf lafẓī*.
