> ### Usage rules (READ FIRST)
> - **Every citation below is an UNVERIFIED LEAD** until confirmed against the **primary** source and run
>   through `apologia-citations → apologia-argument → apologia-orthodoxy` (+ `apologia-neutrality`: this is
>   **resurrection tier → dual-consensus** for any live change). Never quote Loke's prose; never cite "Loke
>   says X." Cite the **primary** he routes to, verified.
> - **Loke is a committed DEFENDER, not a neutral historian** (the book is dedicated to William Lane Craig,
>   "for answering so many of my questions"; Loke did his PhD under Alister McGrath and is a formal
>   analytic philosopher of religion). His *conclusions* are advocacy; his *value to us* is (a) the
>   primary sources and named critics he engages, and (b) the **formal rigour** of his method. Weigh his
>   verdicts, port his citations.
> - **Keep the standing resurrection guardrails** (see `CLAUDE.md`): the case is **the best explanation,
>   not a proof**; the near-consensus fact is that the disciples **had experiences they took to be
>   appearances**, NOT that a body left a tomb (the appearances/empty-tomb firewall); lead with the
>   1 Cor 15:3–7 creed; never frame the evidence as "merely written decades later"; "sincerity, not
>   truth" for the martyrdom plank; label the empty tomb the **soft point** (more contested than the
>   appearances). Do not inflate "majority" into "virtually all."
> - **This is an ACADEMIC monograph**, so popular-level overstatement should be rare — but Loke argues a
>   thesis, so treat any unanimity claim ("no scholar denies…") as a lead to soften, per rule 7.

## Book map — chapters, argument, primary-source leads
> *(Filled from a full read of the extracted text, 2026-09-21. Loke's method is a formal step-by-step
> elimination of a "logically exhaustive" disjunction of hypotheses — the distinctive spine of the book.)*

**The book's spine (what makes it distinctive).** Loke runs a single **argument-by-elimination over a
"logically exhaustive" disjunction.** Ch. 1 formalises *every* possible explanation of the appearance
claims (**6 categories**) and *every* possible fate of the body (**8 categories**); the rest of the book
knocks out each naturalistic branch until only "resurrection" remains. This is an analytic-philosophy
elimination *proof* rather than the usual "minimal facts" cumulative case — his signature contribution,
and his answer to Ehrman's boast that he can "dream up" twenty alternatives and to Allison's "you can't
decisively eliminate all the alternatives." Loke is an ex-medical-doctor analytic philosopher (PhD under
McGrath), which is why the hallucination chapter works actual clinical psychiatry rather than waving at it.
The book expands his 2009 *Journal of Theological Studies* 60: 570–584 article.

- **Ch. 1 — Introduction / the framework (pp. 1–39).** Lays out the two exhaustive disjunctions
  (*appearances:* legend / no-experience-hoax / intramental-hallucination / mistaken-identity /
  swoon-escape / resurrection; *body:* escape / unburied / remained-buried / removed-by-non-agent /
  by-friends / by-enemies / by-neutral-robbers / swoon / resurrection) and the deductive skeleton
  (I people claimed to see the risen Jesus soon after; II they had an experience; III caused
  extramentally; IV the entity was the Jesus who died → ∴ V resurrection) — establishing I–IV needs
  *no* appeal to the supernatural; whether *God* (vs. an angel/alien) caused it is deferred to Ch. 8.
  Distinctive: the exhaustiveness *proof*; the clean "did it happen" vs "what caused it" split (Licona).
  **Primary leads:** Origen, *Contra Celsum* 2.59 (**Phlegon's *Chronicles*, c. AD 140** — a checkable
  non-Christian notice); **James Ware, *NTS* 2014, p. 486/494** (*egeirō*/*anastasis* = revivification of
  the corpse); Justin, *First Apology* 21, 23–24, 54 and *Dial.* 108; Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 (authenticity
  defence); Sandmel 1962 "parallelomania." ⚠ **Ch. 1 hazard — see verdict 5 below** (the "the body was
  missing" consensus claim over-includes the empty tomb).
- **Ch. 2 — the claim was really made (pp. 40–67).** Eliminates *legend*: named individuals + groups
  genuinely *claimed* to have seen the risen Jesus. **1 Cor 15:3–11 is not a later interpolation** (vs Price
  2005 / Sturdy 2007 — no extant copy lacks it; no caliph-like authority to alter dispersed copies); the
  eyewitnesses were **checkable** (1 Cor 15:6 "most remain until now"); apparent contradictions are the
  signature of independent testimony, several resolvable via ancient literary devices (Licona/Plutarch).
  Distinctive: the psychology-of-testimony frame (DiFonzo & Bordia 2007); the valid-vs-invalid
  argument-from-silence rule; the (A)-interpret / (B)-show-compatible / (C)-prove-a-harmonisation task
  distinction. **Primary leads:** Craig 1989 pp. 2–3 (six non-Pauline stylistic markers of the creed);
  Licona 2010 pp. 325–329 (third-day multiple-attestation verse list); Dunn 2003 p. 856 ("all the apostles"
  ≠ the Twelve).
- **Ch. 3 — it was experience, not a hoax (pp. 68–88).** Eliminates *no-experience/lie*. ⭐ Distinctive and
  **well-calibrated:** reframes the martyrdom plank as **willingness-to-suffer, not "they died therefore it
  is true,"** and *concedes on the record* the Moss/Meier debate over how many apostles were actually
  martyred. Adds the **monotheistic false-witness lever** (1 Cor 15:15 — devout Second-Temple Jews would
  fear divine condemnation for fabricating a resurrection: "we are found to be misrepresenting God"), and
  the "you couldn't fake piety consistently in front of both believers and persecutors" point. **Primary
  leads:** Josephus *Ant.* 20.9 (James); McDowell, *Fate of the Apostles* (2015) p. 91 (Peter's death at
  "highest possible probability" + the source-chain); Tacitus *Ann.* 15.44 (Nero); Hengel, *Crucifixion*.
- **Ch. 4 — the entity was extramental, not a hallucination (pp. 89–111).** ⭐ The chapter where Loke most
  out-rigours a popular treatment: he works the **actual clinical-psychiatry literature** (Aleman & Larøi
  2008; Blom, *A Dictionary of Hallucinations*, 2009; David 2004; Barber & Calverley 1964's "White
  Christmas" study; **Bergeron & Habermas 2015** — collective hallucinations are *absent from the
  peer-reviewed medical literature*). Careful definitional groundwork (hallucination vs illusion vs
  delusion vs vision); the group-perception-needs-a-common-cause argument; the hostile-fact-checker point
  (Matt 28:11–15). He honestly steelmans Allison and **refuses to rule out genuine spiritual manifestations
  a priori** (deferred to Ch. 8) — doctrinally careful. ⚠ **Denominational hazard — see verdict-5/NR:**
  he classifies **Marian apparitions** (Pontmain, Garabandal) as illusions/frauds.
- **Ch. 5 — it was Jesus, who really died (pp. 112–120).** Eliminates *mistaken-identity, swoon, escape* at
  once with a single move: **"transphysicality"** (physical yet passing through doors, appearing/vanishing)
  — no twin, substitute, or revived-half-dead man could naturalistically produce it. Uses eyewitness-ID
  psychology (Wells & Bradfield) and crucifixion physiology (Edwards et al., *JAMA*, 1986; Josephus *Life*
  420 — the one-survivor-of-three case; *JW* 6.304). ⚠ **Bauckham 2011 cited — observe the standing
  universalism fence** (historian-only, name the work).
- **Ch. 6 — the fate of the body (pp. 121–142).** Eliminates the tomb branches. ⚠ **Loke's linchpin is the
  historicity of Matthew's guards** — an *affirmative* case (Matt 28:15 "to this day" as a falsifiability
  window), which is a **minority position** most critical scholars reject. Also a granular refutation of
  **Carrier's "two-body" reading** of 1 Cor 15 (pp. 134–138). **Primary leads:** Craig 1984, "The Guard at
  the Tomb," *NTS* 30: 273–281; **Peter Bolt 1996, "Mark 16:1–8: The Empty Tomb of a Hero?" *Tyndale
  Bulletin* 47: 27–37** (open-access; debunks the Hellenistic empty-tomb-of-a-hero parallel); Justin *Dial.*
  108 + Tertullian *De Spect.* 30 (enemy attestation — already ours). ⚠ Talpiot "one in 79" statistics are
  reported figures — verify against Ilan/Hachlili onomastics before repeating.
- **Ch. 7 — combination hypotheses (pp. 143–165). ⭐ THE BOOK'S MOST DISTINCTIVE CHAPTER — and the genuine
  gap in our library.** Loke names and defeats the skeptic's best move — *stacking* individually-failed
  theories (hallucination + cognitive dissonance + memory distortion + confirmation bias) — **as
  combinations**, and does it by turning the skeptics' *own* memory-science authorities against them:
  **Vansina** (Ehrman quotes him for the "telephone game" but omits his crucial qualifications — foundational
  traditions aim "to repeat exactly," and "the method of transmission is of far greater importance than the
  length of time"); **David Rubin 1995** (the **"net vs. chain"** distinction — real oral tradition is a
  *net* where parallel versions correct errors, not a one-line *chain*); **Schudson** (his own
  four-distortion model does *not* imply memory relativism); **Kirk 2017 / Rodríguez 2016** (Ehrman
  misreads the memory scholars he cites); **Redman 2010** (flashbulb memories for high-salience events);
  the **Keim 1883** "why did the ecstasy stop dead after the fifth appearance?" argument (public domain);
  and the **Chabad/Schneerson** comparative case handled with a *sympathetic* source (Marcus 2001) and real
  disanalogies. **This whole apparatus is absent from our essays (grep-confirmed).**
- **Ch. 8 — the problem of miracles (pp. 166–198).** Hume/Spinoza fail. ⚠ **Loke uses Timothy McGrew
  (SEP "Miracles" 2013; McGrew & McGrew, Blackwell 2009), NOT Earman** — Earman is absent from his
  bibliography, so Loke is *not* a cite for our Earman material. His distinctive reframes: **"causal
  pathways, not frequencies"**; the explicit **prior-vs-posterior** Bayesian distinction (a low prior
  Pr(R|B) is compatible with a high posterior Pr(R|B&E) once the naturalistic branches are excluded); the
  **free-agent-vs-infrequency** point (infrequency excludes impersonal natural causes, not a free personal
  agent — Davis's red car); Whately's Napoleon satire. ⚠ **Three hazards → verdict 5 / NR:** the **99.4% /
  "< 1/2¹²⁸"** figures are illustrative constructions, **do not import as a statistic**; the **Marian
  apparitions** dismissal (denominational); the **Islamic substitution/escape** handling (Qur'an 4:157–58;
  route through the Islam guardrails — EXPLICIT-VERDICT + FALSE-COMMON-GROUND). Modern-miracle empirical
  leads (Candy Gunther Brown, *Testing Prayer*, Harvard 2012; Keener, *Miracles*) do **not** fit
  `miracles.html`, which is deliberately narrowed to the in-principle question.
- **Ch. 9 — conclusion (pp. 199–206).** The **"X must entail false consequences"** test for historical
  certainty (a hidden overturning-cause would have left detectable traces it didn't). ⚠ Loke concludes
  **"historical certainty,"** going *further* than Habermas/Licona's "best explanation" — **past our own
  guardrail** (see verdict 5). Quotable convert testimony: **Paul Williams** (Buddhologist), *The Unexpected
  Way* (2002), pp. 134–135. ⚠ p. 199 repeats the "consensus… the body was missing" over-inclusion.

## Is the source better than our essays anywhere? Where, and how?
**Yes — in three places, and all three are real.** (1) ⭐ **Ch. 7's combination-hypotheses + memory-science
apparatus** is genuinely *missing* from our resurrection cluster: nowhere do our essays answer the
"telephone game / the story just got exaggerated in the retelling" objection — one of the most common
objections a believer actually meets — and Loke answers it with the skeptics' own authorities (Vansina,
Rubin, Schudson, Kirk). This is the top find. (2) Ch. 3's **monotheistic false-witness lever** (1 Cor
15:15) is a clean, distinctive argument against the hoax hypothesis that our `disciplesbelief.html` does
not have. (3) Ch. 4 **grounds in the actual clinical-psychiatry literature** (Bergeron & Habermas 2015)
the very claim our `appearances.html` currently rests on assertion ("clinical psychiatry recognises no
mechanism"). A fourth, weaker, is the **formal exhaustive-disjunction framework** as a complement to our
IBE framing — but that is Loke's own contested methodological claim, so it is a careful *maybe*, not an
unqualified win. **Everywhere else the comparison runs the other way or is a tie:** our essays are equal or
*more careful* on the swoon theory (we now carry JAMA 1986 + Josephus *Life* 420–421 + Strauss 1:412), on
the martyrdom plank (we already reframe to sincerity + steelman Moss), on the empty tomb's tiering as the
soft point, and on refusing to overclaim (Loke's "historical certainty" and guards-as-linchpin are moves
we are *right* not to make).
