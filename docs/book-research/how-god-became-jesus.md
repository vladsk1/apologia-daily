## Thesis / spine (our words)
Ehrman's *How Jesus Became God* argues an **evolutionary** account:
1. Jesus was an apocalyptic prophet who did not think he was God.
2. The resurrection visions led his followers to say God had **exalted** him (an "exaltation" or "adoptionist"
   Christology, which they pushed back from the resurrection to the baptism and then to the birth).
3. Alongside this ran an "incarnation" Christology (Paul's Christ as an angel who became human; John's as higher still).
4. Equality with the Father was settled only at Nicaea.

The five contributors reply that:
- **Jewish monotheism was strict enough** that the inclusion of Jesus in the worship and identity of the one God was a
  real "mutation" (Hurtado), not one more step on a porous ladder (chs. 1–2).
- **Jesus' own self-presentation** as the agent of YHWH's return was the seed (ch. 3).
- **The burial** in a known tomb is history, not late fiction (ch. 4).
- **The Synoptics** already carry preexistence and divine prerogatives, and the "adoptionist" fragments describe new
  **roles**, not a new identity (ch. 5).
- **Ehrman's categories** (two Christologies, "divine", Gal 4:14 as angel Christology, "inclusive monotheism") are
  imposed on the texts (chs. 6–7).
- **The second- and third-century church** did not suppress an earlier adoptionist majority; the paradoxes are
  already in the NT, and anti-Judaism does not follow from Christ's deity (chs. 8–9).

## ⭐ STANDING OUTPUT — Is the source better than our essays anywhere? Where, and how?
**Yes, in four places. Everywhere else ours is as good or better, often clearly better.**
1. **The "adoptionist" proof-texts (Gathercole, ch. 5).** Our deity essays state Ehrman's exaltation/adoption
   Christology fairly and answer it with **worship** and **Synoptic preexistence**, but never touch the texts it is
   built on:
   - Rom 1:3–4; Acts 2:36; Acts 13:33; the baptismal voice of Mark 1:11.

   Gathercole answers them directly:
   - **Mark 1:11** "You are my Son" *declares* a status, repeated at the transfiguration (9:7); no one is adopted twice.
   - **Ps 2:7 in Acts 13:33** is applied typologically, the way Matt 2:18 applies Jer 31:15.
   - **Acts 2:36:** Luke already calls Jesus "Christ the Lord" at his birth (2:11).
   - **Exaltation** confers **new roles** (giver of the Spirit, Lord of the church, judge of all), not a new identity.

   A reader meets these verses in the wild; we leave them hanging. → **HGBJ-1 (P2)**.
2. **Paul's devotional language beyond 1 Cor 8:6 (Tilling, ch. 7).** Several verses carry the same move:
   - 1 Cor 8:12 (sinning against Christ);
   - 1 Cor 10:9 and 10:22 (testing Christ / provoking the Lord to jealousy, Deut 32's language for Israel and YHWH);
   - 1 Thess 3:11–13 (a prayer addressed to Jesus, with a singular verb in 3:11).

   Together they show Paul treating Christ the way Scripture treats YHWH. That is stronger than our single verse.
   → **HGBJ-4**.
3. **The second-century evidence (Hill, chs. 8–9 + excursuses).** We answer the Artemonites but not the Ebionites. We
   cite Tertullian for *trinitas* but never answer "modalism was the majority" or "Tertullian was a subordinationist".
   We never use the material evidence of worship (nomina sacra; Megiddo; the Alexamenos graffito *as* evidence of
   worship). → **HGBJ-2, -3, -5, -9**.
4. **The Similitudes counter-example (Bird, ch. 2).** Bird names the one Jewish text where a figure other than God
   receives obeisance (1 Enoch's Son of Man) and answers it. Our essays hedge with "characteristically refuse worship"
   but never name the case. → **HGBJ-7**.

- **Son of Man / Daniel 7:** ours is the correct one; Bird's is a retired claim.
