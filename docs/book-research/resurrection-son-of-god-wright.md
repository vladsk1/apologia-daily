## Thesis / spine (own words)
Wright's case is cumulative and historical: he first maps, exhaustively, what "resurrection" and "life after death" meant across paganism (Part One), the Old Testament and post-biblical Judaism, to show the word had a fixed, bodily, future, corporate sense and that **no one in that world expected a single individual raised bodily in the middle of history**. He then traces the resurrection belief through Paul (Part Two), the rest of early Christianity (Part Three), and the four Easter narratives (Part Four), showing a set of unprecedented "mutations" in Jewish hope. Part Five argues that the empty tomb **and** the appearances, together, are the only sufficient historical cause of that belief, and that the rise of the belief points to the bodily resurrection of Jesus as "the Son of God."

Cross-check our `isaiah53` wording.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **(iv) On the Third Day: Hosea (pp. 116–117) — the standout lead:**
  - ⭐⭐⭐ **Hosea 6:1–2** — "Come, let us return to YHWH … After two days he will revive us; **on the third day he will raise us up, that we may live before him.**" Wright: in its original 8th-c. context this is a prayer the prophet treats as *inadequate/shallow*, but "later readers, including later biblical writers, would have taken it in a more positive sense … the earliest explicit statement that YHWH will give his people a new bodily life the other side of death," and **it appears to have influenced Daniel 12 (perhaps via Isaiah).** ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the OT root of "raised on the third day *in accordance with the Scriptures*" (1 Cor 15:4)** — a text our `earlycreed` essay does NOT currently identify as the "third day" source. **STRONG backlog candidate (P2/P3) for `earlycreed`** (pair with the corporate/Jonah "third day" typology; keep Wright's caution about the original sense).

Original Hebrew ≈ a **threat** (YHWH will NOT redeem), but the **LXX and Paul (1 Corinthians 15:55!)** take it positively — Paul quotes it at the climax of the resurrection chapter. ⭐ Direct `earlycreed`/`appearances` lead (shows Paul reading Hosea as resurrection Scripture).
- ⚠ **(v) Dry Bones: Ezekiel 37 (pp. 117–119).** The most famous "resurrection" image, but in context an **allegory/metaphor for national restoration** (return from exile; opening Israel's "graves," 37:11–14), using concrete resurrection imagery + Genesis 1–2 breath/*ruach*. Later read as literal resurrection support (rabbinic; the **Dura-Europos** paintings). ⚠ **ACTIONABLE caution: do not cite Ezekiel 37 as a straightforward OT proof of individual bodily resurrection** — it is national-restoration metaphor (though it helped the idea along). Cross-check any essay/`/sources` use.
- **(vi) Resurrection and the Hope of Israel (p. 119+):** Wright's frame — resurrection hope is "**not a move away from the hope which characterized all of ancient Israel, but a reaffirmation of it**" (national restoration, creation renewed), NOT a backdoor import; and it is bodily, unlike the "blessed but non-bodily" hint of Psalm 73.

#### Ch 3 §4(vi) origin + §5 Conclusion — pp. 120–127 (CHAPTER THREE COMPLETE)
- ⭐⭐ **Two "borrowed resurrection" theories REFUTED (direct `uniqueness.html` / "was resurrection copied?" leads):**
  - **Zoroastrian / Persian borrowing** (the stock claim that Israel got resurrection from Persia during the exile): Wright rejects it — the Zoroastrian primary sources are **late and uncertainly dated**; Daniel (the main biblical exponent) echoes **Isaiah and Hosea**, taking the stream back *behind* any Persian influence; and Israel's belief was **not dualistic**, whereas Zoroastrianism was. The borrowing thesis only survives if a discredited "apocalyptic = dualism" assumption is in play. ⭐⭐ **Strong lead** — this specific skeptical claim ("resurrection is Persian/Zoroastrian in origin") may not be answered on `uniqueness.html`; Wright is the answer.
  - **Canaanite dying-rising god (Baal) borrowing** (John Day et al. on Hosea): has surface plausibility for Hosea alone but cannot explain Daniel 12 / Isaiah / Ezekiel 37; and **YHWH was not a vegetation/fertility god** ("sovereign over creation, not part of it"), so the Baal model does not transfer.

"A bold step … but the last step in a comprehensible line going back to the earliest roots of Israelite belief." → the frame for "in accordance with the Scriptures" as organic, not proof-texted.
- **§5:** the constant factor is **YHWH himself** (creation + covenant). Resurrection = the **reversal** of death (not a reconstrual of "life after death"); its biblical language ("standing up," "awakening") is "simple and direct."

### Chapter Four: Time to Wake Up (2) — Hope beyond Death in Post-Biblical Judaism — pp. 129 ff. mined 2026-10-26 (IN PROGRESS; long chapter, mostly CORROBORATION of our "bodily/corporate/two-stage/future" framing in `disciplesbelief`/`postres`)
- **§1 Introduction: The Spectrum (pp. 129–131).** "Jews believed in resurrection, Greeks in immortality" is a half-truth; 2nd-Temple Judaism is "an artist's palette: dozens of options." Key points (all corroborate our essays): by Jesus' day most Jews held resurrection or knew it as standard (Daniel 12 the touchstone); **resurrection is TWO-STAGE** (an *interim state*, then re-embodiment) and so ≠ the single-stage disembodied immortality; the precise mode (resuscitation vs transformation) was left imprecise.
- **§2 The Sadducees (pp. 131–134).** The ruling priestly elite **denied** resurrection (and any significant future life) on a strict/conservative OT reading. Sources: **Mark 12:18–27 // Matt 22 // Luke 20** (the 7-husbands *reductio* to Jesus); **Acts 23:6–8** (Paul splits the Sanhedrin — "the Sadducees say there is no resurrection, neither angel nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge them both"); **Josephus** (Sadducees: the soul perishes with the body); **m. Sanhedrin 10:1** (denying "the resurrection of the dead prescribed in the Law" forfeits the world to come); **Ben Sira / Ecclesiasticus** (14; 17; 38 — "there is no coming back," a no-afterlife witness the Sadducees would approve).
  - ⭐ **Intermediate-state lead (minor, for `appearances`/`disciplesbelief`):** Wright reads Acts 23:8 + Acts 12:15 ("It's his angel," of Peter) as evidence that the intermediate dead were thought to be "like angels or spirits" awaiting re-embodiment — i.e. "resurrection" never meant that disembodied state, but the *future bodily* life beyond it. Useful background for why the disciples' "we saw Jesus bodily" claim was not merely "we saw his ghost/angel."

#### Ch 4 §2 end + §3 Blessed (Disembodied) Immortality — pp. 134–140 (corroboration; the 2nd-Temple spectrum)
- **Why the Sadducees resisted (pp. 134–136):** resurrection was a **revolutionary doctrine** (Daniel 12 → resistance + martyrdom; the coming new age overturning the present order), so the priestly aristocracy had political reasons to deny it. (Corroborates `postres`' point about why the belief mattered.) Also **Tobit** (no real afterlife hope).
- **§3 the "middle" position — blessed *disembodied* immortality (soul separable from body), in hellenistic Judaism:** **Pseudo-Phocylides**, **Testament of Abraham**, **1 Enoch** (Ethiopic — immortal soul to bliss/torment), **4 Ezra** (bodily resurrection + interim soul/vessel language), **Hillel / Johanan ben Zakkai** (soul as guest in the house of life).
  - ⭐ **The key contrast (corroboration for `appearances`/`disciplesbelief`): 2 Maccabees vs 4 Maccabees.** **2 Macc 7** (the seven brothers) = unmistakably **bodily** resurrection — "I got these [hands] from Heaven … from him I hope to get them back again" (7:11) — *already cited on our `appearances` essay*. **4 Maccabees** goes the other way: the body can be killed but the **soul cannot** — immortality of the soul, not bodily resurrection (the patriarchs "live to God"). Together they display the spectrum. Low new-lead value; confirms our framing.

#### Ch 4 §3 end + §4 (i)–(ii) — pp. 141–148 (⭐ one good `earlycreed` lead: the LXX makes resurrection clearer)

(Plus the **Shema**.)
- ⭐⭐ **"Resurrection in the Bible: the More Greek the Better" (pp. 145–148) — a genuine `earlycreed` / `messianic-prophecy` lead.** In the **LXX** (3rd-c. Secondary: **H. C. C. ### ⭐⭐ Chapter Seven: Resurrection in Corinth (2): The Key Passages — pp. 311 ff. mined 2026-10-27 (THE most apologetically load-bearing chapter for `appearances`/`emptytomb`)
#### Ch 7 §1 (i)–(ii) Intro + 1 Cor 15:1–11 — pp. 311–316 (⭐⭐ strong corroboration + one wording lead)
- **Structure of 1 Cor 15** (Wright's outline): A 15:1–11 (gospel/witnesses); B 15:12–28 (the *when* — two stages, Messiah first then his people; firstfruits); C 15:29–34 (interlude); b 15:35–49 (the *what* — the kind of body); a 15:50–58 (conclusion — incorruptibility, victory). Genesis 1–3 is the subtext (new creation, Adam reversed, Ps 8:7).
- ⭐ The whole chapter is an argument for **FUTURE BODILY resurrection** — "if Paul were going to argue for a *non*-bodily resurrection, he would have done better not to structure his argument this way." "When Paul said 'resurrection', he meant 'bodily resurrection'."
- ⭐⭐ **WORDING/CITATION LEAD for `appearances` (the *sōma pneumatikon* section): Dale Martin's point** — "we should not assume that the ontological dualism between what modern westerners since Descartes think of as 'physical' and 'spiritual' … would have meant much to Paul's audiences. Most pagan philosophers who believed in souls thought they, like the body, were composed of **material, albeit in finer particles**." So "spiritual body" could NOT have meant "non-physical" to Paul's readers; and "neither in outline nor in detail does 1 Corinthians 15 resemble an argument for the immortality of the soul." (**Dale B. Martin, *The Corinthian Body*, Yale UP 1995.**) → strengthens our `appearances` anti-Carrier / *sōma pneumatikon* case; verify + consider adding.
- ⭐⭐ **1 Cor 15:1–11 = Paul citing WITNESSES to a real event** ("a fifth witness to the original Easter events, alongside the four gospels"). **Bultmann** criticised Paul for citing witnesses (as if treating it as an actual event rather than a "mythological" way of saying Jesus' death was a good thing); Wright: "Bultmann was simply wrong — the resurrection of Jesus was a real event as far as Paul was concerned."
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The appearances were a CLOSED, one-off class, NOT ongoing "spiritual experience" (corroborates our `appearances` "appearances ≠ later visions" / Keim section):** the witnesses are "a large, though finite, number … at least 500 … some had already died, and no more would be added, because the sightings had a temporal end; when Paul saw Jesus, that was the last (15:8)." "This reference to seeing the risen Jesus cannot … have anything to do with regular and normal … 'Christian experience' … As is clear from **1 Cor 9:1**, this 'seeing' constituted people as **apostles**, the one-off witnesses to a one-off event. The Corinthians had had every kind of spiritual experience imaginable … but they had not seen the risen Jesus, nor did they or Paul expect that they would." = our closed-appearances-class argument, source-of-record.

#### Ch 7 §1 (ii) 1 Cor 15:1–11 (the creed) — pp. 317–323 (⭐⭐⭐ major corroboration for `earlycreed`/`emptytomb`/`appearances`)
- ⭐ **Creed dated "within the first two or three years after Easter … said two decades or more before Paul wrote this letter"** — corroborates our `earlycreed` dating. The core (15:3b–5); *Christos* = **Messiah** (title, not proper name — a very early formula).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"on the third day in accordance with the scriptures" → HOSEA 6:2 (the strongest form of the `earlycreed` lead, now tied DIRECTLY to 1 Cor 15:4).** Wright: "'in accordance with the scriptures'" is NOT proof-texting but the whole biblical narrative reaching its climax; and the "third day / after three days" phrase "looking back mainly to **Hosea 6:2**, is frequently referred to in rabbinic mentions of the resurrection … by Paul's day this passage was being read in terms of resurrection itself ('after three days')." (Also Ezek 37; Isa 40–55.) → **This is the third independent place Wright grounds "third day" in Hosea 6:2 (Ch 3, Ch 4 Targum, now at 1 Cor 15:4 itself) — the `earlycreed` backlog row is now rock-solid (P2).**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"buried, then raised" entails the empty tomb (EXACT source for our `emptytomb` argument):** "The fact that the empty tomb … does not appear to be specifically mentioned in this passage is not significant; the mention here of 'buried, then raised' no more needs to be amplified in that way than one would need to amplify 'I walked down the street' with 'on my feet'." The burial (15:4a) certifies Jesus really died AND signals the **body** raised, leaving an empty tomb. (Our `emptytomb`/`appearances` already make this; Wright is the source-of-record.)
- ⭐ **The perfect-tense "raised" (*egēgertai*)** — unlike the aorists "died/buried/appeared" — marks the **ongoing result**: Jesus IS *now* the risen Messiah and Lord (passive = divine action).
- ⭐⭐ ***ōphthē* ("appeared/was seen") cannot settle objective-vs-subjective by itself** (LXX concordance: 85 occurrences, mixed visionary and ordinary) — "its meaning must be judged on wider criteria than linguistic usage alone." = **our `earlycreed`/`appearances` point about *ōphthē*** (we already say the verb alone can't establish a vision — Wright corroborates).
- ⭐ **The 500 could be interrogated:** "most are still alive … the strong implication — they could be interrogated for their own accounts … Paul would hardly call eyewitnesses for an experience which continued unabated." = our `appearances`/`earlycreed` "most still alive to be questioned" point. The great variety of times/places "makes it difficult to hold all the reports … to be legendary." James (the brother; not a disciple during the ministry) and the Twelve (institutional label; Judas already dead) handled as our essays do.

#### Ch 7 §1 (ii) cont. — 1 Cor 15:5–11 (witnesses, Paul "last of all") — pp. 323–325

Corroboration of our closed-class argument.

#### Ch 7 §1 (iii) 1 Cor 15:12–28 — pp. 326–332 (CORROBORATION: anastasis = bodily; firstfruits)
- ⭐ **The words *egeirō* / *anastasis* denote BODILY resurrection** — "in regular use to denote something specifically distinguished from non-bodily survival, namely, a return to bodily life. There is no evidence that these words were capable of denoting a non-bodily survival." So "granted the normal meaning of the words Paul uses, what he has in mind is bodily resurrection." (Supports our `appearances`/`emptytomb` anti-"spiritual-only" reading.)
- **15:12–19 reductio:** denying future resurrection → Jesus not raised → gospel null, **"your faith is futile, you are still in your sins" (15:17)**, the dead "perished," Christians "most to be pitied" (15:19).
- **15:20–28:** Jesus raised = **"firstfruits" (*aparchē*)** guaranteeing the rest (15:20–23); Adam/Christ ("as in Adam all die, so in the Messiah all shall be made alive," 15:21–22); Jesus "the truly human being who fully bears the divine image" (Gen 1:26–28; Ps 8; Ps 110); the last enemy death destroyed; God "all in all" (15:28). ⚠ **15:28 "the Son himself will be subjected"** = the Messiah's mediatorial kingdom completed, NOT subordinationism — keep our deity-fence; Wright reads it the orthodox way.

#### Ch 7 §1 (iv) 15:29–34 + (v) 15:35–49 intro — pp. 334–339 (setting up the *sōma pneumatikon* crux)
- **15:29–34 interlude:** baptism "on behalf of the dead" (15:29 — whatever it means, it assumes the dead ARE raised); Paul's sufferings ("I die every day"; "fought with wild animals at Ephesus"; "if the dead are not raised, let us eat and drink," Isa 22:13); resurrection = the ultimate grounding of Christian moral effort.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **(v) 1 Cor 15:35–49 opens — the key passage. Wright's method: Paul's account of the FUTURE resurrection body (of Christians) reveals what he believed about JESUS' resurrection body.** The question (15:35): "How are the dead raised? With what kind of body?"; the climax (15:49): "we shall bear the image of the man from heaven" (the final Adam, the Messiah).
- The whole passage is built on **Genesis 1–2 / new creation**: the "sowing" analogy (seed → plant, 15:36–38); the varieties of flesh/bodies (15:39–41); and ⭐ ***pneuma* = the life-giving creator-wind/Spirit** (Gen 1:2) — the key to *sōma pneumatikon*. "the recreation of humankind through the life-giving activity of the final Adam … a deliberate and careful theology of new Genesis, of creation renewed." (Core translation argument — *sōma psychikon* vs *sōma pneumatikon* — on the next pages.)

#### Ch 7 §1 (v) 1 Cor 15:35–49 CORE — pp. 340–346 (⭐⭐⭐ THE *sōma pneumatikon* crux — definitive source for our `appearances` reading)
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Wright's three crucial points (p. 341):** (1) Paul argues for a **BODILY** resurrection — and the "major misreading" is to take *sōma pneumatikon* (15:44–46) as "a spiritual, i.e. non-physical, body … composed of glorious light rather than anything physical"; (2) but a bodily resurrection **very different from mere resuscitation** (the seed → plant: continuity + transformation); (3) the Messiah as the final Adam is the **model** AND the **means** (the power is the **Spirit**). "These three points tell us a good deal about what Paul thought had happened to Jesus."
- **The seed/plant (15:36–41):** continuity + discontinuity; the present body is "naked," not yet "clothed" (links to 2 Cor 5:3–4 + the nakedness of Gen 2); the new body is a **gift** (resurrection is a work of grace). ⭐ **"glory" (*doxa*) ≠ luminosity** — it means honour/dignity (15:40b, 43); "Paul does NOT think of resurrection as 'becoming a star'" / astral immortality (he's not buying the *Timaeus* cosmology).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The decisive move:** *sōma psychikon* = a body animated by the *psychē* (soul/ordinary human life); *sōma pneumatikon* = a body animated by the *pneuma* (**the Spirit**). "Precisely because the soul is NOT, for him, the immortal fiery substance it is for Plato, Paul sees that the solution to the human plight is to **REPLACE the 'soul' as the animating principle of the body with the Spirit**." The contrast is about **what ANIMATES/empowers the body, not what it is MADE OF.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The four contrasts (15:42–44):** sown corruptible / raised incorruptible; sown in dishonour / raised in glory; sown in weakness / raised in power; **sown a *sōma psychikon* / raised a *sōma pneumatikon*.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **"Plato's ugly ditch" — the mistranslation Wright attacks (THE key lead for our `appearances` essay):** the KJV "natural body / spiritual body," and especially the **NRSV "physical body / spiritual body"** (and NEB "animal body," REB following RSV) make readers "assume Paul is describing the new resurrection body as … **non-physical — something you could not touch, could not see with ordinary eyesight, something which, if raised to life, would leave NO EMPTY TOMB behind it.**" Wright: this is a translation error; the *-ikos* adjectives denote the **animating power**, not the material. → **Our `appearances` essay already makes this point** ("*sōma pneumatikon* = a body animated by the Spirit … not a disembodied apparition") but hedges that "the precise force is debated." ⭐ **Consider citing Wright (RSG pp. 340–356) as the source-of-record, and note the NRSV mistranslation explicitly** (strengthens the anti-Carrier case; weigh in cross-check — our balanced hedge may be kept, but Wright is the anchor).

#### Ch 7 §1 (v) 1 Cor 15:35–49 — the *-ikos* grammatical argument (completes the crux) — pp. 347–354 (⭐⭐⭐ P3 backlog candidate for `appearances`)
- ⭐⭐⭐ **THE decisive grammatical point:** adjectives in **-ikos** denote an **ethical/functional** meaning — "what something is **animated/driven BY**, NOT what it is composed OF." Wright's analogy: "**the difference between a ship made of steel or wood** (composition) **and a ship driven by steam or wind**" (animation). Classical usage: Aristotle's *hysterai pneumatikai* (wombs "swollen with air"), Vitruvius's *pneumatikon organon* (a machine "moved by wind"). So ***sōma pneumatikon* = a body animated/driven by the Spirit (*pneuma*), NOT a body made of "spirit"/non-physical stuff.** (The Living Bible got it right: "when it is sown it embodies the soul, when it is raised it embodies the spirit.")
- ⭐⭐ **The translations are BACKWARDS:** if a first-century reader met *psychikos* in a physical/non-physical contrast, *psychikos* (soul-ish) would be the **non-physical** side and whatever it was contrasted with (*pneumatikos*) "would be seen as **more firmly bodily, more substantial**." So the resurrection body is MORE real/substantial, not less. Confirmed by 1 Cor 2–3 (*pneumatikos*/*psychikos*/*sarkikos* = Spirit-indwelt / ordinary human / corruptible — never physical-vs-nonphysical) and **Gen 2:7** (Adam a *psychē zōsa*; 15:45 "the last Adam a life-giving *pneuma*").
- ⭐⭐ **15:45–49 (Adam/Christ):** the second man "from heaven" (15:47) — but "the point is NOT that the new humanity will exist in a place called 'heaven' … it will ORIGINATE there … and TRANSFORM the life of those presently on earth. The whole argument runs in the opposite direction to Philo and all Platonism — not to escape earth for heaven, but to let the 'heavenly' life change the earthly reality." 15:48 "the heavenly ones" ≠ astral/"pneumatic bodies." (Rom 8:29; Col 3:10; Gen 1:26–28 image.)
- ⭐⭐⭐ **ACTIONABLE (P3) for `appearances.html`:** our essay already reads *sōma pneumatikon* as "a body animated by the Spirit … not a disembodied apparition" but hedges "the precise force is debated." **Wright (RSG pp. 340–356) is the definitive anchor** for the *-ikos* = "animated-by-not-made-of" argument + the point that the NRSV/RSV "physical body / spiritual body" rendering is **misleading (even backwards)**. Consider adding Wright's ship analogy + a note on the mistranslation to sharpen the anti-Carrier section. ⚠ Keep our balanced hedge where the "refined-substance" minority reading is noted — but Wright shows even that is secondary to the "animated-by" sense. Weigh in cross-check.

#### Ch 7 §1 (vi)–(vii) 1 Cor 15:50–58 + Conclusion — pp. 355–358 (⭐⭐ corroboration: Jesus' body transformed, not abandoned)

The Hebraic parallel "nor can corruption inherit incorruption" (15:50b) glosses it: "flesh and blood" = **ordinary, corruptible, decaying human existence** (the present physical humanity subject to death), NOT "physical humanity" in the modern sense. #### Ch 7 §2 (iii)–(iv) 2 Cor 4:16–5:10 — pp. 361–367 (⭐⭐⭐ definitive refutation of Carrier's 2 Cor 5 reading; confirms our `appearances` anti-Carrier section)

- ⭐⭐⭐ **2 Cor 5:1 "a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal, in the heavens" does NOT mean the soul goes to a heavenly home (Carrier's reading) — it is the FUTURE RESURRECTION BODY, currently "kept safely in store" in heaven, to be brought FROM heaven to earth** (5:2 "our dwelling from heaven"). The future body … is at present 'in the heavens' … **but it will not stay there**."

The OPPOSITE of escaping the body. = **our `appearances` essay's exact point** ("not that we would be unclothed, but that we would be further clothed … what is mortal is swallowed up by life, not shed and left in a tomb"). **Wright (RSG pp. 364–368) is the source-of-record for our anti-Carrier handling of 2 Cor 5.**

### ⭐⭐ Chapter Eighteen: Easter and History — pp. 685–717 mined 2026-10-27 (CHAPTER COMPLETE; the cumulative historical argument — source-of-record for `minimalfacts`/`emptytomb`/`appearances`; ⭐ important REGISTER refinement)

- ⭐⭐⭐ **§5 The Historical Challenge + the REGISTER REFINEMENT (pp. 708–717) — important for our bounded register.** Wright frames the whole case as **"inference to the best explanation"** (abduction): "the question… is whether the explanation… that Jesus really was risen from the dead, 'explains the aggregate' of the evidence better than these sophisticated scepticisms.
