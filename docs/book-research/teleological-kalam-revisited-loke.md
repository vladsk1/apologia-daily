## The book's spine
Loke's aim is to strengthen both arguments against the newest objections (Ratzsch & Koperski 2019 on
unconsidered alternatives; Sober 2019 on the prior for design; Linford 2020 on bounce cosmologies and the
direction of causation) and then **fuse** them into one "KCA-TA". Two distinctive engines:
1. **A modus tollens argument for the causal principle (ch. 3).** If the first state really began uncaused,
   then nothing would select *it* rather than anything else to begin uncaused, so uncaused beginnings would be
   happening around us; they are not; so the first state did not begin uncaused. He defends a **contingent**
   causal principle ("has a cause", not "must have"), which disarms Almeida's appeal to chaotic possible worlds.
2. **An "exhaustive" elimination of design's rivals (chs. 4, 7):** uncaused / chance / regularity /
   chance-plus-regularity / design, each ruled out as an ultimate explanation, leaving design.

## Chapter map (in our own words) with primary-source leads
- **Ch. 1, Introducing the quest (pp. 1–36).** Review of the debate (Philoponus frag. 132; al-Kindi;
  al-Ghazali; Craig); Q. Smith 2007 p. 183 on how much the kalam is discussed. A long defence of a priori
  metaphysical knowledge against scientism and verificationism (Feser 2017; Stenmark 2003; Ellis 2007 §8.1;
  Carnap 1950; Ladyman et al. 2007). §1.8 previews the 14-step fused argument. Generic and often overstated
  ("100% epistemic certainty", p. 18); not needed for our essays.
- **Ch. 2, Causation and laws of nature (pp. 37–70).**
  - The key terms: "whatever" covers events and arrangements; "begins to exist" means a finite, edged
    temporal extent, so it works on dynamic or static time.
  - **Necessary vs sufficient conditions:** a quantum event or a free act lacks a *sufficient* condition, not a
    *necessary* one, so undetermined does not mean uncaused (Bussey 2013 p. 20).
  - **Efficient vs material cause:** creation from nothing denies a material cause, not an efficient one
    (Craig & Sinclair 2009 pp. 188–9).
  - ⭐ **Hume's 1754 letter**, in which he wrote that he never asserted anything might arise without a cause,
    only that our certainty of it comes from neither intuition nor demonstration (*Letters of David Hume*, ed.
    J. Y. T. Greig, vol. 1, p. 187). Also Reid 1983 p. 330.
  - The vacuum is not nothing; Ellis 2007 §2.7 (a non-theist cosmologist: "from nothing" models presuppose
    quantum field theory); a zero-energy universe still needs an efficient cause.
  - Loke leans on Bohmian determinism in places; we should not.
- **Ch. 3, Arguments for the causal principle (pp. 71–139).** The modus tollens argument (Edwards 1830 p. 53;
  Prior 1968 p. 65; Craig & Sinclair 2009 p. 186). The reply to **Oppy's "initial state of reality is the only
  thing that begins uncaused"** (Oppy 2010 and 2015, *Faith and Philosophy* 27 and 32; Oppy 2013a/b), built on
  three facts: (I) nothing causally prior picks the first state out; (II) whatever makes it different exists
  only once it exists; (III) present circumstances allow other things to begin. He works through brute fact
  (special pleading; radioactive decay is disanalogous because the atoms already exist), abstract objects
  (laws describe, they do not act; Feser 2013 p. 254), a property of the thing itself (cannot ground its own
  beginning; Oderberg 2002 p. 330), "laws prevent it now" (energy-conserving processes are allowed yet never
  start uncaused), could vs would, libertarian freedom (an agent's act is not an uncaused event), the
  directionality objection (Linford 2020), and the black-swan charge. ⚠ Loke's rhetoric ("Oppy's theory
  entails a contradiction") is far beyond ours.
- **Ch. 4, Fine-tuning and order (pp. 141–194).** Fine-tuning data (Collins 2009; Lewis & Barnes 2016;
  Penrose via Holder 2004); ⭐ **Fred Adams 2019** (*Physics Reports* 807: 1–111, arXiv:1902.03928) widens the
  life-permitting ranges but concedes "unnaturally small" ratios are still required (pp. 141–2 of the arXiv
  version); Stenger answered with Barnes 2012 §4.2. Order and the applicability of mathematics (Wigner,
  Steiner). The "logically exhaustive" categories. Chance (Leslie's firing squad vs Sober 2019 p. 73);
  normalization (McGrew, McGrew & Vestrup 2001; **Lewis & Barnes 2016 p. 286**: infinity problems would
  paralyse probabilistic reasoning across physics; **pp. 287–8**: with good data the prior matters little;
  Ratzsch & Koperski on truncation); the multiverse (Boltzmann brains; **inflation's own fine-tuning: *Q*
  must lie between 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁴, Lewis & Barnes 2016 pp. 172–3 citing Turok**; Goff on the inverse
  gambler's fallacy). Regularity (deeper laws push the question back); combination (Smolin's cosmological
  natural selection via Penrose 2004).
- **Ch. 5, Arguments for a first cause (pp. 195–245).** A survey of cosmologies without a beginning; ⭐
  **Linford 2020's entropy-reversal bounce** answered causally (the first event on each side still needs a
  cause), not with physics; Wall 2013 GSL (⚠ stated without Wall's spatial-infiniteness condition). Five
  independent arguments against an infinite regress (actual infinites; traversal; dependence regress; Grim
  Reaper; Methuselah's diary). His traversal argument ("finite plus finite stays finite", with an induction
  "proof") and, ⭐ the honest disclosure that **Craig & Sinclair 2009 p. 124 concede the successive-addition
  argument presupposes a dynamic (A-theory) view of time**. Causal loops (Gott & Li 1998 conflict with the GSL).
  Replies to Morriston 2013/2021 and Malpass 2021 (*Mind*).
- **Ch. 6, What the first cause is (pp. 247–296).** Uncaused, beginningless, initially changeless,
  transcendent, immaterial, free, powerful. ⭐ **Craig & Sinclair 2009 p. 192 n. 100: the kalam "strictly
  demonstrates only that metric time had a beginning"** (the cause might exist changelessly in undifferentiated
  time). ⭐ The **impersonal-cause dilemma** against Oppy's indeterministic initial state (Oppy 2009 n. 8;
  Pearce 2017): a necessitating cause gives a beginningless effect; an unstable impersonal cause could not last
  beginninglessly; a stable one would never act. Imaginary time as a calculational device (Isham 1997; Vilenkin
  2006 p. 182; Barrow 1991; Hawking 1997 p. 169, "positivist"). 🔴 §6.2/§6.4.4–6.4.5: Craig's hybrid view and the
  rejection of classical theism (do not port).
- **Ch. 7, Ultimate design (pp. 297–331).** Against the "uncaused" option (Oppy, Lange, Ellis, Mumford);
  "who designed the designer?" (only what begins needs a cause); Oppy's brute free choice; Hume's eleven
  objections triaged (via Topham 2010); Halvorson 2018's theological critique; Sober on priors; Grünbaum on
  explanatory latitude. ⚠ The 99.6% figure (p. 318) and "avoids priors entirely" are overclaims.
- **Ch. 8, Ultimate designer (pp. 333–352).** God-of-the-gaps reply (from reasons, not ignorance); stated
  limits (one cause or many; a first sustaining cause; Goff 2019's cosmopsychism); the argument does not reach
  moral perfection or the Trinity.

## Is the source better than our essays anywhere? Where, and how?
**Yes, in five specific places, all on the kalam side; on fine-tuning it mostly supplies better citations.**
1. ⭐ **Premise 1 against Oppy.** Our essay defends "whatever begins to exist has a cause" with the ex nihilo
   intuition and the "why not bicycles?" question, and answers Oppy only with "a personal cause is a better
   stopping point". It never meets Oppy's actual move, that **only the first state** began uncaused. Loke turns
   the rhetorical question into an argument that meets that restriction head-on, and a lay reader can follow it.
2. ⭐ **Hume's own 1754 disavowal** is a stronger witness for Premise 1 than anything we cite, and the letter is
   public domain.
3. ⭐ **The scope of "timeless".** Our essay says the cause "must be spaceless, timeless", yet elsewhere says
   the kalam does not settle how God stands to time. Craig & Sinclair's own n. 100 says the argument strictly
   reaches only a beginning of metric time. Our wording overstates what the argument shows and sits uneasily
   with our own neutrality. (`bigbang.html`'s "not spatial or temporal in the ordinary sense" is the safer form.)
4. **Successive addition needs dynamic time.** Craig & Sinclair concede it (p. 124); our essay asserts the past
   "is built up successively" without saying the argument depends on one view of time.
5. **Cause to personal Creator.** The impersonal-cause dilemma answers Oppy's indeterministic initial state more
   rigorously than our "better stopping point", and it does not need Craig's view of God and time.

On fine-tuning Loke is **not** better on the measure problem (same Planck-cutoff and truncation moves, then he
reintroduces the problem via an indifference measure over law-space), and his Hume replies mostly lean on his
own exclusion and kalam machinery. What he adds is **better citations** (Adams 2019; Lewis & Barnes pp. 172–3,
286–8) and a **Hume triage** we lack entirely.
