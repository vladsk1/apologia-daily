## Thesis / spine (our words)
Messiahship is the **root of all early christology**, not a minor or late title. Every other Gospel title
(Son of God, Son of David, Son of Man, Lord, King of Israel) works as an *aspect of* the messianic role. Each
evangelist both **continues** Jewish messianic hope and **redefines** it around the cross and resurrection:
- **Mark:** the crucified Messiah; an apology for the cross.
- **Matthew:** the Davidic Messiah who ends the exile and gathers the nations.
- **Luke–Acts:** the prophetic, Spirit-anointed Messiah of the Isaianic new exodus; Messiah and Lord.
- **John:** the "elusive" Messiah, where the messianic question opens onto the divine identity.

The historical ground under all four: **Jesus himself acted out a messianic claim**. The early church did not
invent it after Easter, because a resurrection by itself would not make anyone a Messiah.

## ⭐ STANDING OUTPUT — Is the source better than our essays anywhere? Where, and how?
**Yes, in three places; elsewhere ours is as good or better.**
1. **We have no case that Jesus claimed to be the Messiah, and Bird does.** `jesus_claims.html` argues the
   *deity* question and lets Ehrman's "perhaps Israel's messiah" stand unexamined. No essay argues the prior
   historical question. Bird's six strands are listed under BIRD-1 below. Isaiah 61 returns **zero hits**
   site-wide; Wrede, Psalms of Solomon, 1QS 9:11 and Acts 11:26 also return zero.
2. **His "a resurrection does not make a Messiah" argument is sharper than ours.** `crucified-messiah.html` says
   the scandal explains why no one would invent the cross, but not why anyone would celebrate it, and that this
   needs the resurrection. Bird adds the missing step. Even granting a resurrection, nothing in Judaism turns a
   raised man into a Messiah: Herod thought John the Baptist had been raised, and nobody called John the Christ
   (Mark 6:14–16). So the title must have been there before Easter. Allison makes the same point.
3. **The Synoptic "Lord".** `titles.html`'s Kyrios section rests entirely on Paul (Rom 10:13; 1 Cor 8:6; 16:22;
   Phil 2). Bird adds two Synoptic uses: Luke's double title "Christ the Lord" (Luke 2:11; C. Kavin Rowe's
   narrative christology), and Mark placing Jesus' Shema citation (12:29) immediately before the "David calls
   him Lord" riddle (12:35–37).

**Where ours is better (do not "improve" backwards):** Son of Man / Daniel 7 (`titles.html` separates
Daniel's clouds from Ps 110's seat; Bird blurs them); the *aposynagōgos* question (`jewishness.html` engages
Kimelman, Reinhartz, Bernier and de Boer, where Bird has Klink alone); Isaiah 53 and the Targum
(`messianic-prophecy.html` is more careful); the critics' currency (ours engage Ehrman 2014 and Kirk 2016; Bird
predates both); Stoner (ours retires the statistical argument; Bird does not touch it).

**Error in ours:** none found.

## Hazards / fences carried by this note
- **Rom 9:5** — Bird reads the doxology as "the Messiah, who is God over all". The punctuation is genuinely
  disputed, so never cite Bird as settling it.
- **Acts 2:36 / Rom 1:3–4** — adoptionist-sounding; keep the enthronement fence.
- **1 Cor 15:24–28** (the Son hands the kingdom to the Father) — Bird uses it for a "messianic interregnum". It
  is a taxis/subordination proof-text; port nothing from it without the EFS/eternal-generation fencing.
- **"Christ" ≠ "Messiah" in every Pauline use** — Bird is maximalist (titular nearly everywhere). Others (Kramer,
  Miller) disagree; state it as debated.
- **The church as "true/new Israel"** — Bird's conclusion touches supersessionism. Denominationally and
  Jewish-relations sensitive; **do not import** his ecclesiology.
- **Colwell's rule** appears here only about John 20:31's word order (Carson vs Wallace). It has nothing to do
  with the retired John 1:1 misapplication; do not conflate them.
- ⚠ **Matt 16:18 ("on this rock")** — Bird reads Peter himself as the rock and treats "the confession only"
  as Protestant anxiety about the papacy. That adjudicates the **papacy** question, one of the six intra-Christian
  disputes the denominational-neutrality guardrail names. **Do not port his reading of the rock.** If the verse
  is ever used, use it only for the confession (16:16) and leave the rock's referent unstated or named as
  disputed.
- **Isa 7:14** — Bird reads it typologically; ours ("dual fulfilment, contested") already says this.
