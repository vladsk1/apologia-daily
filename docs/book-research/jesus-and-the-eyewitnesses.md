## Thesis (our words)
The Gospels should be read as **testimony** — a category Bauckham argues is both a reputable
historiographic one (following **Samuel Byrskog**) and a theological one. Against the form-critical
picture of anonymous community transmission over decades, he argues that the Jesus tradition stayed
**personally attached to named eyewitnesses** from the events to the writing of the Gospels. His stated
aim is to make that personal link, if not "historically undeniable," then "historically very probable."
In practice: **Mark stands very close to Peter**, and **John's Gospel was written by a disciple of Jesus.**

## Argument map (chapter-level → the primaries to VERIFY)
1. **Papias as the key witness.** His preference for "the living voice" over books reflects not a general
   preference for orality but the widespread ancient historiographic conviction that **living eyewitnesses
   take priority**. → *Primaries:* **Eusebius, *Hist. eccl.* 3.39** (esp. 3.39.3–4 and 3.39.15). *Already
   live on our site* — `library/eyewitnesses.html` cites 3.39.15 with the Loeb (Kirsopp Lake) rendering.
2. **⭐ Names and onomastics.** The pattern of who is named and who is not reflects real recollection (the
   women at the cross; Simon of Cyrene **and his two sons**; Zacchaeus; Bartimaeus), and the Gospel names
   fit the Palestinian-Jewish name distribution strikingly well. → *Primaries:* **Tal Ilan, *Lexicon of
   Jewish Names in Late Antiquity, Part I: Palestine 330 BCE–200 CE*** (Mohr Siebeck, 2002); **Mark
   15:21; 10:46; Luke 19:1–10**. Bauckham's own table of the 99 most popular names is at chs. 3–4
   (esp. pp. 67–92). *Already live and thoroughly worked on our site.*
3. **The Twelve as "an official body of eyewitnesses"** — with the variations among the apostolic lists
   reconciled, the one hard case being **Levi (Mark 2:14) → Matthew (Matt 9:9)**.
4. **⭐ The *inclusio* of eyewitness testimony.** Mark names **Peter first (1:16) and last (16:7)**;
   Luke and John do comparable things — a recognised ancient literary device signalling the author's
   principal eyewitness source. → *Parallels adduced:* **Lucian** and **Porphyry**. *Already live* —
   `library/eyewitnesses.html` carries the device with the Lucian/Porphyry parallels.
5. **Mark from Peter.** Reviving **C. H. Turner**'s observation that Mark alternates third-person plural
   and singular ("they went to Gethsemane… and he said") as an echo of a first-person Petrine narrative
   ("we did… Jesus did"). → *Primary:* **Mark 14:32** and the wider pattern; Turner's *JTS* studies.
6. **Protective anonymity.** Some unnamed figures may be unnamed deliberately, to protect people still
   living or endangered — e.g. the woman who anoints Jesus, the young man who flees naked (Lazarus?), the
   man whose ear is cut off. ⚠ **Bauckham's own most speculative move**; Wenham's review flags the
   book's tendency to "attractive but uncertain readings." **Present as a suggestion, never as a finding.**
7. **Papias again on Mark and Matthew** — Mark as Peter's *translator*; "not in order" as a comparison
   with John; our Matthew as one rendering of an original Aramaic Matthew.
8. **Oral tradition vs. oral history.** Trenchant criticism of **form criticism**; a sympathetic but firm
   critique of **Birger Gerhardsson** and the Scandinavian school, and of **Kenneth Bailey** and **James
   D. G. Dunn** — the charge being that they underrate the community role of *official eyewitnesses*.
   Supporting: **Riesner** on Jesus as teacher; **Stanton** and **Lemcio** on the Evangelists distinguishing
   Jesus' time from their own; Paul's own appeal to received tradition. Also questions the assumption that
   the Gospels were originally anonymous (**Luke 1:1–4**; John's Beloved Disciple references).
9. **The psychology of memory** — modern memory research brought to bear against the form-critical
   assumptions of Nineham and others.
10. **John (roughly the final hundred pages).** John 21 as an integral epilogue; the "we" of **John 21:24**
    as a "we" of authoritative testimony rather than a Johannine school; **Peter as pastor, John as
    witness** (no rivalry), with **21:23** pointing to the Beloved Disciple's testimony continuing.
    ⚠⚠ **The contested conclusion:** Bauckham identifies the Beloved Disciple as **John the Elder — an
    original disciple but NOT one of the Twelve, and not John son of Zebedee.** Wenham, reviewing
    favourably overall, presses exactly here: on that view, **where has John son of Zebedee gone?** —
    given that the Synoptics are unanimous about his place in the inner three and **Paul names him a
    pillar (Gal 2:9)**. **This is a genuine, unresolved dispute; never present Bauckham's identification
    as the answer.**
11. **Historical method and testimony.** A defence of trusting testimony as a proper historical stance —
    "though not credulity" — with a comparison between Holocaust testimony and the Gospels as
    "uniquely unique events" (Ricoeur), closing on testimony as the category where "history and theology
    meet" (p. 508).
    ⚠ **The Holocaust comparison is a serious rhetorical risk in compressed formats.** It is a
    *philosophy-of-testimony* analogy in a 500-page academic book. It must **never** be used in a reel, a
    card, an answer opening, or a social post. If it is used at all, it belongs only inside a full essay
    treatment, handled with the gravity the subject demands.

## Guardrail fit
- **⚠ Universalism — see the fence above.** Historiography only.
- **⚠ Contested, not consensus.** Wenham's summary is the right calibration: "many fascinating and largely
  persuasive observations," but "sometimes the judgments are fine ones, involving attractive but uncertain
  readings." Bauckham's thesis has serious critics (Tuckett's *RBL* review is already cited on our site;
  Gregor & Blais on the statistics). **Our `library/names.html` already carries the critics on the page —
  that is the standard.**
- **⚠ Eyewitness testimony ≠ inerrancy ≠ miracle.** Establishing that the Gospels rest on named eyewitness
  sources bounds *how* the tradition travelled; it does not by itself establish that any reported event
  occurred. `library/eyewitnesses.html` and `library/coincidences.html` both state this bound already.
- **⚠ Do not import the John-the-Elder identification** into our voice. Where authorship comes up, our
  essays should keep the traditional and the Bauckham options both on the table, as live scholarship.
