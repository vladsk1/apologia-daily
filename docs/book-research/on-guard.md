## 🔴 HEADLINE FINDING (the one thing to carry forward)

**Chapter 8 (p. 262) runs a claim we have formally RETIRED.** In the "Son of Man" section On Guard
calls the Daniel 7:13–14 figure **"the divine-human figure,"** quotes Dan 7:14 ("all peoples…
should **serve** him"), and states that the dominion/glory given to that figure **"belong properly
to God alone"** — and the chapter's own summary outline repeats it ("Reference to the *divine-human
figure* of Daniel 7"). It also cites the *Similitudes of Enoch* (1 Enoch 48:3–6) for a "preexistent
Son of Man."

This trips **BOTH** registered retired claims — `daniel7-figure-is-divine` **and**
`pelach-only-to-God` (confirmed present in `tools/retired-claims.json`) — the exact reading that
`library/titles.html` took **five gate rounds** to remove. **On Guard is now the THIRD popular
apologetics book found carrying this** (after Geisler & Turek, and Strobel's *Is God Real?* ch. 4
p. 72). This confirms `CLAUDE.md`'s standing warning: **it is a recurring hazard in popular
deity-of-Christ writing — grep every new deity source for it.**

➡ **DO NOT MINE ch. 8's Son-of-Man / Daniel-7 material.** Do not use it to support a "Daniel 7 Son
of Man is himself divine / worshipped" argument, and do not let it seep back into `titles.html`,
`jesus_as_god_nt.html`, the mastery pages, flashcards, quizzes, or pocket cards. Our essays' careful
handling is the correct one. (Craig's *other* deity arguments in the chapter — Mal 3:1/Isa 40:3 as
the coming YHWH, the tenants parable, Matt 11:27, the trial + Ps 110:1 — are fine and corroborate us;
it is specifically the Daniel-7-figure-is-divine move that is our retired claim.)
- ⚠ Related, softer flag: the *Similitudes of Enoch* "preexistent divine Messiah" material (1 Enoch

  the ~1st-c.-AD, possibly post-Christian section of 1 Enoch, not the 2nd-c.-BC part — Craig's own

  the same care.

## Chapter-by-chapter map (argument shape + primary-source leads)

**Ch. 1 — What Is Apologetics?** Methodology, low mining value. Apologetics = *apologia* / defense
(1 Peter 3:15); gentleness and respect; "an argument is a series of statements leading to a
conclusion, not a quarrel." "Apologetics is biblical": Jesus/apostles appealed to miracles +
fulfilled prophecy + resurrection (Luke 24:25–27; John 14:11; Acts 2:22, 25–31, 32; Eph 4:15).
*Leads:* none needing chase.

**Ch. 2 — What Difference Does It Make if God Exists?** The absurdity of life without God (his
signature existential chapter). Structure: without God **and** immortality there is no ultimate
**meaning**, **value**, or **purpose**. Beats + leads: Andrei Grib's "proof by the opposite"
(*reductio ad absurdum*); **Sartre, Camus** (life is absurd without God); the heat-death of the
universe → **Bertrand Russell**, "A Free Man's Worship" (the "universe in ruins"); **Stephen Crane**,
"A Man Said to the Universe"; **Dostoevsky**, *Brothers Karamazov* ("if there is no immortality…
all things are permitted"); **Richard Wurmbrand** (Communist torturers' nihilism); **Stewart C.
Easton** (historian); **H. G. Wells**, *The Time Machine*; Ecclesiastes 2:11; 3:19–20; **Loyal D.
Rue** (the "Noble Lie," his 1991 AAAS address). Closes with the "My Story" testimony.

**Ch. 3 — Why Does Anything at All Exist?** The **Leibnizian contingency argument** (→ `leibniz.html`).
Formal argument: (1) everything that exists has an explanation of its existence, either in the
necessity of its own nature or in an external cause; (2) if the universe has an explanation, that
explanation is God; (3) the universe exists; ∴ (4,5) the explanation of the universe is God. Defenses/
objections handled: the "then God needs an explanation too" objection → necessary vs. contingent
being (God's explanation in the necessity of his own nature); **the Taxicab Fallacy (Schopenhauer)**;
the ball-in-the-woods illustration (**Richard Taylor**, size doesn't remove the demand for an
explanation); the "abstract object or unembodied Mind" move → an unembodied Mind = God; the
different-particles contingency argument (a different collection of quarks = a different universe);
the atheist-necessity alternative rebutted (**Adolf Grünbaum**). *Leads:* Leibniz ("why is there
something rather than nothing?"), Richard Taylor (*Metaphysics*), Schopenhauer, Grünbaum.

**Ch. 4 — Why Did the Universe Begin?** The **kalam** (→ `kalam.html`, `bigbang.html`). (1) whatever
**begins to exist** has a cause [note: Craig states it correctly, our guardrail]; (2) the universe
began to exist; (3) ∴ the universe has a cause. Pedigree margin: **John Philoponus of Alexandria** →
Islamic *kalām* → **St. Bonaventure**. Premise 1: "something cannot come from nothing" (the
quantum-vacuum "is not nothing" reply); the "what caused God?" reply (**al-Ghazali**: God is eternal/
uncaused). Premise 2 — four arguments: (a) impossibility of an actual infinite (al-Ghazali; **Georg
Cantor**; **Hilbert's Hotel**; reality-vs-fiction reply); (b) impossibility of forming an actual
infinite by successive addition (the counting-down-from-eternity illustration); (c) the expansion of
the universe (**Hubble**; the **Friedmann–Lemaître** model; balloon illustration; **Augustine** —
God created "simultaneously with time," *City of God* XI); (d) thermodynamics (**Boltzmann**'s
fluctuation hypothesis rejected; heat death; the oscillating-model rebuttal; the Preskill–**Hawking**
2004 information-paradox bet). Capstone: the **BGV theorem** (Borde–Guth–Vilenkin 2003) + **Vilenkin**,
*Many Worlds in One* (2006, "no escape… face the problem of a cosmic beginning"). Self-caused-universe
rebutted (**Daniel Dennett**'s "ultimate boot-strapping trick"). The First Cause: uncaused, spaceless,
timeless, immaterial, enormously powerful, **personal** (al-Ghazali: a free agent explains a temporal
effect from a timeless cause). *Leads:* al-Ghazali, Cantor, Hilbert, Friedmann/Lemaître, Hubble,
Augustine (*City of God* XI.4–6, **PD**), Boltzmann, Preskill–Hawking, BGV/Vilenkin, Dennett.

**Ch. 5 — Why Is the Universe Fine-tuned for Life?** (→ `finetuning.html`, `cosmic.html`). History:
**Plato** (*Laws* 10.893b–899c; 12.966e), **Aristotle** (the lost *On Philosophy*). The constants +
initial-condition quantities; the data ("fine-tuned **does NOT mean designed**" — margin, our exact
guardrail): weak force 1 in 10^100; cosmological constant 1 in 10^120; **Roger Penrose**'s low-entropy
odds 1 in 10^10^123 (*The Road to Reality*, 2005, pp. 762–5). Three-step argument (necessity / chance
/ design). Necessity rejected (constants not fixed by law; a TOE wouldn't explain them). Chance:
**John Barrow**'s red-dot illustration; the lottery/black-ball rebuttal; the multiverse ("Many Worlds
hypothesis" → it would itself need fine-tuning → the multiverse is finite [BGV] / Boltzmann brains —
confirmed by the chapter's argument-map, though its multiverse section is **thin** vs. ours). The
anthropic-principle objection rebutted (**John Leslie**'s firing-squad + lone-fly illustrations,
*Universes*, 1989). Dawkins' "who designed the Designer?" rebutted (a divine mind is simple —
*The God Delusion*, 2006, pp. 157–8; **Quentin Smith** cited from *Theism, Atheism, and Big Bang
Cosmology*, Oxford 1993, p. 322). *Leads:* Plato, Aristotle, Penrose, Barrow, Leslie, Dawkins, Smith.

**Ch. 6 — Can We Be Good Without God?** (→ `moral.html`). Leads with **William Sorley**, *Moral
Values and the Idea of God* (1918, **PD**). Formal argument: (1) if God does not exist, objective
moral values and duties do not exist; (2) they do exist; ∴ (3) God exists. The **values (good/bad)
vs. duties (right/wrong)** distinction. Premise 1 defended vs. the "shopping list"/atheistic moral
realism. **The Euthyphro Dilemma** answered by the third horn (God wills the good because he *is*
good; his nature is the standard). Premise 2: the reality of moral experience. The
sociobiological/evolutionary debunking objection answered by the genetic fallacy (how ≠ whether) +
self-defeating naturalism. Romans 2:14–15 (law on the heart). *Leads (from the ch.-6 endnotes,
VERIFIED as printed):* **Charles Darwin**, *The Descent of Man*, 2nd ed. (1909), p. 100 (**PD**);
Craig–Kurtz debate ("Goodness without God is Good Enough," 2008); Craig & Sinnott-Armstrong, *God?*
(Oxford, 2003). ⚠ The chapter's closing line "we cannot truly be good without God" is exactly the
compression our guardrail forbids as a bald claim (see hazard table).

**Ch. 7 — What About Suffering?** (→ `evil.html`). Structure identical to ours: **Problem of Suffering
→ Intellectual (Logical / Evidential) + Emotional.** Concede the emotional difficulty first
(think vs. feel). **Logical version** (Mackie-style): the atheist's hidden premises (an all-powerful
God can create any world; an all-loving God prefers a world without suffering) are not *necessarily*
true — free will → worlds God can't actualize; Craig frames it explicitly as needing only to be
**logically possible**, i.e. a **defense (consistency), not a proof** [our guardrail — honored].
**Evidential version:** we're not in a position to judge probabilities (skeptical theism — chaos
theory / *Sliding Doors* illustrations); assess on total evidence; **Christian doctrines that raise
the probability of coexistence** (life's purpose is the knowledge of God not happiness; the prosperity
gospel fenced as a false gospel; eternal life beyond the grave, 2 Cor 4:16–18; 6:4–5; the knowledge
of God as an incommensurable good). **Emotional problem:** "the most important thing may be just to
be there as a loving friend and sympathetic listener" — **directly corroborates our PASTORAL-CARE
guardrail** (compassion/presence, not an argument, for someone in pain). Closes with the "Mabel"
story + a hymn [⚠ do not reproduce the lyrics — copyright]. *Leads:* Plantinga's Free Will Defense;
the standard evidential-problem literature (chase in *RF*).

**Ch. 8 — Who Was Jesus?** (→ `jesus_as_god_nt.html`, `titles.html`, `jesus_claims.html`,
`messianic-prophecy.html`, `canon.html`). Opens with **John Hick (ed.), *The Myth of God Incarnate***
(1977) and the "revolution in NT scholarship." Gospel reliability (5 reasons: real people/places vs.
urban legends; reliable Jewish oral transmission — "oral history not the telephone game"; eyewitness
restraints; proven track record — Luke as historian, Luke 1:1–4, the "we" passages). Apocryphal
gospels are 2nd-c.+ (→ corroborates `canon.html`, chronology-not-theology). Criteria of authenticity
(embarrassment, dissimilarity, multiple attestation) → establish, not deny. **Phil 2:5–7:** within
~20 years monotheistic Jews worshiped Jesus as God incarnate (early-high-Christology / Hurtado).
Jesus' self-understanding: **4Q521** ("Messianic Apocalypse") + the answer to the Baptist (Matt
11:2–6 // Luke 7:19–23, Q); triumphal entry (**Zech 9:9**; Mark 11); **Isaiah 9:6** ("Mighty God");
**Mal 3:1 + Isa 40:3** (John the Baptist as forerunner → the one prepared for is *YHWH*, and Jesus is
that coming Lord — a strong implicit-deity argument); the parable of the tenants (Mark 12 // *Gospel
of Thomas* 65) + **Matt 11:27 // Luke 10:22** (the "Johannine thunderbolt") for unique Sonship —
with a **careful** "Son of God" nuance (in a Jewish context it was not customarily a divine title;
4 Ezra 7:28–29's mortal messiah-son). The **Sanhedrin trial** (Mark 14:60–64): Messiah + Son of God
+ coming Son of Man **seated at God's right hand** (Ps 110:1) → blasphemy. 🔴 **The Son of Man /
Daniel 7 section is the RETIRED claim — see the headline above; do not mine it.** *Leads (chase the
exact scholar list in RF 3rd ed.):* Hick (1977), 4Q521, the oral-transmission scholarship
(Gerhardsson / Riesner / Bauckham), Ps 110:1, Zech 9:9, Mal 3:1, Isa 40:3, 9:6.

**Ch. 9 — Did Jesus Rise from the Dead?** (→ the whole resurrection tab). Opens with **Wolfhart
Pannenberg** (Craig's Munich doctoral supervisor) and **Pinchas Lapide** (Jewish scholar affirming
God raised Jesus — *The Resurrection of Jesus: A Jewish Perspective*, 1983). Two steps: the evidence,
then the best explanation. **Empty tomb:** burial by **Joseph of Arimathea** (Sanhedrist =
embarrassment; Acts 2:23, 36; 4:10); **John A. T. Robinson** ("one of the earliest and best-attested
facts about Jesus"); the **Jerusalem factor**; **the 1 Cor 15:3–5 four-line creed** (buried→raised
implies the empty tomb; Mark's passion source; Mark 15:42–47); **Mark's restraint vs. the *Gospel of
Peter*** legend (9:1–10:5 — the talking cross). **Appearances** (1 Cor 15:5–8): to Cephas, the
Twelve (Luke 24:36–42; John 20:19–20, physical demonstrations), the **500** (**C. H. Dodd**: "the
witnesses are there to be questioned"), James, all the apostles; the physical-body defense
(*psychikos*/*pneumatikos* = soul-led vs. Spirit-led, not material vs. ethereal, 1 Cor 15:44; 2:14–15).
**Origin of the disciples' belief:** the Jewish conception of resurrection (end-of-world, of *all* the
righteous, never an isolated individual; no messianic movement claimed their executed leader raised —
**N. T. Wright**) → they couldn't have invented it. **The three facts** (empty tomb, appearances,
origin of belief) = the **majority view of mainstream NT criticism** [calibrated language — "wide
majority," not "virtually all," matching our care]. **Best explanation** (**McCullagh**'s criteria):
resurrection over conspiracy / apparent death / hallucination; **Gerd Lüdemann**'s hallucination
hypothesis rebutted (no explanation of the empty tomb or origin of belief; the diversity of the
appearances "bursts the psychological casebooks"); assumption vs. resurrection vs. revivification
(2 Kings 2; John 11). *Leads:* Pannenberg, Lapide, Robinson, Dodd, Wright, Lüdemann, McCullagh, the
1 Cor 15 creed — **all already carried, and better, in our resurrection tab.**

**Ch. 10 — Is Jesus the Only Way to God?** (→ `religious.html`, `uniqueness.html`). Biblical basis
for salvation through Christ alone (Romans 1–3; Acts 4:12) — common to Catholics/Orthodox/Protestants,
within our neutrality bounds. The pluralist objection (**John Hick**; the "scandal of particularity").
The "particularism is arrogant/immoral" objection rebutted (the AIDS-vaccine analogy — truth is
independent of the holder's character; the genetic fallacy; the pluralist is equally "arrogant"). The
fate of the unevangelized / God's justice (Ezek 18:23, 32; 33:11 — "God will not send us to hell;
we send ourselves"; the lost are self-condemned); "Does the Punishment Fit the Crime?" (defends
eternal hell vs. the annihilationist/purgatorial objection). ⚠ **Denominational-neutrality hazard**
(see table): Craig's specific eschatological adjudications (eternal conscious hell vs. annihilationism)
and his broader Molinist framework touch the **end-times / predestination** disputes our guardrail
says not to settle. Present the problem and the range of orthodox Christian views; **do not adopt
Craig's specific position as the site's**.
