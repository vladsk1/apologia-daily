## Thesis (our words)
A comprehensive, deliberately *maximalist* case that the Old Testament's narratives fit the ancient Near
Eastern settings they claim, far better than a late invention could — argued from Egyptology and ANE
comparative material rather than from theology, and working **backwards** from the well-attested later
periods (the divided monarchy, where synchronisms with Assyrian and Babylonian records are secure) toward
the less-attested earlier ones.

## What we already use, and how (the certified bit)
`library/archaeology.html` carries **one** Kitchen argument, and carries it well: the claim that the
**covenant structure of Sinai and Deuteronomy matches the form of second-millennium Hittite treaties** —
historical prologue, stipulations, blessings and curses, provision for deposit of the text — a form that
had fallen out of use by the first millennium, so that a writer inventing the covenant in Josiah's day or
in exile would have reached for the wrong template (**ch. 6**). The essay labels it **"a real argument,
but… contested,"** and attaches **J. G. McConville** (*Deuteronomy*, AOTC [IVP, 2002]) urging caution about
how tightly the treaty parallels can date a text. **That pairing — the argument plus its named critic — is
the model for every future Kitchen pull.**

## ⚠ Guardrails (these are the point of this note)
1. **⚠⚠ Kitchen is an avowed maximalist, and the brief flags this explicitly. Keep every verdict
   bounded.** Archaeology corroborates the **setting**, not the **message** — the exact sibling of the
   manuscripts rule (preservation, not truth) and the Acts rule (setting corroborated, not doctrine
   proven). `library/archaeology.html` states this plainly in its conclusion; do not let a Kitchen pull
   erode it.
2. **⚠⚠ Never cite Kitchen alone on a contested point.** Every one of his major theses has serious
   opposition (Finkelstein on the tenth century; Thompson/Van Seters on the patriarchs; McConville on the
   treaty dating). The site's existing practice — argument + named critic, in the same breath — is
   non-negotiable for this author.
3. **⚠⚠ The pre-1974 patriarchal-customs material is a trap, not a gap.** See the article note's
   **deliberate non-recommendation**: the Nuzi/patriarchal-parallels argument was overturned by
   **Thompson (1974)** and **Van Seters (1975)**, and must not be imported without working through that
   literature and Kitchen's reply. Recorded so it is not re-opened blind.
4. **⚠ OT canon / Apocrypha scope is denominationally sensitive** (the brief's standing flag, and the same
   one carried by `book-research/in-defense-of-the-bible.md` ch. 16). Use shared-tradition material only;
   never adjudicate canonical scope in our own voice.
5. **⚠ Tone.** Kitchen's polemical register (see the 1966 article: "melancholy survey," "casuistic
   acrobatics") is incompatible with 1 Peter 3:15 and our house voice. Mine the arguments, leave the
   rhetoric.
