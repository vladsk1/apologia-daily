## Thesis (our words)
Who Jesus is matters to *everyone* (the interview opens by noting Muslims, Jehovah's Witnesses, Mormons,
Unitarians, Oneness Pentecostals, progressive Christians, and skeptics all agree the identity question
matters — they just answer it differently). Bowman's case does **not** rest on a few isolated proof texts or
on the Gospel of John alone; it is a **cumulative pattern** running through all four Gospels and the
Epistles, in which Jesus is shown sharing five things that belong to God alone — summarized by the **HANDS**
acronym (Ed Komoszewski's device). The Incarnation is the claim that the eternal, pre-existent divine Son
took on full humanity without ceasing to be God — a genuine **paradox** (Bowman's preferred word), not a
contradiction: the same contexts show Jesus' humanity and deity *together* (asleep in the boat, *then*
stilling the storm with a word). **(Scope: a NT-textual + historical case for Christ's deity; squarely
inside our orthodoxy guardrails — this is a defense *of* Nicene Christology, not a challenge to it.)**

## Argument map — Part 1: the HANDS case (→ primaries to VERIFY)
The five "fingers," each a category of divine prerogative the NT ascribes to Jesus:
- **H — Honors.** Jesus receives the honors due to God alone — worship, prayer, reverence. **John 5:23**
  (all should honor the Son as they honor the Father); **Matt 28:17–18** (they *worshiped* him — some
  doubted — and he claims *all authority*); **Rev 5** (the whole heavenly host worships **God and the
  Lamb** together in the throne room). The post-resurrection explosion of Jesus-devotion = **Larry
  Hurtado's "big bang of Christology"** (not an evolution over centuries but immediate). → *Primaries:*
  John 5:23; Matt 28:17–18; Rev 5:11–14. *Scholar:* Hurtado.
- **A — Attributes.** Jesus has the defining attribute of God — **eternal pre-existence** (**John 1:1–3**:
  "in the beginning *was* the Word," and all things came to be *through* him, so he is not among created
  things; **John 17:5**: the glory "before the world was"). Divine knowledge/omnipresence implied by his
  *answering prayer* (**John 14:14**) and *knowing hearts* (**Mark 2**). → *Primaries:* John 1:1–3; John
  17:5; John 14:14; Mark 2:6–8.
- **N — Names.** Jesus bears the divine names/titles: **God/Theos** (**John 1:1; John 20:28** "my Lord and
  my God"; **Titus 2:13** "our great God and Savior"; **2 Pet 1:1** "our God and Savior") — Bowman says
  Jesus is called *God* ~8× and *Lord* hundreds of times in the NT; and **Lord/Kyrios = YHWH** via
  OT-quotation (**Rom 10:9,13 → Joel 2:32** "call on the name of the Lord"). → *Primaries:* John 1:1; John
  20:28; Titus 2:13; 2 Pet 1:1; Rom 10:9,13 / Joel 2:32.
- **D — Deeds.** Jesus does what only God does — **forgives sins on his own authority** (**Mark 2**: the
  scribes rightly note only God can; Jesus proves it by healing); **stills the storm / walks on the sea**
  (in the Psalms, God alone rules the sea); **raises himself** (**John 2:19–21**; **John 10:17–18**);
  **judges the living and the dead** at the end. → *Primaries:* Mark 2:1–12; John 2:19–21; John 10:17–18;
  Ps 89/107 (God over the sea). *Scholar (aside):* Craig Keener, *Miracles* (nature-miracles like
  storm-stilling are *not* among reported modern parallels — cuts against a "just like faith healers" read).
- **S — Seat.** Jesus sits on **God's own throne**, sharing (not by delegation) the divine rule — the
  capstone. **Mark 14:61–62** (before Caiaphas: "I am… you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand
  of Power and coming on the clouds" = **Ps 110:1 + Dan 7:13**). → *Primaries:* Mark 14:61–62; Ps 110:1;
  Dan 7:13–14.

**Framing beats also worth noting (corroboration for our existing framing):**
- *"Why didn't Jesus just say 'I am God'?"* — he also never said the flat sentence "I am the Messiah"; his
  self-revelation is deliberately *indirect / oblique* (Messianic secret: "tell no one"), because
  first-century hearers had no Trinitarian framework; the disciples only see it *in retrospect* after the
  resurrection. Bowman notes this indirection is itself a mark of the Gospels' **authenticity** (a later
  fiction would have inserted direct claims). *(Our `answers/did-jesus-claim-to-be-god.html` + certified
  deity essays already make this move.)*
- *Synoptic deity is real, not John-only* — the demoniac(s) recognizing/fearing Jesus "from afar" (Mark 5)
  implies pre-existent knowledge of him; "I have come to…" sayings imply mission-from-elsewhere.

## Argument map — Part 2: the objection-answers (all map to existing on-site homes — see cross-check)
Each audience objection + Bowman's reply (every one already answered on-site unless flagged):
1. **John 4:25–26** ("I who speak to you am he") — didn't Jesus claim Messiah? *Reply:* he affirmed it
   *roundaboutly*, never the flat sentence; the "I am he" locution hints at *more* than Messiah (the
   Johannine "I am" sayings = divine identity).
2. **Mark 15:39** (centurion: "Son of God" vs "a son of God") — *Reply:* Greek word-order emphasizes *Son*;
   Mark's inclusio (1:1 → 15:39) means the *reader* knows more than the centurion did.
3. **Phil 2:9** ("highly exalted" — if already God, what exaltation?) — *Reply:* *hyperypsoō* = exalted to
   the *highest* place (not higher than before); Septuagint uses it of YHWH; it's *recognition / public
   naming* of the God-man, not a promotion or adoption (cf. Mark 9 "beloved Son" ≠ re-adoption). → *our
   `library/phil2.html` already answers this at/above this level.*
4. **John 17:5 vs 17:22** (shared-glory objection: Jesus gives disciples "the glory you gave me," so shared
   glory ≠ deity) — *Reply:* two glories — the **unshareable** glory of the "only-begotten" / pre-existent
   Son (John 1:14; 17:5), and a **shareable** glory that is *moral/relational* (unity and love, 17:21–26),
   possible only because he became incarnate. **← the one genuine GAP (see cross-check).**
5. **Mark 1 / Isa 40:3** (forerunner for YHWH → Jesus is YHWH; Cyrus counter) — *Reply:* Cyrus is never
   called YHWH and never stills storms/walks on water/rises; read through Mark, the "agent of God" take
   proves inadequate.
6. **Col 1:15** ("firstborn of all creation" — Arian/JW) — *Reply:* *prōtotokos/bekhor* = rank/heir
   (preeminence), not first-created; settled by 1:16–17 ("*for* in him all things were created… for him").
   → *our `library/trinity_jw.html` already makes exactly this argument, with a FAQ.*
7. **Mark 13:32** ("not even the Son" knows the day/hour) — *Reply:* the Incarnation paradox — Jesus has
   divine knowledge yet voluntarily experiences human limitation; note the progression *above the angels*;
   he never says "thus saith the Lord" but "amen, I say to you." → *our `trinity_jw.html` +
   `answers/if-jesus-is-god-why-did-he-pray.html` cover this.*
8. **Baptism of repentance** (Mark 1:4 — why baptized if sinless?) — *Reply:* identification with us, not
   confession of sin ("fulfill all righteousness," Matt 3:15); C. S. Lewis's "one foot on the bank" image;
   sinlessness attested across 2 Pet / 1 John / Heb / Gospels / Paul.
9. **Mark 10:18 / Luke 18:19** ("why do you call me good? none is good but God") — *Reply:* a *diagnostic*
   question exposing the man's assumptions, **not a denial** of his own goodness; Mark 10:45 "ransom for
   many" implies sinlessness. → *our `jesuschar.html` / `jesus_claims.html` /
   `answers/was-jesus-just-a-good-moral-teacher.html` cover it.*
10. **Immaterial = nothing?** (skeptic) — *Reply:* immaterial things have properties (thoughts, numbers,
    souls, God); "nothing" has none — an equivocation. Also rebuts **Mormon materialism** (1 Kings 8:27
    Solomon; Isa 66:1 throne/footstool as metaphor). → *our `library/trinity_mormons.html` covers this.*
11. **John 10:34–36** ("you are gods," Ps 82) — *Reply:* Ps 82 addresses the *false gods of the nations*;
    Jesus contrasts himself as the one "the Father sanctified and sent," and 10:36 ("I am the Son of God")
    is the one place he says it flatly. → *our `trinity_mormons.html` / `ot_trinity.html` / `nt_trinity.html`
    handle Ps 82.*
12. **Evolutionary Christology** (low view → high view over time) — *Reply:* the *disciples' understanding*
    grew (Luke 24 Emmaus; Thomas' "my Lord and my God," John 20:28; Acts 2 "Lord and Messiah" at once), but
    the *Christology didn't evolve* — **Martin Hengel: "more development in Christology in the first 30 years
    than in the next 600."** → *our `early_church_trinity.html` / `earlycreed.html` + the Habermas
    early-high-Christology note already carry the Hengel point.*
13. **"The Word made flesh" but wrote nothing** — *Reply:* *logos* ≠ a written text; it's God's
    self-expression / the pre-existent agent of creation. Inspiration handled separately (2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet
    3:15–16).
14. **"Jesus pre-exists his own existence"** — *Reply:* an *a priori* objection; *Jesus the man* didn't
    pre-exist, but the *divine person* did — no contradiction unless Scripture said he *began* at conception.
15. **Modalism / Oneness Pentecostalism** — defined (one person in three modes) and located as one of the
    book's targets. → *our `library/modalism.html` covers this.*
16. **"Why wait until ~30 to begin?"** — *Reply:* Luke 2:52 (grew in wisdom/stature — real human
    maturation), *contra* the Gnostic infancy gospels' wonder-child; forerunner order (John the Baptist first).
