## Thesis (our words)
When the spade turns up a specific *named person* — king, general, priest, governor, scribe, even an
obscure figure the Bible mentions once — independently attested in a *contemporary* inscription, in the
*right place and time*, it corroborates the Bible as the work of well-informed reporters of their world.
Across the "significant" figures, roughly **30–40%** are archaeologically attested (higher for the NT, a
literate Roman period). Crucially, the pattern runs one direction: figures once dismissed as invented
(David, Belshazzar) keep being confirmed by later finds. **(Scope, which the video keeps: this establishes
the *existence* of persons and the reliability of the *setting* — NOT the theology, the miracles, or that
every narrated *event* happened. Kennedy grades evidence A–D and refuses over-claims.)**

## Method (the attestation criteria — our words; builds on prior scholarship)
A figure counts as firmly attested when the inscription matches on: (1) **name** (allowing for
language/spelling shifts — e.g. *metathesis*, consonant-flipping); (2) **family lineage** (the "son of…"
patronymic motif); (3) **title / occupation / accomplishment**; (4) **chronology** — *contemporary*
attestation (within a ~50–90-yr lifetime; a later mention is weaker, "tradition, not attestation" — e.g.
Lot named only in Byzantine-era texts); (5) **geography**. A sixth, when needed: **artifact authenticity /
chain of custody** (unprovenanced/looted/market items and forgeries must be screened). *(This is the standard
"identifying biblical persons in inscriptions" method — likely built on **Lawrence Mykytiuk**'s work
`[AUTO: unnamed — "a PhD dissertation… focusing on figures from the monarchy"]`; verify.)*

## Argument map — the 10 figures (each → the primaries/inscriptions to VERIFY)
**Old Testament (6):**
1. **King David** — the **Tel Dan Stele** (1993, Tel Dan; Aramaic victory stele, prob. **Hazael**; the phrase
   *bytdwd* "**House of David**"; 9th c. BC); the **Mesha Stele** (reassessed to also read "House of David");
   a tentative reading on **Shoshenq I**'s relief `[AUTO: "Shoshenq"]`; the proposed **palace of David**
   (**Eilat Mazar**, on **Kathleen Kenyon**'s earlier work — ashlar masonry, a Phoenician column capital,
   pottery dating just before/after 1000 BC, cf. 2 Sam's Phoenician builders — *identification disputed*);
   administrative **bullae**; and the fortified network (**Khirbet Qeiyafa, Lachish, Mizpah, Beth Shemesh,
   Tel Zayit**) attesting a centralized kingdom + literacy. → *cross-check: already the backbone of
   `library/archaeology.html`.*
2. **King Ahab** — the **Kurkh Monolith of Shalmaneser III** `[AUTO: "Shalmaneser III"]` (names Ahab king of
   Israel with "10,000 soldiers + 2,000 chariots" at the coalition battle — **Qarqar, 853 BC**; most chariots
   of any ally); father **Omri** on the Mesha Stele; **Jezebel** seal (probable); son **Jehoram** on the Tel
   Dan Stele; the **Jezreel** fortress (chariot storage); the **Samaria "ivory house"** (1 Kings 22:39); an
   **Ahab ring** (unprovenanced — lower weight). → *primaries: Kurkh Monolith; Mesha Stele.*
3. **Uzziah / Azariah** — **Tiglath-Pileser III** text (743 BC, tribute from "**Azaryahu of Yaudi**" —
   *some scholars dispute the ID*); two 8th-c. seals of officials ("**Abiyaw servant of Uzziah**";
   "**Shebnayahu servant of Uzziah**"); the "**Uzziah tablet**" — a **1st-c. AD tomb memorial**, NOT
   contemporary (tradition, not attestation). → *primaries: Tiglath-Pileser III annals; the two seals.*
4. **Isaiah the prophet** — the **Great Isaiah Scroll** (DSS, complete book; *not contemporary*); the **Isaiah
   bulla** (Eilat Mazar's Ophel dig, ~700 BC, "**Yesha'yahu**" + a second line reading "**nvy[?]**" =
   *prophet* with the final letter broken off — *ID disputed*), found near a **Hezekiah bulla**. → *primary:
   the Isaiah bulla (hedge the broken reading).*
5. **Adrammelech / Arda-Mulissu** `[AUTO: "Adrammelech / Arda Mulissu"]` — a son of **Sennacherib** who helped
   assassinate his father (**2 Kings 19:37 // Isaiah 37:38**); attested in **Assyrian sources** (name = the
   biblical one via metathesis; the ID is *not disputed*); a possible **image** on a relief from Sennacherib's
   SW palace at **Nineveh**. → *primaries: 2 Kings 19:37; Isa 37:38; the Assyrian assassination records.*
6. **Belshazzar** — long doubted (classical lists name **Nabonidus** as last king of Babylon, no Belshazzar →
   "Daniel is unreliable"), then confirmed: the **Nabonidus Cylinder** (Belshazzar = his **firstborn son**);
   the **Babylonian Chronicles** (the crown prince ruled *in* Babylon while Nabonidus was away in Arabia/Tema);
   the **Verse Account of Nabonidus** (kingship *entrusted* to Belshazzar) — which neatly explains why Daniel
   is offered only **third** place in the kingdom (Dan 5:16: Nabonidus #1, Belshazzar #2); a cuneiform **oath
   tablet** sworn to *both* (co-regency). → *primaries: Nabonidus Cylinder; Babylonian Chronicles; Verse
   Account; Dan 5. **← the strongest, most teachable case not yet named in our essay (see cross-check).***

**New Testament (4):**
7. **Quirinius** (**Luke 2:2**) — the **Lapis Venetus** (found in Beirut/Syria; names Augustus, a census, the
   *legate* Quirinius, and his officer's census of Apamea in Syria); the **Res Gestae** of Augustus (an
   empire-wide census, **8 BC** — Kennedy's preferred correlation to Luke); plus **Josephus, Tacitus, Pliny
   the Elder, Suetonius**. ⚠ **The birth-of-Jesus chronology (how Quirinius's Syrian governorship squares
   with a Herod-the-Great birth) is genuinely contested** — Kennedy brackets that debate and defends only
   Quirinius's *existence and position*, which are not disputed. → *primaries: Lapis Venetus; Res Gestae.*
8. **Aretas IV** — king of Nabataea; **2 Cor 11:32** (his ethnarch guarding Damascus during Paul's
   basket-escape, an undesigned coincidence with **Acts 9:23–25**); attested by **coins** (name + image),
   officials' burial inscriptions; the **Petra "Treasury" (Al-Khazneh)** is almost certainly his tomb (carved
   in his reign); family web (his daughter married **Herod Antipas**, who divorced her for **Herodias** → the
   marriage **John the Baptist** condemned; all in **Josephus**). → *primaries: 2 Cor 11:32; Aretas coins.*
9. **Herod Agrippa I** (**Acts 12**) — executes **James son of Zebedee**, arrests Peter, then dies at
   **Caesarea Maritima** after accepting acclamation as a god; **Josephus** (*Antiquities* 19) gives a strikingly
   parallel death account (undesigned coincidence: Josephus independently notes his eagerness to please the
   Jews, matching Acts 12:3); plus his **coins** and building projects. → *primaries: Acts 12; Josephus Ant. 19.*
10. **Julia Berenice / Berenice II** (**Acts 25–26**) — Herodian princess (daughter of Herod Agrippa I,
    great-granddaughter of Herod the Great, sister of **Herod Agrippa II**); named by **Josephus, Tacitus,
    Suetonius, Cassius Dio**; a Beirut inscription naming "**Berenice and Agrippa**," and an **Athens** statue
    base inscribed (Greek) "**Julia Berenice, great queen**." → *primaries: Acts 25–26; the two inscriptions.*

**The "Spider-Man objection" (the interview's methodological payoff).** *Objection:* naming real places/people
proves nothing about the story's events (Spider-Man is set in a real New York). *Kennedy's reply:* the analogy
fits the **Iliad** (Troy was real, but Hector/Achilles/Priam have **zero** contemporary attestation — they're
named only centuries later, depicting Homer's story) — but it fails for the Bible, whose *specific persons*
(David, Hezekiah, Belshazzar…) ARE attested by contemporary evidence, and whose *events* are often
corroborated by outside sources. Capped by **Luke 3:1–2** (Tiberius, Pilate, Herod, Philip, Lysanias, Annas,
Caiaphas — a dense cluster of independently-attested names framing "the word of God came to John"). → *This
is exactly the "a novel can be set in a meticulously accurate London" point our essay already makes.*
