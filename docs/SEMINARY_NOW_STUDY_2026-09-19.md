# Seminary Now → Apologia Daily: competitor study — 2026-09-19

**What this is.** A benchmark of **Seminary Now** (seminarynow.com), an external subscription video-learning
platform, as a comparable to Apologia Daily's learning surfaces. Companion to
[`docs/BIBLEPROJECT_STUDY_2026-09-09.md`](BIBLEPROJECT_STUDY_2026-09-09.md) — same "accessible seminary-level
learning" reference class, a different business model (subscription, not donor-funded).

## 1. How it started

- **Origin sequence.** InterVarsity Press (IVP) was announced as the platform's **"premiere publishing
  partner" in 2019**; Seminary Now then **launched in beta in spring 2020** for wide release that year. Early
  promotion ran at the **ETS and AAR/SBL** academic conferences in San Diego (late 2019). (unverified on exact
  dates)
- **Who's behind it.** **Jason Gile** is executive director (PhD, Wheaton College; also VP of Strategic
  Initiatives and a Scripture professor at **Northern Seminary**). **Northern Seminary** is the primary
  institution underwriting the venture; **IVP** supplies much of the scholarship/authors.
- **Stated rationale.** Gile's framing: on-demand video lets seminaries + publishers collaborate to equip
  church leaders affordably — "seminary-level" teaching without seminary enrollment.
- **Editorial ethos (important for us — see §4).** The founding partnership foregrounds **women in leadership,
  multiethnic ministry, and biblical justice** as shared commitments. That is a real editorial lean, not a
  neutral catalog.

## 2. What it does

- A **subscription, on-demand video learning platform** for **pastors and lay church leaders** — *not* a
  degree-granting seminary and *not* an apologetics site.
- **~80–90+ streaming video courses + 10+ live online classes** (unverified counts), taught by a roster of
  well-known scholars/practitioners. Confirmed-in-search pairings include **Scot McKnight** ("Jesus & the
  Gospels," "Paul & Judaism"), **Esau McCaulley** ("Reading While Black"), and **Brenda Salter McNeil**
  ("Roadmap to Reconciliation"); a "Knowing Jesus through the Old Testament" course exists (instructor not
  confirmed in search). (instructor/course pairings unverified at primary)
- **Membership perks:** unlimited course access, **live Q&As with teachers, discussion forums / Community+
  groups, and downloadable workbooks/discussion guides**.
- **Learning Tracks** = curated course sequences ending in a **downloadable PDF certificate** (explicitly *not*
  accredited). Named tracks seen in search: **Story of Scripture, New Testament, Seminary Foundations**.
- **For-credit path:** some **graduate-level, for-credit** classes via the **Northern Seminary** tie — the same
  "convert learning into real credit through a partner seminary" mechanic BibleProject Classroom uses.
- **Topic focus is practical ministry:** preaching, evangelism, mental health, racial reconciliation, women in
  ministry, biblical justice — formation and leadership, not evidential apologetics.
- **Pricing:** roughly **$14.99/month billed annually** (unverified; tiers may vary).

## 3. The transferable lessons — ranked by impact ÷ effort

"Gate" = touches `apologia-argument` / `apologia-orthodoxy` (+ `apologia-neutrality` for deity/Trinity/Islam).

1. **Learning Tracks = named, ordered course sequences ending in a certificate.** Apologia already has the
   atoms (essay + mastery page + flashcards + Explain It Back) but no *named, ordered* course. *Change:* bundle
   4–6 certified essays into "The Case for the Resurrection: a 5-session track" with next/previous flow + a
   completion share-card. Study Plans may already be most of this — cheap to validate. *Effort:* Medium.
   *Gate:* none for sequencing; small on intro/certificate copy. **(Same top pick as the BibleProject study —
   two competitors converging on it is a strong signal.)**
2. **Certificate as a completion artifact**, reusing the share-card generator. *Effort:* Small. *Gate:* small —
   **must never imply accreditation or theological authority** (Seminary Now itself flags its certificate as
   non-accredited; be at least as careful).
3. **Live teacher Q&A → a scheduled live "Ask the tutor / office hours" event.** Apologia's `/api/tutor` is
   always-on but asynchronous; a *scheduled, communal* answer session is a distinct engagement + retention
   loop. *Effort:* Medium. *Gate:* the pastoral-care carve-out and the normal answer gates apply to anything
   said live.
4. **Per-course downloadable workbook framed for group use.** Direct analog of the BibleProject "Guides" item:
   a printable discussion guide per essay, questions **ported verbatim** from its certified objections section +
   pocket card. *Effort:* Medium. *Gate:* small (ported text, new surface).
5. **A single named "start here" default.** Someone landing cold on `library/index.html` / `evidence-library.html`
   meets 94 essays and 8 tabs with no default. *Change:* one "New here? Start with [X]" banner. *Effort:* Small.
   *Gate:* none — routing.

## 4. What NOT to copy (and the honest positioning)

- **It is a business-model / format comparable, not a content competitor.** Seminary Now is
  **practical-ministry formation**; Apologia is **evidential apologetics**. The transferable material is the
  *packaging* (tracks, certificates, live Q&A, workbooks) — **not** the subject matter.
- **🔴 Do NOT mine it for content — the editorial lean crosses our denominational-neutrality guardrail.** Its
  headline commitments (biblical justice, women in ministry/leadership, reconciliation framings) sit in
  positions that divide Catholics, Orthodox, and Protestants — exactly the intra-Christian disputes `CLAUDE.md`
  forbids us to adjudicate in our own voice. Several of its instructors are fine *scholars* on other topics
  (e.g. Scot McKnight on the Gospels), but a course roster is not a neutral source, and picking from it would
  import a tilt. If a specific scholar is ever wanted, go to their **own peer-reviewed primary work** through
  the normal research pipeline, never via this platform's framing.
- **Subscription model validates ours, doesn't threaten it.** Unlike BibleProject's donor model (a fundraising
  operation that is a *trap* if read as "make it all free"), Seminary Now is **freemium-adjacent subscription at
  ~$15/mo** — the *same* reference class Apologia's own economics point to (Brilliant/Duolingo, per
  `docs/GROWTH_PLAN_SIMPLE.md` / `docs/META_ADS_PLAN.md`). It's quiet corroboration that a paid tier for
  depth-of-learning is a normal, defensible model in this space.
- **The for-credit partnership is not replicable cheaply.** Both Seminary Now and BibleProject Classroom lean
  on **Northern Seminary** to convert learning into real credit. That requires an accredited institutional
  partner; don't imply credit or accreditation without one.

---

## 5. The one thing to do first

**Item 1 — package existing certified essays into one named, ordered Learning Track with a completion
share-card.** It's the pick both this study and the BibleProject study independently land on; it needs **zero
new doctrinal prose** (sequence already-certified content); it reuses the existing share-card generator; and it
turns the depth Apologia already has into a *finishable* experience — the single thing subscription learning
platforms do that a library of essays does not. Validate against Study Plans first (it may already be 80% built).
