## 2. The rule that governed everything, stated once

Across five gate rounds on the essay, **every sentence ported verbatim from already-certified text
survived review untouched, and nearly every sentence written fresh produced the next defect.**
`apologia-argument` stated it unprompted in round 3: *"every sentence ported from ev-s3.html
survived this review untouched; every sentence authored fresh generated a finding."*

Two refinements were learned the hard way and both cost a round:

1. **A gate's *suggested* rewrite is NOT ported wording.** It is un-gated content and it broke
   exactly like authored prose. `apologia-neutrality` supplied the "rivalry argument"; `apologia-argument`
   then demolished it as a non-sequitur whose premise the essay's own previous paragraph falsified.
   `apologia-orthodoxy` drafted an `orthonote` box and **overturned its own draft** in the next round.
   ⭐ Only text carrying a `content-review` stamp counts as ported.
2. **A ported predicate detached from its qualifier is not a port.** All three doctrinal gates
   independently caught `ev-s3.html:916`'s "the Creator's own side of the line" being attached to the
   *seat alone*, when the certified original attaches it to the *combination* and says the seat alone
   is not the point.

⚠ **And two of my own fix scripts introduced duplicated sentences**, both caught by a reviewer and
not by me. Both had the same cause: an idempotency guard testing a token the OLD and NEW strings
*share*. **A re-application guard must test a token unique to NEW.**
