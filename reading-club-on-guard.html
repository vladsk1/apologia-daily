William Lane Craig’s On Guard is a step-by-step tour of the central arguments of Christian apologetics — why anything exists at all, why the universe began and why it is fine-tuned, whether we can be good without God, what to make of suffering, who Jesus was, and whether he rose from the dead. It is the accessible, popular-level companion to his scholarly work. This club walks the book chapter by chapter, with discussion questions for each, so a group can work through the whole case together.

About this study

The sessions follow the book's own structure — one per chapter, in order, from “what apologetics is” through to “is Jesus the only way?” This is an independent companion study: it is not affiliated with, nor endorsed by, the author, and no permission was sought or given. The summaries, questions and judgements in it are ours.

The chapter headings here are our own working titles, and the summaries and discussion questions are ours too. Nothing on this page reproduces the book's text. Every summary was written in our own words from a structured reading of the argument of each chapter.

We keep the arguments carefully bounded — the kalam is stated as “whatever begins to exist has a cause,” fine-tuning distinguishes the conceded data from the inferred design, the moral argument is about the ground of morality rather than whether an atheist can be good, and the free-will response to suffering is a defence of consistency, not a complete theodicy. Where the book takes a position that divides faithful Christians — on the fate of those who never hear, or the nature of final judgment — we present the range of orthodox views rather than settling an in-house dispute. That is our own house standard for this material, and where a judgement is ours rather than the book's we mark it as such.

The extra Apologia Daily links are clearly labelled as our own optional resources — not part of the book, and not the author's endorsement of them. A reader can complete the whole club without ever opening one.

Finish the study · Mastery check

Can you make the case — kindly?

One capstone for the whole book — eight prompts that walk through the argument, in two modes. Flashcards: read a prompt, answer it in your head (or out loud to the group), then tap to reveal a model answer — the book’s argument, put in our own words and in our own house tone. Self-rate quiz: take the same eight prompts one at a time, rate how well you answered each, and get an honest score at the end. If you can walk through all eight, you have the shape of the whole case in hand.

01 · The approach
What is Christian apologetics, and what is it for?
Tap to reveal →
Giving a reasoned defence of the faith (the apologia of 1 Peter 3:15) — with gentleness and respect. Its job is to show the faith is true and reasonable and to open honest conversations. It undergirds a believer’s trust; it never replaces it, and it is never a quarrel to be won.
02 · Contingency
Why is there something rather than nothing?
Tap to reveal →
Everything that exists has an explanation — in the necessity of its own nature or in an external cause. The universe exists and could have been otherwise, so it has an explanation beyond itself: a necessary, self-existent being. And “what explains God?” doesn’t regress — a necessary being is explained by its own nature, not a further cause.
03 · The beginning
Why did the universe have a cause?
Tap to reveal →
Whatever begins to exist has a cause (not “everything has a cause”). The universe began — supported both philosophically and by the evidence that it traces back to a beginning. So it has a cause beyond space and time: immaterial, powerful, and personal. We hold the science as strong corroboration, not proof.
04 · Fine-tuning
What does fine-tuning show — and what does it not?
Tap to reveal →
The constants sit in an extraordinarily narrow life-permitting range — the data, conceded even by atheist physicists. Design is the inference, weighed against necessity and chance as the best of the three — never a scientific consensus. Keep “fine-tuned” (data) and “designed” (conclusion) apart.
05 · Morality
Can we be good without God?
Tap to reveal →
Yes — atheists can live good lives and know right from wrong. The real question is the ground: if God does not exist, is there any objective basis for moral values and duties? The argument says no — and their reality points to God. Euthyphro is answered by the third option: God wills the good because he is good.
06 · Suffering
How should a Christian face the problem of suffering?
Tap to reveal →
Separate the emotional problem — answered first, and often by presence rather than argument — from the intellectual one. The logical version fails: a free-will defence shows God and suffering are consistent (it need only be possible). The evidential version is met by our poor vantage point and the total evidence. The mystery is conceded, not explained away.
07 · Who Jesus was
How do we know Jesus claimed — and shared — God’s identity?
Tap to reveal →
Not from one sentence but a pattern: he was worshipped by monotheists within about twenty years (Philippians 2); he steps into roles Scripture keeps for God (the coming LORD of Malachi 3:1; the beloved Son and heir; the unique knowing of Matthew 11:27; the right hand of God at his trial, Psalm 110:1, heard as blasphemy). The church confesses one person, fully God and fully man.
08 · The resurrection
What is the case that Jesus rose?
Tap to reveal →
Two steps — the facts, then the best explanation. Three facts a wide majority of scholars grant: the empty tomb, the appearances (the very early creed of 1 Corinthians 15), and a belief the disciples couldn’t have invented. Fraud, swoon and hallucination each leave part unexplained; God raised him explains all three.
